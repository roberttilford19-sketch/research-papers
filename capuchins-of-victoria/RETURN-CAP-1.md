# RETURN-CAP-1 — THE ORDER: The Capuchin Franciscans (OFM Cap.)

Research wave ordered by Robert Tilford, 2026-11-12. Research only; no contact made.
Labels: VERIFIED = checked against the source named, in the same breath. SUPPORTED = solid secondary sourcing. CANDIDATE = plausible, unconfirmed. NOT FOUND = looked for, not established.

## The name and the family

- The order's full name is the **Order of Friars Minor Capuchin** (Latin *Ordo Fratrum Minorum Capuccinorum*), postnominal **OFM Cap.** It is one of the three "First Orders" of Franciscan friars, alongside the Friars Minor (OFM) and the Conventuals (OFM Conv). — VERIFIED (Wikipedia, "Order of Friars Minor Capuchin," read at source: "one of three 'First Orders' that reformed from the Franciscan Friars Minor Observant… the other being the Conventuals (OFMConv)").
- All three branches follow the Rule of St. Francis (1223); they differ in how literally, and how severely, they keep its poverty.

## The founding

- The reform began **about 1525** in the Marches of Italy, when **Matteo da Bascio** (born Matteo Serafini, c. 1495–1552), an Observant Franciscan priest, became convinced that the friars of his day were no longer living the life Francis had intended, and set out to return to "the primitive way of life of solitude and penance, as practised by the founder." — VERIFIED (Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Matteo da Bascio"; the order's own general site, ofmcap.org, "The History of the Capuchins": "The Order was founded around 1525, when the Franciscan friar Matteo da Bascio… was convinced that the lifestyle led by the Franciscans of his time was not what Saint Francis had imagined").
- **The hood.** Matteo's superiors tried to suppress the innovation and the first companions were hunted as runaways; they were sheltered by the **Camaldolese monks**, and in gratitude adopted the long pointed hood — the *cappuccio* — worn by that order, the mark of a hermit in the Marches, together with the beard. The people named the friars from the hood: *cappuccini*. — VERIFIED (ofmcap.org, order's official history; Wikipedia, "Matteo da Bascio," carries the same account). Britannica adds the companion telling: Matteo, convinced Francis's own habit had a pointed, pyramidal hood, made one for himself and grew a beard and went barefoot. The two tellings sit side by side in the sources and are compatible; the desk prints both rather than choosing.
- **3 July 1528** — Pope Clement VII, through the mediation of Caterina Cibò, Duchess of Camerino, issued the bull ***Religionis zelus***, canonically approving the reform: the friars might live as hermits and go everywhere preaching to the poor. They were formed as the "Hermit Friars Minor," a branch under the nominal jurisdiction of the Conventuals, with a vicar of their own. — VERIFIED (Wikipedia, "Matteo da Bascio": "On 3 July 1528, the Pope issued the bull *Religionis zelus*, by which the new reform was canonically approved"; Britannica, same date and bull; ofmcap.org, same year, bull, and mediator).
  - Desk flag: the St. Conrad province's own "Who Are We" page compresses this into approval "in 1525 in the papal bull Religionis Zellus." The bull is 1528; 1525 is the reform's beginning. The three stronger sources above agree on 1528, and the returns follow them.
- At the first chapter (Albacina, April 1529) Matteo was elected vicar general; he soon resigned the office to return to preaching. — VERIFIED (Britannica; Wikipedia, "Matteo da Bascio").

## The shape of the life (the 1529 rules)

- The first constitutions fixed a life of "extreme austerity, simplicity and poverty": communities small — **eight friars the normal number, twelve the limit**; neither house nor province to possess anything; stores never to exceed a few days' needs; everything obtained by begging, and **the friars were not allowed even to touch money**; barefoot; a plain brown tunic with the large pointed hood reaching to the waist fastened to it, girdled with the cord of three knots. — SUPPORTED (Wikipedia, "Order of Friars Minor Capuchin," "Rules of the Order" section, summarizing the 1529 chapter).
- Prayer was structural, not decorative: besides the Divine Office sung in choir — part of it recited at midnight — **two hours of private prayer daily**. The order's main outward work was "preaching and spiritual ministrations among the poor." — SUPPORTED (same source).
- Early wound, kept on the record: the third vicar general, Bernardino Ochino, fled to Geneva and left the Catholic faith (1542–43); Pope Paul III came close to suppressing the young order and for a time forbade the friars to preach before permission was restored. — SUPPORTED (Wikipedia, "Order of Friars Minor Capuchin," "Early setbacks").

## Independence, and how the branches differ

- In 1574 Gregory XIII permitted the order to settle "in France and all other parts of the world." In **1619 the Capuchins were freed from dependence on the Conventuals and became an independent order**, by then reckoned at some 1,500 houses in fifty provinces. — VERIFIED (Wikipedia, "Order of Friars Minor Capuchin": "in 1619 they were freed from their dependence on the Conventual Franciscans and became an independent Order"; corroborated by the Capuchin Friars Australia province history page: "constituted into an independent order" in 1619).
- The three branches, plainly: the **OFM** descend from the Observants, the main line; the **Conventuals (OFM Conv)** kept the great urban convents and a moderated corporate poverty; the **Capuchins** were the literalists — the strictest poverty of the three, no ownership even in common, tiny hermitage-like houses, long hours of contemplative prayer, beards and the pointed hood, and a deliberate ministry to the common people. The St. Conrad province's own statement of the charism today: Capuchins have "a special charism for working among the common people and **taking those assignments which others refuse**" — VERIFIED as the province's own wording (capuchins.org, "Who Are We," read at source).
- Scale today: about **10,349 members (2020)** worldwide; the 2018 count carried in the same reference is 10,480 friars in 108 countries. Minister General: Roberto Genuin, OFM Cap. — VERIFIED (Wikipedia, "Order of Friars Minor Capuchin," infobox and "Modern era," read at source).
- A light footnote, sourced like everything else: the **cappuccino** coffee and the **capuchin monkey** both take their names from the colour of the Capuchin habit. — SUPPORTED (Wikipedia, "Order of Friars Minor Capuchin"; BiblicalTraining library entry on the Capuchins).
