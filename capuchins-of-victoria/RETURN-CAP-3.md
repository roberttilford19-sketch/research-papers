# RETURN-CAP-3 — THE PROVINCE TODAY: St. Conrad, Denver

Research wave ordered by Robert Tilford, 2026-11-12. Research only; no contact made. Public-record facts only; nothing here proposes naming-for-contact or outreach to anyone.
Labels: VERIFIED = checked against the source named, in the same breath. SUPPORTED = solid secondary sourcing. CANDIDATE = plausible, unconfirmed. NOT FOUND = looked for, not established.

## The desk's candidate: CONFIRMED

- The Capuchins who serve Kansas today are the **Capuchin Province of St. Conrad** (also called the Mid-America province), headquartered in **Denver, Colorado**. The desk's candidate was right. — VERIFIED (province's own site, capuchins.org, "Who Are We," read at source: "The Capuchin Province of St. Conrad was established in 1977. The province serves the people of Colorado, Kansas, Texas and the foreign missions in Papua, New Guinea").

## How the province was born — in Victoria itself

- On **12 April 1977**, "the Pennsylvania Province of St. Augustine was divided into 'two separate and independent provinces,'" the eastern part keeping the name St. Augustine and the western part becoming the new province for the mid-continent. — VERIFIED (the province's own commissioned *Audit of the Capuchin Province of Mid-America (aka Province of St. Conrad)*, 1 March 2019, introduction).
- The establishment ceremonies for the new Mid-America province were held **14 April 1977 at St. Fidelis Church in Victoria**, the Minister General himself promulgating the eastern province at Pittsburgh on April 12 and the Mid-American one at Victoria two days later; about **85 priests and brothers** belonged to the new province, and the friars planning it "met Dec. 29 and 30 in Victoria to discuss the various hopes." — SUPPORTED (The Chancellor, newspaper of the Diocese of Salina, 28 January 1977, contemporary reporting; the audit above fixes the legal division date as April 12, 1977).
- The province's **first headquarters was in Hays, Kansas**; in 1980 the headquarters moved to Kansas City; it is now in Denver. — SUPPORTED (contemporary/retrospective profile of Fr. Charles J. Chaput, the province's early vicar provincial, carried by Daily Catholic: "On April 12, 1977 the Province of Pennsylvania was dissolved and renamed St. Augustine while the new one, headquartered in Hays, Kansas was called St. Conrad Province… In 1980 the Province's headquarters were moved to Kansas City"; current Denver seat VERIFIED from capuchins.org).
- **The patron's name is a Kansas story.** The 1977 Chancellor report states that St. Conrad was chosen as patron of the Mid-America province "because of his promise to his fellow Bavarian, **Fr. Hyacinth Epp**, who established the Capuchins both in Pennsylvania and Kansas." Epp is the same commissary who walked into Herzog with Bishop Fink in January 1878 and sent Hau and Mueller that May (see RETURN-CAP-2). — SUPPORTED (The Chancellor, 28 Jan 1977; the Epp–Herzog facts VERIFIED in RETURN-CAP-2 from Laing's Kansas State Historical Society history). The province is named, in effect, for the doorkeeper-saint of the friar who opened Kansas.

## Territory and friaries today

- The province's canonical territory comprises **Colorado, Kansas and Missouri, and the Illinois dioceses of Belleville, Peoria and Springfield**, with **two houses in San Antonio, Texas**, outside that territory; its friars also serve the foreign missions in **Papua New Guinea**. — VERIFIED (2019 province audit for the territory; capuchins.org for the Papua New Guinea missions).
- The province's own current page lists **"eight stateside friaries"** located in **Denver, Colorado; Colorado Springs, Colorado; Lawrence, Kansas; Victoria, Kansas; and San Antonio, Texas**. — VERIFIED (capuchins.org, "Who Are We," read at source, crawled November 2026).
- The Kansas friaries are therefore two: **St. Conrad Friary, Lawrence**, and **St. Fidelis Friary, Victoria**. — VERIFIED (the province list above; both house names appear throughout the province's own published assignment records).
- Kansas ministry beyond the two friaries: a St. Fidelis bulletin of August 2026 lists the Capuchin seminarian fundraiser's locations as "Victoria, Lawrence, **Wichita**, San Antonio, Colorado" — CANDIDATE for a current Wichita ministry presence; Wichita does not appear as a friary on the province's own list. Hays, thick with Capuchin history (St. Joseph Friary; St. Bonaventure Friary; a century of the friars' schools), likewise does **not** appear on the province's current friary list, though parish bulletins as recent as 2025 mention Capuchin priestly service at the Comeau Catholic Campus Center in Hays. Both points are recorded as CANDIDATE ministries, not friaries.

## Leadership

- At the province chapter held **in Victoria, Kansas, 19–23 May 2025**, the friars "elected **Fr. David Songy** as provincial minister" for the province's seventeenth triennium. His predecessor was **Br. Mark Schenk, OFM Cap.**, noted by the province as the first lay brother to serve as its provincial minister. — VERIFIED (capuchins.org news, "Capuchins Elect New Leadership," May 22, 2025, and "Br. Mark Reflects on Highs and Lows as Provincial"). (An automated Facebook summary garbles the name as "Sanje"; the province's own site reads Songy, and the returns follow the province.)

## St. Fidelis today, as the public record shows it

- The parish's own bulletins through 2026 (stfidelischurch.com) list the staff: **Pastor: Fr. Curtis Carlson, OFM, Cap.**; **Fr. Vince Mary Carrasco, OFM, Cap.**, as assistant. The bulletin of 1 March 2026 announces Carrasco's arrival — "In March, we will have an additional Capuchin priest as my assistant" — and identifies him as "a Capuchin Franciscan friar for the Province of St. Conrad." — VERIFIED (St. Fidelis parish bulletins of 1 March, 15 March, 28 June, 12 July, and 9 August 2026, parish staff blocks). Nothing in this return draws on anything but these published bulletins.
- St. Fidelis Friary is a living house, not a relic: spring 2026 bulletins carry the notice "The Capuchins at St. Fidelis Friary seek a paid cook… Position to begin May 1, 2026," and the bulletins run a standing "Capuchin Corner." — VERIFIED (St. Fidelis bulletins, March–April 2026).
- The parish website presents St. Fidelis with its sister parishes as the "Tri-Parishes" (St. Fidelis, St. Ann, St. Bonaventure appear across the bulletin mastheads and councils). — SUPPORTED (St. Fidelis bulletins, 2025–2026).
- One further public-record item about the friary: in the fall of 2018 the St. Fidelis Friary "made national headlines" when former Cardinal Theodore McCarrick was sent there to live out a "life of prayer and penance" after his removal from ministry; he was laicized in February 2019. — VERIFIED (news reporting carried by bishop-accountability.org, April 2019, summarizing the diocesan announcement). Recorded because it is part of the house's public story; the friars' part in it was to keep a house of prayer that could receive such an assignment.

## The hard page, kept on the record

- In March 2019 the province published the results of an independent audit it had itself commissioned, naming **13 friars with credible allegations of sexual abuse** of minors or vulnerable adults; the *Denver Post* reported the release (28 March 2019), and the province published the audit and a reporting contact on its own site. — VERIFIED (province audit, 1 March 2019, "Origins and Scope of the Audit": "In 2018, the current Provincial Minister… and the Provincial Council commissioned a comprehensive independent audit of the Province's personnel files"; Denver Post report as mirrored at bishop-accountability.org). The desk records this in one neutral paragraph because it belongs to the province's public record — and because the province's own act was to commission and publish it.
