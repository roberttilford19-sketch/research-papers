# RETURN-CAP-2 — THE ROAD TO KANSAS: Capuchins, the Volga Germans, and St. Fidelis

Research wave ordered by Robert Tilford, 2026-11-12. Research only; no contact made.
Labels: VERIFIED = checked against the source named, in the same breath. SUPPORTED = solid secondary sourcing. CANDIDATE = plausible, unconfirmed. NOT FOUND = looked for, not established.

## How the friars got to America, then to Pittsburgh

- The Kansas Capuchins came by the German road. Under Bismarck's **Kulturkampf**, the Capuchins of Bavaria were driven out; they "first found a welcome in Pittsburgh, Pa., in 1873." — VERIFIED (The Cadet Journal, St. Joseph's Military Academy, Hays, April 30, 1959: "As a result of the edict against religious orders during the Kulturkampf, the Capuchin Fathers of Bavaria came to America. They first found a welcome in Pittsburgh, Pa., in 1873"; corroborated by Fr. Francis S. Laing, O.M. Cap., *German-Russian Settlements in Ellis County, Kansas*, written for the Kansas State Historical Society: Fr. Hyacinth Epp, "at the time commissary of the Capuchins, who had come to Pittsburgh, Pa., in 1873, because of the 'Kulturkampf,' then at its height in Germany").
- (For scale: the other first American foundation was Swiss, at Mount Calvary, Wisconsin, in 1856–57, ancestor of the Province of St. Joseph, 1882. The Kansas line is the Bavarian–Pittsburgh line, not the Wisconsin line. — VERIFIED, Wikipedia, "Order of Friars Minor Capuchin," "United States — Foundation.")

## The bishop asks; the friars come (1878)

- The Volga German colonies of Ellis County lay in the Diocese of Leavenworth. **Bishop Louis M. Fink, O.S.B.**, asked the Capuchins to take spiritual charge of the newcomers. On **31 January 1878**, Fink and **Fr. Hyacinth Epp, O.M. Cap.**, the Pittsburgh commissary, visited Herzog together. — VERIFIED (Laing, *German-Russian Settlements in Ellis County*, full text at archive.org: "Rt. Rev. Louis M. Fink, O.S.B., of Leavenworth, in whose diocese the colonies then lay, and Rev. Hyacinth Epp, O.M. Cap.… visited Herzog January 31, 1878. Bishop Fink had requested the Capuchins to take spiritual charge of the colonies").
- In May 1878 the first two friars arrived at Herzog: **Fr. Matthew Hau, O.M. Cap.** (in America since 1873) and **Fr. Anastasius Mueller, O.M. Cap.** (in America since August 1876). — VERIFIED (Laing; *The Golden Jubilee of German-Russian Settlements of Ellis and Rush Counties*, 1926, "The Capuchin Fathers": "The first ones to reach the scene… were Rev. Mathew Hau, O.M. Cap., and Rev. Anastasius Mueller, O.M. Cap., who arrived at Herzog… 1878").
  - **Date discrepancy, preserved, not blended:** Laing gives the arrival as **May 11, 1878**; the 1926 Golden Jubilee book gives **May 15, 1878**. Both are the desk's best sources and they differ by four days. The exact day stands NOT FOUND at the level of agreement; May 1878 is VERIFIED.
- Hau died within weeks — 25 June 1878 — and on 15 July 1878 **Fr. Joseph Calasanz Mayershofer, O.M. Cap.** succeeded him. — VERIFIED (Laing; Golden Jubilee pastor list: "1878 (May) Rev. Matthew Han [Hau], O.M. Cap. (Jul.) Rev. Jos. Cal. Meyershofer, O.M. Cap.").
- Before the friars, the settlers were served by visiting and diocesan priests: **Fr. Adolf Wibbert** visited in 1876; **Fr. Valentine Sommereisen** watched over the flock from October 1876 until the Capuchins took charge in May 1878. The first Mass was celebrated in **April 1876 in the home of settler Alois Dreiling**. A parish bulletin of 2026 still calls Sommereisen "the French priest who founded St. Fidelis Parish." — VERIFIED (Golden Jubilee, Herzog chapter, for Wibbert and Sommereisen; National Catholic Register, "'Cathedral of the Plains' Worth the Long Drive," for the April 1876 house Mass; St. Fidelis parish bulletin, 14 June 2026, for the Sommereisen line).
- On **25 August 1883**, Bishop Fink entrusted the Capuchin fathers with "the spiritual care of all Catholics in Ellis county north of the Smoky Hill river." — VERIFIED (Laing).
- The friars' first Herzog residence was Alois Dreiling's dwelling; in September 1878 Mayershofer built an annex to the church as a residence, completed spring 1879. — VERIFIED (Laing, part 3).

## The churches, one after another

- The people outgrew a succession of buildings: worship in houses (1876); a small wood-frame church **1878**, "which they immediately outgrew"; a larger church **1884**; and finally the present church. Sisters of St. Agnes from Wisconsin ran the school from 1879. — VERIFIED (Society of Architectural Historians, SAH Archipedia, "Basilica of St. Fidelis"; Golden Jubilee/Laing for the Sisters of St. Agnes, arrived 29 August 1879).
- A stone church was also raised in the first years — its cornerstone was laid **1 June 1880**. — SUPPORTED (Volga German Institute, "St. Fidelis Catholic Parish (Cathedral of the Plains) — Victoria," which records the 1877 plans by benefactor Walter C. Maxwell and the 1 June 1880 cornerstone; the several early buildings' exact sequence varies slightly between sources, so the desk lists the dated anchors rather than a forced numbering).
- **In 1884, during the building of that larger church, the parish name changed from Mother of Sorrows Church to St. Fidelis Church** — taking the name of a martyred Capuchin, Fidelis of Sigmaringen, after the friars' arrival. — VERIFIED (National Catholic Register: "It was during the building of the third church, in 1884, when the name was changed from Mother of Sorrows Church to St. Fidelis Church in honor of a martyred member of the Capuchin Franciscan order").
- A two-story native-stone **Capuchin monastery was built 1901–1903** adjoining the church, also housing a preparatory seminary of the order. — VERIFIED (National Park Service, National Register nomination form for St. Fidelis Catholic Church, 1971).

## The present church (1908–1911)

- Under pastor **Fr. Jerome Mueller, O.F.M. Cap.**, the parish resolved to build "a spacious and artistic church that would be a worthy house of God for present and future ages," engaging **John T. Comes of Pittsburgh**, one of the country's foremost church architects; Topeka architect **John Marshall** slightly modified the plans (1907). The church stands on **10 acres donated by the Kansas Pacific Railway Company**. — VERIFIED (Catholic Shrine Basilica, "Basilica of St Fidelis – Victoria Kansas," for Mueller, Comes, Marshall; SAH Archipedia for the 1907 modification; National Catholic Register for the railway land).
- Groundbreaking began **November 1908**. The first contractor, Blanchard Construction of Topeka (bid $49,375), fell into litigation and bankruptcy; a new contract went to **E.F.A. Clark Construction of Topeka (bid $54,785) in June 1909, and "they completed the construction in 1911."** The cornerstone was laid **4 October 1909**; the building was **dedicated Sunday, 27 August 1911**; total construction cost is estimated at **$132,000**. The exterior is native limestone **quarried seven miles south of Victoria**; walls run three feet thick at the sanctuary base and four feet at the towers; capacity 1,100; and "when constructed it was the largest Catholic church west of the Mississippi river." — VERIFIED (Catholic Shrine Basilica for the contracts and the quarry; National Register nomination form for the cornerstone, dedication date, $132,000 estimate, and the largest-west-of-the-Mississippi finding; SAH Archipedia for the wall thicknesses).
- The church was entered on the **National Register of Historic Places in 1971**. — VERIFIED (SAH Archipedia; NPS nomination form).

## "Cathedral of the Plains"

- **William Jennings Bryan** visited soon after the dedication and named the church from its twin spires rising off the flat land. — VERIFIED (National Register nomination form; SAH Archipedia: "William Jennings Bryan visited the building soon thereafter and called it 'the cathedral of the plains'").
  - **Wording variant, flagged honestly:** the 1971 National Register nomination — the earliest documentary form the desk found — records Bryan's phrase as **"Cathedral of the Prairies,"** adding: "Common usage has changed it to 'Cathedral of the Plains.'" Later sources (Volga German Institute; SAH) print "Cathedral of the Plains" as Bryan's own coinage. The desk reports the nomination form's wording as the older witness and the familiar name as the settled usage.

## Basilica (2014)

- On **21 February 2014** the church "was decreed by Pope Francis to be a basilica, the first in Kansas." — VERIFIED (Volga German Institute).
- After Vatican approval in early March 2014, **Bishop Edward Weisenburger of the Diocese of Salina dedicated St. Fidelis Church as a Minor Basilica on Saturday, June 7, 2014**. It remains **the only basilica in Kansas**. — VERIFIED (Catholic Shrine Basilica for the dedication; SAH Archipedia: "In 2014, St. Fidelis Church was dedicated a Minor Basilica, the only one in Kansas").
