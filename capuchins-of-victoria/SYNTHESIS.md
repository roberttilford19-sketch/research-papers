## The story in one breath

In 1525 an Italian friar named Matteo da Bascio sewed a long pointed hood to his habit and walked back toward St. Francis's poverty; the people named his followers for the hood — Capuchins — and in 1528 the bull *Religionis zelus* made them an order-in-the-making, independent by 1619, sworn to the strictest poverty of the Franciscan family, to hours of prayer, and to ministry among the common people. Three and a half centuries later, Bismarck's Kulturkampf threw the Bavarian Capuchins out of Germany; they landed at Pittsburgh in 1873. In that same decade, on the other side of the world, the czar broke Catherine the Great's century-old promises to the German colonists on the Volga, and the colonists of Herzog, Russia — founded 1764 — sold what they had, crossed at Baltimore in November 1875, wintered in Topeka, and planted a second Herzog on the Kansas prairie in the spring of 1876, half a mile north of George Grant's English town of Victoria. In January 1878 Bishop Fink of Leavenworth and the Capuchin commissary Fr. Hyacinth Epp rode out to Herzog together; that May, friars Hau and Mueller arrived to take the parish, and the friars have never left. The farmers outgrew church after church, changed the parish's name in 1884 to honour a Capuchin martyr, St. Fidelis, built a stone monastery in 1901–1903, and then — hauling limestone from seven miles south — built the largest Catholic church west of the Mississippi (1908–1911), which William Jennings Bryan named from its spires and which Pope Francis made Kansas's only basilica in 2014. The chain closes where it began: Epp's Kansas mission belonged to the Pittsburgh province until 12 April 1977, when the Province of St. Conrad was carved out of it — and the new province was solemnly established two days later **inside St. Fidelis Church, Victoria**, under the patronage of St. Conrad of Parzham, chosen, the diocesan paper of the day reports, because of Conrad's promise to his fellow Bavarian Hyacinth Epp, "who established the Capuchins both in Pennsylvania and Kansas." Robert's neighbouring basilica is not merely served by the province; in a real sense the province was born in it.

## Strongest verified find

**The province was founded at Victoria.** Two independent sources interlock: the province's own commissioned 2019 audit fixes the legal division of the St. Augustine Province at **12 April 1977**, and the Salina diocese's contemporary newspaper, *The Chancellor* (28 January 1977), reports the establishment ceremonies for the new Mid-America province set for **14 April 1977 at St. Fidelis Church in Victoria** — with the province's patron, St. Conrad, chosen for the sake of Fr. Hyacinth Epp, the friar who brought the Capuchins to Herzog in 1878. St. Fidelis is not a posting at the edge of the Province of St. Conrad; it is the province's birthplace, and the current provincial, Fr. David Songy, was elected at a chapter held in Victoria in May 2025 (capuchins.org). The through-line Epp → Conrad → Victoria is the single strongest thread in the wave.

## Surprises, honestly labelled

- **The desk's province candidate was right** — St. Conrad, Denver — but incomplete in an interesting direction: the province's first headquarters was **Hays, Kansas** (1977), then Kansas City (1980), then Denver. Kansas did not just receive the province; it housed it.
- **Bryan's phrase, in the earliest document, is "Cathedral of the Prairies."** The 1971 National Register nomination records: Bryan "called it the 'Cathedral of the Prairies.' Common usage has changed it to 'Cathedral of the Plains.'" Later sources print "Plains" as Bryan's own word. Both are carried in RETURN-CAP-2; the desk does not blend them.
- **The arrival day of the first friars is genuinely unsettled**: Laing's Kansas State Historical Society history gives 11 May 1878; the settlers' own 1926 Golden Jubilee book gives 15 May 1878. May 1878 is certain; the day is NOT FOUND at the level of agreement.
- **The province's own website misdates its order's founding bull** (1525 for the 1528 *Religionis zelus*) — a small thing, corrected in RETURN-CAP-1 from Britannica, the order's general site, and the reference record.
- **A living friary, not a monument**: in the spring of 2026 the St. Fidelis friars were advertising in their own bulletin for a paid cook. The 1903 monastery's successor house still keeps brothers at table a century and a quarter on.
