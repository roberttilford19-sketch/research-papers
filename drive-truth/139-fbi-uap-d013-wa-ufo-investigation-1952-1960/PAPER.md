## Premise

The file gathers the Seattle Field Office's paperwork on flying-saucer reports from Washington State in the 1950s, released under the Freedom of Information and Privacy Acts, as the review stamp on its opening pages indicates. Like other Bureau files of the period, it was produced to record referrals, newspaper coverage, and interviews, and to show what the office did with the reports it received. It is a record of administrative handling, assembled case by case, rather than a study of the phenomenon itself.

What can be established from the legible portions is the file's shape. It opens with a Seattle file cover sheet bearing a FOIPA review stamp. An early item is a clipping from the Columbia Basin News of Pasco, Washington, dated July 29, 1952, reporting that a local man had observed a disc-like object the previous night, with the account touching on the Pasco and Kennewick area. Later legible fragments show Air Force Office of Special Investigations material associated with McChord Air Force Base, Washington, including correspondence about reported objects. The file documents that sightings in the region were reported to, and passed among, the Bureau, the Air Force, and the local press. It records no Bureau conclusion about the objects reported, and this assessment draws none from it.

## Content Account

Because so much of the text layer is garbled, the content account is necessarily an account of the file's components rather than of its details. The components visible in the extraction are: a file cover and FOIPA review markings identifying the Seattle office; newspaper clippings from the Tri-Cities area press in the summer of 1952, at the height of that year's national wave of saucer reports, describing a disc-like object seen at night near Pasco; Air Force Office of Special Investigations correspondence connected with McChord Air Force Base; and standard Bureau memoranda and routing forms. The one substantive point the legible text supports is procedural: reports in this region moved between citizens, newspapers, the Air Force, and the FBI, and each agency's paper was filed with the others. Every description of an object in the file originates with a witness or a newspaper, as recorded by an agent, and is attributed to that source here.
