## Premise

The file is the Newark Field Office's accumulated paperwork on unidentified flying object reports in northern New Jersey between 1952 and 1967. It was produced in the ordinary course of Bureau and Air Force business: other agencies referred sightings and photographs to the FBI, agents recorded what citizens reported, the office clipped newspaper coverage, and the results were filed. The file exists to document that this correspondence and these inquiries took place, not to argue any conclusion about the objects reported.

## Abstract

The extracted text of this file runs to 172,795 characters across roughly sixty-six numbered release pages, and it is heavily degraded by optical character recognition errors. Its earliest legible item is an August 1952 letter from the Air Force's 20th District Office of Special Investigations in New York to the Special Agent in Charge at Newark, transmitting an Air Technical Intelligence Center memorandum and a letter from a Passaic newspaper staff writer, and asking the Bureau to investigate the background and reliability of John H. Riley of Paterson, New Jersey, who had photographed alleged unidentified aerial objects, and of a witness named George J. Stock of Passaic. A February 1958 Bureau memorandum records a Union, New Jersey, resident's telephone report of a bullet-shaped, silver object seen from her kitchen window, and notes that the Office of Special Investigations at McGuire Air Force Base was notified. The balance of the file consists of newspaper clippings, principally from Newark papers, a letter from the Westwood, New Jersey, police department, correspondence touching on a civilian aerial-phenomena association, routine index search slips, and closing administrative forms. Names and passages are redacted throughout. The file documents referral and record-keeping, and on its own pages it reaches no finding about any sighting it records.

## Content Account

The document's own contents, as distinct from any conclusion a reader might draw, fall into four groups. First, inter-agency referral correspondence: the 1952 Office of Special Investigations letter described in the abstract is the clearest example, and it shows the Air Force asking the FBI to vet a civilian photographer and witness rather than to investigate the objects themselves. Second, citizen sighting reports taken down by agents, of which the 1958 Union, New Jersey, memorandum is representative; the memorandum records the witness's description, the time, and the notification of the Air Force, and nothing further. Third, press material clipped and filed, including Newark newspaper items and a police-department letter from Westwood, New Jersey, which the office evidently kept as part of the local record. Fourth, administrative paper: index search slips, routing material, and a "Record of Information Furnished Other Agencies" form near the end of the release. The file's claims are therefore modest ones, made by the documents themselves: that particular reports were received, particular people were named, and particular notifications were made. Any statement in the file about what a witness saw is the witness's statement as recorded by an agent, and this assessment attributes it accordingly.

## Errors and Anomalies

The dominant anomaly is the condition of the text layer. Optical character recognition has garbled names, dates, and whole pages; some pages are reduced to fragments and stray characters, and a reader cannot rely on any single spelling in this extraction without checking the page image. The release also contains blank or near-blank form pages and duplicated administrative sheets, which is normal for a file release of this era but inflates the page count relative to the substantive content. Redactions appear throughout and are not marked consistently in the extracted text. The filename's date range, 1952–1967, is consistent with the dated items legible in the text, but the latest pages are administrative and their dates could not be confirmed from the extraction.

## Limits

This assessment is based on the extracted text held on disk, read in full-span samples; the underlying page images were not examined, and badly garbled passages could not be recovered. No attempt was made to verify the file against the FBI's own release records, to identify redacted persons, or to evaluate any sighting report the file contains. Nothing in this assessment endorses or disputes any witness account recorded in the file.
