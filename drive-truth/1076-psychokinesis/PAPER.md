## Abstract

This extract summarizes a Japanese experimental report in which researchers attempted to alter the hardness of a pure annealed aluminum plate by psychokinesis alone, without any known dynamic means, and compared cases believed to have been acted on by psychokinesis with cases believed not to have been affected. Because a single claimed psychokinetic action could not be measured directly, the researchers took many hardness measurements and treated the question statistically, asking whether the distribution of measurements differed between the two kinds of case. The extract states the report's conclusions as follows. The distribution of the resulting psychokinetic hardening was normal, and the standard deviation was not changed to any extent, but the average hardness values became slightly larger. The degree of hardening was exceedingly small, with the maximum value on average at a level of 1.03 to 1.04 times the comparison value. Hardness did not depend on the area touched or the location measured, non-touched and touched aluminum were hardened by roughly similar values, and the hardness level did not depend on the length of time of the claimed action. Where psychokinesis showed noticeable hardness, successive actions tended to result in softening, and in rare cases softening could be caused. The authors present the report as a first step, examining methods and results, with problems remaining.
