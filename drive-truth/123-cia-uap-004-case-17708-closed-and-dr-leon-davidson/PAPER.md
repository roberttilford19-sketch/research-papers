## Premise

## Abstract

This declassified Central Intelligence Agency memorandum, dated 9 January 1958 from the Chief of the Chicago Office to the Chief of the Contact Division, reports the closing handling of case 17708, concerning Dr. Leon Davidson. The memorandum states that Davidson was told by telephone that his problem concerning a space message and its transmitter could not be resolved because records on the transmitter had been destroyed by the transmitting agency. It reports that Davidson accepted the statement, noted he had been told it before, mentioned a second article he had prepared for publication concerning Air Force handling of sightings in the light of Pentagon security review, and hinted he might raise the matter again. The memorandum is candid about the answer's character: it states that the noncommittal and evasive answer the office was instructed to give was perhaps the only one possible if previous statements by the Agency and other involved agencies were not to be crossed, while also stating that the answer was hardly fair to Davidson and unlikely to be fully accepted by him. It further records the office's view that Davidson knew he was dealing with Agency contacts despite an effort to conceal their identities, and it closes by predicting that more would be heard from him.

## Content Account

The memorandum is headed from the Chief of the Chicago Office to the Chief of the Contact Division, Support Branch, dated 9 January 1958, and references a Washington teletype and a telephone call of 8 January 1958. Its first paragraph reports that Davidson was contacted by telephone and advised that his problem concerning the space message and its transmitter could not be resolved because records on the transmitter had been destroyed by the transmitting agency. It reports Davidson's response: acceptance, the comment that he had been told this before, mention of a second article for publication concerning Air Force handling of sightings and the Pentagon security review people, which he thought mild enough to pass them, and a hint that he might take the matter up again later.

The second paragraph gives the office's own assessment of the answer it delivered. In the memorandum's account, there had been many cooks in the kitchen on this matter, and the noncommittal and evasive answer the office was instructed to give was perhaps the only one possible if the Agency were to avoid crossing previous statements of its own and of other involved agencies. The memorandum states plainly that the answer was nevertheless hardly fair to Davidson and not likely to be fully accepted by him. The third paragraph addresses identity concealment: the referenced telephone record showed nothing to indicate Davidson knew he was dealing with Agency contacts, and an effort had been made to conceal their identity from him, but the office states its assurance that, when Davidson wrote to them in the Court of Appeals Building conference room, he knew very well with whom he was dealing. The memorandum closes with a one-line prediction that more will be heard from Davidson, and is signed in the extraction by R. P. B. Lohmann.
