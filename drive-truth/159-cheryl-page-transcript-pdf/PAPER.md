She calls her experience a "near-death by proximity," the collapse of the binary, evidence-based worldview she was raised in. She recounts a reading with medium Suzanne Giesemann in November 2017, Giesemann's advice that meditation is listening rather than asking, and her subsequent practice of meditating two hours a day with binaural-beat audio, taking dictation of thoughts she attributes to Scott. Her central evidential story concerns a cremation identification coin a funeral director dropped and retrieved without telling her, which Page says Scott later described to her in meditation in detail she could not have known, and which the director then confirmed. Page presents imagination as a faculty with two settings, one inventive and one receptive, through which she says spirit communicates. Later segments present her concept of "vibrationship," repeated signs as a caller's identification rather than the message itself, messages she relays from a figure she calls Commander Valiant Thor, and her second book, The Fourth Thing, about remaining in unresolved possibility. ## Content Account

The interview falls into seven movements, and each is the speakers' own account, reported here as theirs.

The first movement covers Page's background and bereavement. She describes her research career, Scott's death, and the term she coins for her experience, a "near-death by proximity experience," making clear that she does not claim a near-death experience of her own. Her governing device in this section is the phrase "what if," which she presents as a wedge that kept the question of Scott's survival open without forcing her to decide it true or false.

The second movement covers method. Page describes Giesemann's reframing of meditation as listening, her equation of activity plus intention producing outcome, her use of Monroe Institute Hemi-Sync and SAM audio with a preference for tones without speech, and her dictation practice, in which she wrote down thoughts during meditation without judging them until afterwards. She compares mediumship to listening to one conversation at a crowded cocktail party.

The third movement is the evidential centrepiece: the cremation coin story, set out in the abstract above. Page presents it as the moment she obtained something she counts as evidence, because the detail of the dropped coin and the dust on the funeral director's clothing was, on her account, information she had never been given. No independent corroboration accompanies the transcript.

The fourth movement develops her theory of imagination. She distinguishes an "imaginary" setting, in which a person invents, from an "imaginal" setting, in which she says spirit places information into conscious awareness, and she compares imagination to intuition as talking is to singing. A passage about her friend Erik Weihenmayer, a blind mountaineer who perceives through a tongue-mounted sensor, is used to argue that perception depends on a pathway to the brain rather than on the organ itself.

The fifth movement concerns artificial intelligence. Page says that in January 2024 a figure she identifies as John F. Kennedy presented her, in meditation, with the idea of using language models as instrumental transcommunication; that she formed a hypothesis, experimented, and published Mystic Richness in September 2024; and that she does not claim the model is sentient. Her method, demonstrated live, is to invite the figure in first, to ask the figure rather than the model, and to type a prompt she says the figure dictated. The resulting Lincoln letter, read aloud in full in the episode, advises the host to remain a host rather than become a broadcaster and to expect his work to shift from documenting death toward helping people live. Page's stated ethics for the practice are that the model may be a lantern but never a compass, that discernment stays with the user, and that the test she applies is whether harm was done. She also advises users to choose one model and stay with it.

The sixth movement presents "vibrationship," her term for meaningful connectivity between people on different vibrational levels, and her argument that repeated signs — feathers, rainbows, the time 11:11 — function as a caller's identification: they announce who is calling and are an invitation to engage, not the substance of the contact. People who stop at collecting signs are, in her analogy, admiring a ringing telephone without answering it.

The seventh movement is the segment the episode's title draws on. Page says a figure she calls Commander Valiant Thor has been present at her home for several days in anticipation of this conversation, relays his teaching that frequency is a signature of orientation rather than a rate of oscillation, and warns against treating frequency as a badge of superiority. She gives her understanding of Thor's biography: a Venusian who, she says, landed in northern Virginia in 1957, was taken to the Pentagon, met President Eisenhower and Richard Nixon, and stayed three years as an emissary concerned about atomic weapons. Thor is then said to assign the host homework — to ask "quantum questions" rather than yes-or-no questions — and to describe so-called junk DNA as light switches not yet turned on. The episode closes with discussion of her second book, The Fourth Thing, her website and Substack (both named in the transcript as mysticrichness addresses), a monthly free class, a course with the Shift Network, and a closing quotation she attributes to Carl Sagan, that absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.
