## Premise

The document, dated November 1979, is the project description and experimental protocol for "Remote Perturbation Techniques," prepared at the United States Army Missile Command at Redstone Arsenal, Alabama, with Billy Z. Jenkins as principal investigator, and approved by the Missile Command's Grill Flame oversight committee. It was released in 2001 under CIA-RDP96-00788R002000230004-2. The document was produced to describe, and to obtain approval for, a controlled experiment testing whether a person could, by mental processes alone, bias the output of a random event generator, and to set out in advance the apparatus, the variables, the testing procedures, the volunteer consent arrangements, and the medical and emergency provisions that the work would require.

## Abstract

Remote perturbation is defined in the document as an intellectual and mental process by which a person perturbs remote sensitive apparatus or equipment, without electronic sensing devices at or focused on the agent, and without drugs, hypnosis, special sensory stimuli, or electromagnetic means. The protocol reviews a database of earlier experiments in which subjects attempted to perturb the outcome of random events, and argues that the apparent effect is unlikely to be an artefact of selective reporting, while identifying two weaknesses in that database: the effects were rarely stable for a single individual, being averages over many individuals, and the physical environment of the random sources and electronics was nowhere discussed in detail, leaving open the possibility of subtle electronic interference. The proposed experiment addresses both weaknesses directly. It would use a small number of participants, nine or fewer, who had shown previous expertise as remote viewers; sources of randomness of understood construction, namely beta decay, an electronic noise diode, and a deterministic pseudo-random generator as a control; battery power and optically coupled signals to isolate the sources from environmental influence; real-time statistical analysis using sequential analysis; and immediate visual and auditory feedback to the participant. Testing would proceed in two phases, an exhaustive validation phase and a participant phase with a pilot period followed by a final test period.

## Content Account

### Background and the case for the experiment

The background section is candid about the literature it rests on. It states that many reported accounts of telekinesis, psychokinesis, and teleportation are clearly derived from carefully staged tricks which are revealed whenever they are studied under controlled and well recorded conditions, and that a few describe serious research by reputable investigators. The appealing feature of the random-event experiments, in the document's account, is that they involve no subjective interpretation, since the results can be expressed entirely in probabilistic terms. Appendix A summarises that database. The document's overall evaluation is that it is unlikely the apparent effect is simply an artefact of selected reporting by the laboratories involved, because even on the assumption of ten unreported non-significant experiments for each reported significant one, the expanded database would still show significant effects with odds against chance of better than two thousand to one. That evaluation is the document's own, and it depends on the assumption stated; it is reported here as the document's reasoning, not adopted as a finding.

The scientific merit section argues that the interpretation of quantum physics is flexible enough to encompass a number of seeming paradoxes, that some scientists had suggested quantum mechanisms to explain remote perturbation without violating the basic conservation theorems of physics, and that these hypotheses, while they do not prove the existence of the phenomenon, indicate that further research is appropriate when coupled with the database findings. It states the military interest plainly: in view of the value of being able to disturb sensitive enemy equipment such as computers, locks, switches, and codes, it is to the Army's advantage to assess the validity of the claims. The objective is correspondingly narrow. The experiment is designed to replicate the type of experiment in the database under more rigorously controlled conditions in order to evaluate the claims of having demonstrated the phenomenon, and the document acknowledges that the risk of failure is extremely great while the potential return on investment is correspondingly great. It records that the project was the first Grill Flame investigation of remote perturbation, the other projects concerning remote viewing, and it traces the programme's authority to the Commander of the Army Materiel Development and Readiness Command's approval in principle in April 1978 and the Assistant Chief of Staff for Intelligence's acceptance of lead responsibility in May 1978, with overall Department of Defense responsibility residing with the Defense Intelligence Agency.

### The experimental design

In its simplest form, as the document states it, a truly random sequence of zeros and ones is generated by processing and sampling the behaviour of a noise source or random event generator; the participant is instructed to bias the composition of the sequence in favour of ones or zeros by exercising mental processes; feedback is provided so that the participant can be aware almost instantaneously of the degree to which he is succeeding; and the associated computer equipment simultaneously carries out a statistical analysis of the sequence until it can be deemed perturbed or not, whereupon the trial ends.

Three sources of randomness were to be used. The radioactive decay of promethium 147, detected through its beta emissions, and an electronic noise diode subject to avalanche discharge, both produce events that are Poisson distributed in time and whose theory the document describes as well understood and well verified experimentally; each detected event triggers a flip-flop between zero and one, and the device is sampled at intervals much longer than the average event rate to produce the binary sequence. A pseudo-random binary sequence, calculated by the computer from a starting seed and therefore deterministic for any given seed, would also be used as needed, on the reasoning that a great deal could be determined about the nature of the phenomenon from the difference, if any, in the degree to which truly random and deterministic events are affected. Appendix B adds carbon 14 as a second beta source under consideration and a pseudo-random shift register as the control source, all optically coupled to an LSI-11 microcomputer.

The document is explicit about a tacit assumption and about checking it. It had been assumed that any effect would act upon the random event generator; that assumption would be checked by recording the incidence of random events, the resulting binary sequence, and the output of the statistical analysis on magnetic disc storage for subsequent stage-to-stage analysis, together with the recorded feedback channel, so that the source of any perturbation could be isolated as lying in the random source, the computer equipment, or the display equipment. Feedback would be provided through a colour video display driven by the statistical analysis, with preprogrammed displays ranging from a simple clock motion or race-car displacement to the progress of the statistical path in the sequential analysis diagram, and through an auditory channel in which the frequency or intensity of a tone varies to indicate perturbation; the participant would choose the mode he found most effective.

The design answers the database's two weaknesses in the ways summarised in the abstract. Phase I consists of assembly and checkout of the apparatus and exhaustive validation testing over a wide range of environmental and testing conditions, to assure the randomness of the binary sequence and the proper functioning of the computer and displays. Phase II is the participant testing. There are three primary independent variables: the presence or absence of a participant, the random source, and the mode of feedback. The dependent variable is the quantitative measure, from the statistical analysis, of the degree to which the composition of the sequence differs from what would be expected by chance. For individual trials, the criterion for success is that the odds against chance expectation be greater than twenty to one. The first four months of the investigation would be a pilot period in which participants familiarised themselves with the experiment and explored techniques in a learning mode and device parameters were optimised; the final two months would be the formal test period.

### Administration, consent, and safety

The later sections and appendices are administrative in character and are part of what makes the document a protocol rather than a report of results. They cover the participants and the relation the document posits between remote perturbation and remote viewing, the facilities, with the experiment to take place in Building 7770 at Redstone Arsenal, a volunteer consent form as Appendix D, and, as Appendix E, medical facilities and procedures: accident and illness reporting, the designation of Fox Army Hospital at Redstone Arsenal for cases occurring on post, an emergency response programme, first-aid procedures, and insurance coverage, which the document divides into three classes with different entitlements, SRI employees in California under state workers' compensation, United States government employees under the federal equivalent, and individual consultants as independent contractors, who would have to look to their own personal insurance. The references run to thirty-five items, dominated by the random-number-generator literature of the 1970s.
