## Premise

The file is a short piece of United States naval administrative correspondence from December 1948. It was produced to pass a Chief of Naval Operations instruction down to naval district activities: if flying discs or other unidentified aerial objects were sighted, stations were to report the sightings promptly through naval intelligence channels to the United States Air Force, and to obtain photographic evidence whenever possible. The document establishes a reporting procedure. It does not investigate, confirm, or explain any particular sighting.

## Abstract

This staged assessment describes a three-page declassified United States government file dated 1948, held in Robert Tilford's Drive as part of a Department of War unidentified aerial phenomena release series. The first page is a file cover from the Office, Chief of Army Field Forces, marked "A. G. Files," bearing a declassification authority stamp, NND 740185, and a closed-file designation for 1948. The second page, the substantive document, is a 13 December 1948 letter from Headquarters, Fifth Naval District, Norfolk, Virginia, in which the Commandant transmits a confidential Chief of Naval Operations letter to activities on a distribution list. The quoted letter states that the Director of Intelligence, U.S. Air Force, had informed the Navy Department that a cycle of reappearance of "Flying Discs" was becoming apparent and that a new interval was considered imminent. It directs all stations to report any sighting of flying discs or other unidentified aerial objects to the nearest Air Force command and to the Naval District Intelligence Office, to obtain photographic evidence whenever possible, and to forward information through district intelligence officers to the Chief of Naval Intelligence for passage to the Air Force by the fastest means. The third page is a faint reverse-side sheet bearing receipt and routing markings that optical character recognition could recover only in fragments. The file records reporting procedure, not any investigated sighting.

## Content

The file consists of three scanned pages.

The first page is a file cover. Optical character recognition recovered the year 1948, a "DATE CLOSED" heading, and the wording "Office, Chief Army Field Forces" and "A. G. Files." The file's embedded text layer carries the declassification authority line "Authority: NND 740185." The cover identifies the file as a closed 1948 Adjutant General file of the Office, Chief of Army Field Forces, and it contains no substantive discussion of flying discs.

The second page carries the document's substance. It is a letter on the letterhead of Headquarters, Fifth Naval District, Norfolk 11, Virginia, dated 13 December 1948, from the Commandant, Fifth Naval District, addressed to the activities indicated in its distribution list. Its subject is "'Flying Discs' — Report of." The body of the letter quotes a confidential Chief of Naval Operations letter, reference (a), for information and compliance. In the quoted letter, the Chief of Naval Operations states that the Director of Intelligence, U.S. Air Force, has informed the Navy Department that a cycle of reappearance of "Flying Discs" is becoming apparent and that the beginning of a new interval is imminent, and that the Air Force has requested that any such sightings be reported as quickly as possible and that the phenomena be investigated. The quoted letter then directs that all stations report any reported sighting of "Flying Discs" or other unidentified aerial objects to the nearest Air Force command and to the Naval District Intelligence Office, and that photographic evidence of the phenomena be obtained whenever possible. It further states that district intelligence officers will forward such information to the Chief of Naval Intelligence, who will pass the information to the Director of Intelligence, U.S. Air Force, by the fastest means. The quoted letter is signed "Thos. B. Inglis" with the notation "By direction," and the Fifth Naval District letter itself is signed by R. C. Hudson, Chief of Staff. A distribution list follows, naming distribution categories 1A, 2A, 3A, 6A, 7A, and an 85-copy entry, with numbered recipients and one copy each for most entries; optical character recognition recovered this list only in part.

The third page is a faint sheet that appears to be the reverse side of the correspondence, showing receipt and distribution-center markings and handwritten file notations showing through the paper. Optical character recognition recovered only fragments, including the words "DISTRIBUTION CENTER" and a December 1948 date fragment. No substantive text could be recovered from this page by the methods described in the audit log.

Two things should be kept separate. The document's own claims are limited to what the quoted Chief of Naval Operations letter asserts: that the Air Force Director of Intelligence had reported an apparent cycle of reappearance of flying discs and an imminent new interval. Those assertions are attributed here to the document and to the officers it quotes, and this assessment does not adopt them as findings. The findings that this assessment can support from the recovered text are narrower: in December 1948 the Fifth Naval District circulated a Navy-wide reporting instruction for flying disc sightings, the instruction routed reports through naval district intelligence offices to the Chief of Naval Intelligence and onward to the Air Force, and it asked for photographic evidence whenever possible.

## Errors and Anomalies

The recovered text carries optical character recognition errors throughout, concentrated in the letterhead reference block, the signature and routing annotations, and the distribution list. The cited reference, reference (a), could not be recovered reliably: its serial number and its exact November 1948 date are garbled in the recognition output, and this assessment therefore does not state them. Handwritten annotations on the second page, including routing initials beside the signature block, were not recovered legibly. The third page is a low-contrast, show-through image in which the printed text is largely reversed and faint, and recognition produced only scattered fragments. The original classification markings on the pages are struck through, and the first page carries the declassification authority stamp noted above; those markings are part of the file as released and are not errors.

## Limits

This assessment rests on optical character recognition of the scanned pages, not on a usable text layer, because the file's extracted text measures only 56 characters across its three pages. Page 2, the substantive letter, was recovered well enough to support the account above, including its three operative paragraphs. Page 1 was recovered only in part, and page 3 was recovered only in fragments, so any content unique to the third page is not represented here. Details that recognition garbled, including the reference letter's serial number and exact date, the full distribution list, and the handwritten routing notes, are stated as unresolved rather than reconstructed. No independent check against another copy of this file in a public archive was performed, so the file's archival provenance beyond its own markings and its place in the release series named in its filename is not verified here.
