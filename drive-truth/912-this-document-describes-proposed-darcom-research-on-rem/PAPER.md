## Premise

The file combines two things. Its core is a declassified United States Army research proposal describing an experiment on remote perturbation, the proposed mental influencing of random event generators, prepared under the Department of Defense program called GRILL FLAME. Around that core, the file records a long AI working session that analyzes the proposal and then extends it, at the author's direction, into low-cost tests a solo practitioner could run and into speculation about psychokinesis applied to electronic systems and artificial intelligence.

## Abstract

The proposal reproduced in the file, stamped approved for release on April 2, 2001, under the citation CIA-RDP96-00788R002000230004-2, describes an experiment led by Dr. B. Z. Jenkins at the United States Army Missile Laboratory at Redstone Arsenal, Alabama, with a duplicate experiment at SRI International. Participants, no more than nine experienced remote viewers, would attempt to bias binary sequences produced by a prometheum-147 decay source, an electronic noise diode, or a pseudo-random generator, with real-time visual or auditory feedback and a success criterion of odds against chance greater than twenty to one. The proposal reports no results; it is a plan, with validation and testing phases, safeguards against deception, and criteria for calling the outcome positive, negative, or indeterminate. The session material that follows the proposal first summarizes and analyzes it, then, under instructions to assume its claims, derives streamlined solo methods using coins, dice, pendulums, candles, cards, free applications, and a micro SD card, with simplified success thresholds, and finally lists twenty-five speculative ways psychokinesis might affect artificial intelligence systems. Those derivations are the session's constructions, not contents of the government document.

## Content

The government document at the file's core defines remote perturbation as an intellectual or mental process by which a person perturbs remote sensitive apparatus, expressly without electronic sensing devices at the agent, and without drugs, hypnosis, or electrical or electromagnetic stimulation. It states that the Commander of the Army Materiel Development and Readiness Command approved Army involvement in GRILL FLAME in principle in April 1978, that the Assistant Chief of Staff for Intelligence accepted lead responsibility for applications the following month, and that overall Department of Defense responsibility rested with the Defense Intelligence Agency. The principal investigator is named as Dr. B. Z. Jenkins of the Systems Simulation and Development Directorate, United States Army Missile Laboratory, Redstone Arsenal, Alabama. The document is marked SECRET and NOFORN in its original form, with dissemination controlled by the originator, and carries the CIA release stamp quoted in the abstract.

On the science, the document is candid in both directions. It states that most reported accounts of telekinesis and psychokinesis are derived from staged tricks revealed under controlled conditions, but it also states that a small number of experiments by reputable investigators, in which subjects attempted to bias otherwise random events, showed effects it assesses as unlikely to be an artifact of selective reporting alone, citing odds against chance of better than 2,000 to one across the expanded database it describes. It names two weaknesses in that database: effects were rarely stable for a single individual, and the physical environment of the random sources was not described in detail, leaving subtle electronic interference open as an explanation. It describes quantum-mechanical suggestions, attributed to Mattuck, under which macroscopic effects might be consistent with physical conservation laws, while stating plainly that such hypotheses do not prove the phenomenon exists.

The experimental plan is the document's substance. Two genuinely random sources, prometheum-147 beta decay and an avalanche noise diode, each drive a flip-flop that is sampled at intervals to yield a binary sequence, and a deterministic pseudo-random sequence is included so that any difference in effect between truly random and calculated sequences can be examined. A computer performs sequential statistical analysis in real time and drives feedback displays, including clock motion and race-car displacement, plus an auditory channel. Phase one is assembly and exhaustive validation, including Monte Carlo modeling of the noise sources. Phase two is participant testing: a four-month pilot period, then two months in which each participant contributes one hundred trials, with a trial counted as perturbed when the odds against chance exceed twenty to one and a participant counted as significant when more than ten of the hundred trials meet that criterion. The document states that no participant would be exposed to radiation or instrumented for physiological monitoring.

The safeguards section anticipates hostile scrutiny. The experiment is to be run twice with independently assembled equipment, at SRI International and at Redstone Arsenal, with tamperable equipment in a separate locked room, all phase-two data saved and nothing discarded, artificially biased sequences used to calibrate error rates, and the participant's presence or absence treated as the chief independent variable so that uncontrolled factors such as solar storms would have to act only in the participant's presence to explain an effect. The document also records two limits on interpretation: no attempt is made to isolate which individual present might be the agent of an effect, and no attempt is made to distinguish remote perturbation from precognition, which could produce the same result if a participant initiated trials in anticipation of favorable sequences. Outcomes are defined in advance as positive only if both experiments meet the overall criteria, negative only if neither does, and indeterminate otherwise. The stated military interest is the possibility of disturbing sensitive enemy equipment such as computers, locks, switches, and codes.

The session material appended to the proposal occupies most of the file. It begins with an analysis, summary, elaboration, list of key points, and set of hypotheses about the proposal, and that analysis appears twice in near-identical form. It then extracts the proposal's methodology step by step under an instruction to assume everything in it is true. The remaining session sections move progressively further from the source document: a streamlined methodology for a solo practitioner on a very small budget, substituting coins and dice for the random event generators; a fuller treatment of focused intention and attention as the operative elements, with scenarios involving random number applications, an online retro-psychokinesis experiment, and a radio; a round of deliberately simple tests aimed at glitching free applications and affecting a file on a micro SD card; a further round of no-cost practices using a pendulum, a candle flame, and shuffled cards; and a closing list of twenty-five ways psychokinesis might affect artificial intelligence, each with a feasibility note and a low probability estimate. Throughout these sections the session text includes its own fact-check passages stating that psychokinesis is not scientifically proven, that reported effect sizes are very small, and that success in any single trial is unlikely.

## Errors and Anomalies

The reproduction of the government document carries optical character recognition damage, including a section marker rendered as "(5)" where the classification marking is meant, a Greek beta rendered in a form resembling a German sharp s in the phrase about beta emission, and the word "Thia" for "This" in the description of the pilot period. The session portion contains one stray Chinese character inside an English sentence in its first analysis. The session's simplified statistics are approximations offered as such in the text; for example, its statement that seven or more heads in ten coin flips roughly corresponds to odds against chance greater than twenty to one does not match the proposal's own sequential analysis framework and was not adopted in this assessment. The prompts visible in the file at points direct the assistant toward targets the proposal does not contemplate, including missile guidance systems; the responses themselves repeatedly note that testing such systems is infeasible for a solo practitioner. The file's title is the opening line of the government document, not a title given to the session as a whole.
