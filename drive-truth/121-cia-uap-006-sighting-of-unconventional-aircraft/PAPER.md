## Abstract

This declassified Central Intelligence Agency information report records an account by a United States national who, travelling by train from Baku toward Tiflis on 4 October 1955, reported watching a triangular object being launched from an airfield between the railway and the Caspian Sea coast. The report describes the source as a 41-year-old publicity and advertising executive with no technical or aviation training, and it labels the information unevaluated. According to the account, a second traveller first drew attention to the object, after which the source and his companions watched a searchlight illuminate a triangular object, comparable in size to a United States jet fighter, with a light at each point. The source reported that the object was ejected from its launching site, made between three and seven fast spirals, and then climbed steeply at about a 45-degree angle while the searchlight followed it. The account adds that a steward drew the compartment blinds on the apparent instruction of a security man aboard the train, and that the party's wish to fly between Baku and Tiflis had been refused in Baku although flights were later said in Tiflis to exist. The report records the claim; it does not evaluate or corroborate it.

## Content Account

The report's header identifies the country as the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics, the place acquired as the Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic, and the date of information as 4 October 1955. A source description follows, identifying the informant as a United States national, 41 years old, a publicity and advertising vice president of a large corporation, educated in political science, with no technical training or experience in aviation, who had recently visited the Soviet Union as a tourist at the invitation of a senior Soviet official.

The narrative, in the source's first person, places him on a slow train from Baku to Tiflis, two hours and forty minutes out of Baku, when a companion from the adjoining compartment announced a flying saucer sighting. The source reports that the group then watched, from the left-hand side of the train, a large airfield where a searchlight shone on a triangular object on the ground, estimated on reflection at about two miles from the railroad. The object is described as comparable in size to a United States jet fighter, squat, in the form of an equilateral triangle, with three lights, one on each point. The source reports that it was ejected from its launching site, made not fewer than three and not more than seven fast spirals, and climbed extremely fast at about a 45-degree angle, followed by the searchlight. He emphasizes that this was, in his words, no ordinary take-off but a launching procedure more like a missile ejection, and reports his companion's statement that it was the second launching in rapid succession. The narrative then reports the drawing of the blinds, the party's position estimated at 50 to 65 miles south of Baku, and a closing discrepancy over the availability of flights between Baku and Tiflis.
