## Premise

The memorandum was written to place on record what sponsor and Stanford Research Institute (SRI) representatives agreed during a two-day visit in January 1974, at the start of a one-year, $150,000 sponsored program of paranormal investigations. It exists to fix the contractual base, the reporting schedule, the security and publication guidelines, and the research task list, including points deliberately left unresolved, so that both sides could later be held to the same account of the meeting.

## Abstract

This six-page memorandum for the record, dated 29 January 1974, documents a sponsor visit to the Stanford Research Institute on 23–25 January 1974, during which representatives met Drs. Cox, Jones, Puthoff, and Targ for about eleven hours to settle the terms of a one-year, $150,000 program of paranormal investigations. The memorandum records that the proposed work statement was adopted with modifications: greater flexibility in apportioning time among basic-research tasks, monthly brief reports with lengthy interim reports in May and September 1974, a final report in February 1975, and agreement that SRI's reports should be unclassified rather than secret. It details the handling guidelines adopted, including data-control restrictions, the limited list of sponsor personnel authorized to discuss the project, and arrangements for at least five detailed conferences during the project year. It also records topics discussed without final decision, among them clearing prominent test subjects, polygraphing the investigators, and advance sponsor review of a planned submission to the journal *Nature*. The task discussion covers concurrent medical, sensory, behavioral, and neurophysiological testing of subjects, a referee concept for judging results, and an agreed panel of three prominent subjects, three ordinary psychics, and three controls. Attachments referenced in the memorandum are not present in this copy. (203 words)

## Content Account

The memorandum opens by identifying the meeting: roughly eight hours of discussion on 24 January 1974 and three hours on 25 January, with Drs. Cox, Jones, Puthoff, and Targ of SRI, covering procedures, tasks, and guidelines, and reaching contractual agreement on a one-year effort valued at $150,000. The author states that the memorandum only elucidates the attached tasks and guidelines papers where necessary, and covers other agreements and unresolved points briefly for the record.

On the contract, the memorandum records that both SRI's and the sponsor's contracts officers were present and that the proposed work statement was adopted as the contractual base with modifications. The word "approximately" was to be inserted before each percentage listed under basic research, giving flexibility in apportioning time. Reporting was set as brief written reports each month, lengthy interim reports in May and September 1974, and a final report in February 1975, with each report covering technical progress and financial status for both the applied and basic research areas. After each interim report, the SRI investigators would visit Washington for conferences and possibly presentations to a broader sponsor audience. It was also agreed that SRI's reports should be unclassified rather than secret, subject to an internal approval step, and separately agreed that sponsor representatives should visit the SRI site at the midpoints between reports — April, July, and November — providing at least five detailed conferences in the project year.

On guidelines, the memorandum records that the data-control restrictions do not apply to cleared SRI personnel and consultants essential to special phases of the research, such as medical, sensory, behavioral, and neurophysiological testing. One named individual was added to the list of authorized sponsor personnel; that name is redacted in the released copy. The author describes lengthy discussion intended to make SRI understand that the authorized-personnel rules were practical and bureaucratic necessities rather than discrimination: SRI could discuss paranormal theory with other sponsor personnel, but not this project, without the project officer's approval. The memorandum also notes that the contract bought only seventy-five percent of the SRI investigators' time and that SRI then held two other contracts in the field, both redacted in the release.

Three security matters were discussed without all being settled. The possibility of clearing at least some of the prominent subjects, such as Pat Price, was raised so they could be handled more securely, with a named officer to look into its advisability. Traces (background checks) were to be initiated, and the idea of polygraphing the investigators at some point to support the credibility of the research was discussed; SRI was receptive, provided it had full access to conclusions unless the polygraphy were done for security purposes, and no definitive decision was reached on timing or mode. On publication, SRI mentioned an invitation from *Nature* to publish recent results and showed an early draft of an article focused largely on the Price out-of-body work; the sponsor would see the final draft before submission.

On tasks, the memorandum explains that annotations on the attached outline mark tasks to be implemented and tasks only to be considered. Testing of subjects (Part I) would run concurrently with the applied research rather than later, with all tests used at the outset in the hope that evidence by mid-year would permit greater focus. Neurophysiological testing (Part II) would be done as appropriate during the applied research and integrated with the medical, sensory, and behavioral results. The psychiatric portion might be omitted, subject to further discussion. SRI accepted the referee concept and might use its blue-ribbon panel for that purpose, but two named individuals would not be used because of a serious personality clash involving one of them. An additional guideline was agreed: the investigators would not themselves take part in experiments, for example as senders, with a single possible exception — Targ might be used once to establish a baseline with one possible subject, Hella Hamid, with such data identified and handled separately.

The subject panel was tentatively set at three prominent subjects (probably Price, Swann — spelled "Swan" in the memorandum — and a third yet to be identified, possibly Hamid or Elgin), three run-of-the-mill psychics, and three controls, not including any member of the research team. The sponsor might try to provide some testing equipment, with named officers to check on a radiometer and a polygraph. For the self-inventory checklist subjects would complete each test morning, the parties agreed to ask a named person whether an existing instrument might serve.
