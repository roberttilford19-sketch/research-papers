## Abstract

This document is a quarterly program report, dated 15 October 1987, from the DIA unit designated DT-S to DT, covering the third quarter of calendar year 1987 under the SUN STREAK program. Despite the Drive filename, it is not a paper about pendulum dowsing; the pendulum appears once, in a training paragraph recording that preliminary consideration was given to using the pendulum, dowsing, and automatic writing as adjuncts to remote viewing against search and time problems. The report's substance is operational accounting. It describes two utility assessments: Project P, a purely predictive effort in which six viewers completed seventy-seven sessions and produced weak correlations reported as thirteen and eighteen percent for its two periods, with a recommendation that the project be held in abeyance; and Project N-1, an attempt to access and describe a target document, which the report calls relatively successful and which received a favorable verbal evaluation. It also reports free-flight sessions against events in the Persian Gulf that produced no significant results, cross-training of viewers in two remote-viewing methodologies under an accelerated schedule, two staffing vacancies, and production tables totaling one hundred ninety-six sessions across six viewers. Several passages are redacted in the released copy.

## Content Account

The memorandum is addressed to DT, attention Dr. Vorona in the routing as extracted, and lists five enclosures: a quarterly production report, an operational project report, a utility assessments report, a training report, and, at a further tab, statistical results for the predictive project.

Its operations section leads with Project P, described as a utility assessment of whether a remote viewer can function effectively in a purely predictive mode. Seventy-seven sessions were conducted by six viewers between 17 November 1986 and 2 October 1987. Targeting was revised in June 1987 and a method for automatic weekly feedback was established. The memorandum reports weak correlations of thirteen percent and eighteen percent for the two periods under consideration, states that viewer enthusiasm had waned considerably, and suggests the project be held in abeyance pending an in-depth review.

Project N-1 is reported in opposite terms. It was designed to test whether viewers could access a target document and substantially determine the nature of its content. All four participating viewers are said to have readily accessed the target area; one failed to access the document altogether, a second provided data of limited value, and the remaining two described the substantial content of the document and provided conceptual drawings. The memorandum calls N-1 the first in a planned series of five such projects, and its value section records a favorable verbal evaluation from DT on 2 October 1987, with a written evaluation to follow. A second project, I-3, is recorded as having received a favorable verbal evaluation in early September 1987, with the customer's written evaluation still pending.

The memorandum also encloses draft lead sheets for possible use against foreign intelligence targets concerning military plans and intentions and terrorism, and reports an experiment in method rather than in results: four viewers conducted unmonitored free-flight sessions against events of foreign intelligence significance in the Persian Gulf, on the premise that near-time exposure to future events might improve access. The method produced no significant results against perceived future events, the memorandum says, but would be tried against real-time targets.

Personnel and training occupy the rest of the text. Two vacancies were expected from 1 November 1987. All viewers were being cross-trained in the Coordinate Remote Viewing and Extended Remote Viewing methodologies, individual viewers are tracked by number through training stages, and an intensive effort was under way to reduce training time from two years to six months. It is in this training section, and only there, that the subject of the Drive filename appears: preliminary efforts to consider the pendulum, dowsing, and automatic writing as adjuncts to remote viewing, against search and time problems, were initiated during the period. The production table credits six viewers with forty, forty-five, thirty-five, two, forty-five, and twenty-nine sessions respectively, one hundred ninety-six in total across operational projects, utility assessments, and training. A utility table for calendar year 1987 lists most completed assessments as useful, with N-1 and the predictive project still awaiting evaluation.
