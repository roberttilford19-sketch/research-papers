## Content Account

The record has four parts: a typed session report, the viewer's typed session summary, handwritten transcript pages, and a working-paper tasking sheet dated 3 February 1989.

The session report states the mission plainly: to access and describe a target structure shown in overhead photo imagery. The viewer tasking reads: "Continuing in Stage 4 with yesterday's target, understand the encrypted coordinates to be the inside of the structure you found. Access and describe its contents and purpose." The monitor's comments record an inclemency — a chronic cough — and state that viewer 095 developed excellent site contact, giving detailed descriptors of the building's surroundings, first-floor area, and areas throughout the building, with perceptions delivered in a straightforward manner. The only recurring problem, the monitor reports, was confusion about the "purpose" of the building, which resolved itself into a "dual purpose" with separate but equal chains of command. Because of the session's length and a growing tendency toward AOL (analytic overlay, in the program's terminology — the intrusion of the viewer's own reasoning into reported perceptions), the session was ended before the essential elements of information concerning the "event," and the viewer's own search for the target location, could be done. The evaluation field on the report is blank in this copy.

The viewer's summary describes a wide, very long building, large — about the size of a city block — with streets only on the left side (two lanes) and the front (four lanes). Inside, the main features are narrow hallways with many offices and rooms off them, a feeling of blocked-off areas, and rooms within rooms, with ideas of administration and bureaucracy; the summary itself flags an analytic-overlay association of a polygraph operator with the rooms-within-rooms concept. On the first floor, a wide main hallway cuts across the building's width, lit by hanging lamps like chandeliers but not ornate, with an information or check-in desk at each end. Another first-floor room is described as sparsely decorated, with two tables at the back and people behind them and space in front for people to queue. The person in charge — the summary notes that the monitor supplied the cue "director," but that the figure's specific title does not include the word director — has a corner office on the top floor facing both streets, reached through an intervening staff office.

The summary reports that the building has a civil-military feel, with two separate but equal functions, each with its own chain of command. In response to the cue "information of interest to tasker," the viewer reported ideas of corruption, connection, interrelations, politics on a bureaucratic rather than geographical scale, interplay, exchange, tracking, and decisions, and the further idea that the duality of the organization would make matters difficult and confusing. The same cue also evoked an image of a middle-aged man with thinning blond hair combed almost straight back, a round face, blue eyes with crow's feet, and a light complexion.

The tasking sheet gives the encrypted coordinates as 428013/839461 and lists the required elements: describe the contents of the target structure; describe its key purpose; describe key activities supporting that purpose; describe the key event associated with the target, remaining open to the event being in past, present, or future time, or not occurring at all; and, through the viewer's own search technique, provide map coordinates for the target site, noting the method used and any perceptions gained.

## Errors and Anomalies

The handwritten transcript pages, which make up most of the thirteen pages, are only fragmentarily legible in OCR. They consist largely of stage notations, isolated words, coordinate-like number strings, and sketches, and long stretches reduce to character noise; no reliable continuous reading of them is possible from this text. OCR of the typed pages is also imperfect: the program name appears once as "SUN STREAK" and is rendered in the tasking header with variant spellings, "FEB" appears as "FER," and the classification markings are garbled in places. The session report carries two different session numbers in its OCR rendering ("oe" and "02" in different lines); the ledger card identifies the session as Session Number 02. The viewer's summary is dated "8 February 1989" in one OCR line and 3 February 1989 in the report header; the discrepancy is in the OCR layer and cannot be resolved from this copy. Most importantly, the record contains no feedback: neither the identity of the target structure nor any scoring of the viewer's statements against it appears anywhere in the item, and the report's evaluation section is empty.

## Limits

The full OCR text of this item (8,513 characters) was read, but the handwritten portions are legible only in fragments, as stated above. This assessment therefore rests on the typed session report, the viewer's typed summary, and the tasking sheet. Nothing in the item permits a judgment about whether the viewer's descriptions matched the actual target, and no such judgment is made here: the document records what the viewer reported, not whether any of it was accurate. Descriptions of the building, its personnel, and its supposed activities are the viewer's reported perceptions as recorded by the program, attributed to the viewer throughout this assessment, and are not endorsed as facts about any real building or person.
