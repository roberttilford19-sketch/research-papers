## Abstract

This staged assessment describes a collected Stanford Research Institute report, 'Information Transmission Under Condition of Sensory Shielding', by Russell Targ and Harold Puthoff of the Electronics and Bioengineering Laboratory, released in sanitised form under the reference CIA-RDP79-00999A000200010008-7. The report argues that some individuals appear able to obtain information about graphical material and remote scenes that are shielded against ordinary perception, and it sets out experiments designed to block visual, acoustic, and electrical paths of sensory input. Its best-known section reports drawing experiments with Uri Geller, including targets presented as pictures, as computer drawings on a display, stored only in computer memory, and displayed at zero intensity. A second line of work uses electroencephalography, testing whether a receiver isolated in a shielded room shows alpha-band changes when a distant sender is stimulated with flashing light. The report states its aim plainly: not merely to catalogue striking events, but to find patterns that could support ordinary scientific analysis. Appendices preserve session material, including a remote viewing transcript in which a subject describes people, vehicles, and movements at a distant location. This assessment reports the authors' designs, claims, and conclusions as theirs. The experiments remain historically contested, and nothing in this staging adopts the report's conclusion that a paranormal channel was demonstrated.

## Content Account

The introduction frames the research as an attempt to resolve, under conditions the authors considered unambiguous, whether a class of paranormal perception exists. The figure captions summarise the Geller drawing series and the electroencephalographic spectra. The electroencephalography section hypothesises that perception might occur at non-cognitive levels and be measurable even without a correct verbal response, and it describes the receiver seated in a visually opaque, acoustically and electrically shielded double-walled steel room about seven metres from the sender. The sampled closing pages reproduce a remote viewing transcript with timed interviewer questions and the subject's running description, ending with the party returning downstairs to compare notes. Authorship, laboratory affiliation, and the release reference are stated on the document's own title and stamp pages.
