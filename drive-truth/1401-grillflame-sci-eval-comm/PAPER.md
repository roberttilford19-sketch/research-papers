## Premise

The memorandum sets out the Grill Flame Working Group's response to the report of the Grill Flame Scientific Evaluation Committee, the independent review of United States government parapsychological work whose own findings are assessed under census 1153. Its purpose is twofold: to warn that the committee's report, in the Working Group's judgment, is too flawed to guide policy safely, and to place on record the Working Group's own restatement of the conclusions and recommendations it believed should guide the programme instead.

## Abstract

This two-page memorandum, written by Dr. J. Verona as chairman of the Grill Flame Working Group and recorded for the file, responds to the report of the Grill Flame Scientific Evaluation Committee, the document assessed separately as census 1153. The Working Group states its objection at the outset: the committee's report on psi investigations contains, in its judgment, serious factual errors, an inordinate number of ambiguities, and an incomplete and narrow focus, creating a genuine danger that it will provide arbitrary guidance to the policy makers and planners responsible for these investigations. Rather than argue the point in full, the memorandum offers a restatement of the conclusions and recommendations the Working Group wished to stand on the record. It records agreement with the committee that unexplained observations of human capabilities to generate data of potential intelligence utility had been documented, and that scientific acceptance of such capabilities had not occurred, while adding the possibility that the capabilities might be intrinsically non-scientific in character. Its recommendations favour expanded exploratory testing of intelligence utility, a central authority to manage and fund the work, and continued foreign intelligence analysis. It states strong disagreement with the committee's recommendation to terminate two Army investigative activities, and with a further recommendation affecting the SRI International researchers, which it says is not understood or acceptable without further explanation.

## Content Account

### The objection

The memorandum's first paragraph states that the Grill Flame Working Group formulated its comments on the substance and tone of the committee's report, and that the report contains serious factual errors, an inordinate number of ambiguities, is incomplete and narrowly focused, and therefore risks providing arbitrary guidance to policy makers and planners. It adds that the difficulties had been considered in detail by the Working Group and were available upon request, but that the first priority was to provide clear, consistent guidance to those not well acquainted with the area. These are the Working Group's characterizations of the committee's report, recorded here as its position; the committee's report itself, at census 1153, states its findings in its own terms, and the two documents disagree.

### Points of agreement

The memorandum lists the aspects of the committee's report that the Working Group strongly supports. First, that unexplained observations of human capabilities to generate data of potential utility to intelligence have been documented. Second, that scientific acceptance of the unexplained data-generation capabilities, or other claimed psi effects, has not occurred, because of inadequate investigations and their possible non-existence. To the second point the Working Group adds a note of its own: the possibility that the unexplained capabilities are intrinsically non-scientific, for example inherently statistical in character, or subject to intrinsic observer effects. The memorandum thus accepts the committee's core evidentiary caution while disputing its conclusions for the programme.

### The Working Group's recommendations

The memorandum's recommendations section argues that exploratory testing of the intelligence-related utility of these unexplained capabilities is justified and should be expanded beyond current programs; that, given the elementary state of knowledge, the likelihood of success cannot be predicted, but simple prudence dictates some such investigations, in an order of priority running from utility determination, to existence proofs if possible, and finally to characterization studies; that efficiency demands a central authority to manage and fund these exploratory investigations for the intelligence community and perhaps the entire Government; and that foreign intelligence analysis on claims of unexplained human capabilities should continue.

### Points of disagreement

The memorandum states that the Working Group strongly disagrees with certain viewpoints in the committee's report. It identifies the recommendation to terminate the Army Missile Intelligence Command's psychokinesis investigations and the Material Systems Analysis Activity's remote viewing investigations as resting on a misinterpretation of those programmes; the recognition of the scanned text is badly garbled at this passage, and the identification of the two activities follows the committee report's own account, at census 1153, of the activities whose termination it recommended. It further objects that, since the most successful current exploitation investigations used a data-generation technique derived in close conjunction with the SRI International researchers Puthoff and Targ, a recommendation affecting that work — the recognized text is again partly garbled here — is not understood or acceptable without further explanation.

### The closing offer

The memorandum closes by offering more in-depth and detailed comments on the committee's report, and by recording the Working Group's regret that many of the report's difficulties, especially of fact and incompleteness, could have been avoided if the report had been collectively reviewed in draft rather than allowing only individual inputs without review of the final record. It expresses the wish that the situation not recur in what it calls this first attempt at a comprehensive evaluation of an area of high potential importance to intelligence and other Government responsibilities.
