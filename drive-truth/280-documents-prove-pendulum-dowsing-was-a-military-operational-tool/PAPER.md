## Premise

The document sets out to show, from declassified records it cites by document number, that pendulum map dowsing was not merely experimented with but was used by named viewers on tasked intelligence work during the SUN STREAK era, and that it was recognised in reporting as an adjunct tool to remote viewing even though it never formed part of the core training manuals.

## Abstract

This short document argues that declassified Army and Defense Intelligence Agency records, preserved within CIA declassified files, show pendulum dowsing in operational use during the SUN STREAK era of 1987 to 1990. It cites four records by their CIA document numbers. The first concerns Project 8833, in which, the document says, viewers used a pendulum and maps to locate smuggling ships, and Viewer 095 was recorded as having done an “excellent job” dowsing a target ship carrying white powder. The second is cited for dowsing being used to find cocaine concealed between double partitions in a ship's hull before a physical search began. The third is cited for viewer 011 routinely using a pendulum and ruler to track missing persons and Israeli survivors. The fourth is cited for the military officially labelling the pendulum an “adjunct tool” to remote viewing. On that basis the author argues that dowsing's absence from the core remote viewing training manuals does not reflect its actual use, and he offers his own inference that the integration happened at operator level rather than by formal direction. He closes by noting that the same pendulum-and-map method remains available to anyone today. The documents cited are the author's evidence; they are not reproduced with this item.
