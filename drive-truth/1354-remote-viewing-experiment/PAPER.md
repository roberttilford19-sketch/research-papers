## Premise

The memorandum was produced to record, for the file, the outcome of a checkable test of information volunteered through remote viewing: statements made by Pat Price about a kidnapped officer were broken into individual items and scored against what the Air Force could verify while the officer was still a captive, so that the reliability of the method could be judged on a case where the facts could be established independently.

## Abstract

This one-page memorandum for the record, marked Secret and released under the identifier CIA-RDP79-00999A000400050004-2 as rendered in the OCR text, concerns Colonel Morgan, an American Air Force officer kidnapped in Beirut in early July 1975. The memorandum states that several days after the abduction Pat Price telephoned to volunteer information about the capture, that he was debriefed at length, and that his statement was passed to the Air Force for verification while Morgan was still held. To simplify verification, the report was divided into 187 items of information, each scored on a four-point scale of true, partly true, false, and impossible to determine. The memorandum reports that 128 items proved false, 9 partly true, 6 true, and 43 impossible to determine. On that basis it concludes that, as an experiment in what it calls out of body travel, the case must be considered a failure, and that the correlation was, if anything, negative. It adds two wider observations: that the case is another example of the unpredictability of information produced through remote viewing, and that it reinforced the writer's view of Price's candour, since Price could have drawn on press reporting, which the memorandum states was more accurate than his report, and did not. Those scores and conclusions are the memorandum's own, recorded here as its findings and not independently verified.

## Content Account

The memorandum is brief and entirely evaluative. It identifies the subject only as Colonel Morgan and gives no further identifying detail in the text as extracted. It describes the volunteered information as quite specific and detailed, covering the people involved, the procedures used, and the location of the group holding the officer. It explains the scoring method and reports the outcome in a single set of figures, then draws its conclusion directly from those figures. The memorandum is signed with the office designation OTS/BAB, the same office designation that appears on census item 1353, the memorandum reporting the April 1975 interview with Price. Read together, the two items show the same office first transmitting Price's material for evaluation and then recording a strongly negative evaluation of a later, independently checkable case.
