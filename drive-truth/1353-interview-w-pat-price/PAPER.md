## Premise

The memorandum was produced to transmit, to the office that had requested the work, the raw results of an experimental remote viewing exercise in which Patrick H. Price was given five grid coordinates and asked to describe the associated targets, so that the requesting office could evaluate the material itself and decide whether to debrief Price directly. A secondary purpose, stated in the memorandum, was to help the Agency evaluate the potential of the technique in general and of Price in particular.

## Abstract

This item is a Central Intelligence Agency memorandum, OTS/BAB Memo number 163-75, dated 29 April 1975, marked Confidential, reporting an interview with Patrick H. Price held on 26 April 1975 at the Key Bridge Marriott Motel. The covering memorandum states that the work arose from a request of 31 January 1975 that supplied five grid coordinates for use in an experimental remote viewing programme intended to provide operational leads on various targets. It states that Price's information was recorded essentially as presented, with only minor editorial changes, that little attempt was made to follow up most of his statements, and that the interviewer deliberately avoided learning the targets so as not to tip Price off during the debriefing, and therefore could not say how much of the material was correct. The attached memorandum for the record covers administrative points: that the relationship had become an operational one rather than research and development, that Price described the working conditions he preferred, including more locator information than bare grid coordinates and the value of photographs, and that compartmentation, need to know, and press security were discussed. The substantive descriptions Price gave for the coordinates are absent from this copy, because the following pages are marked exempt in the released document.

## Content Account

The memorandum for the record records that Price was told the relationship was now operational and was, in the interviewer's account, pleased and eager to work. On working method, it records Price's view that grid coordinates establish only a general area, that he needed a specific point to focus on, that photographs were very good particularly where a person or building was involved, and that locator information tied to a known landmark, city, or topographic feature helped him pinpoint a location. On security, it records that compartmentation and need to know would be standard procedure, that publicity could cause embarrassment, and that Price agreed to report other groups he might work with and his press contacts. It notes one interview he had given about six weeks earlier to the National Inquirer, described as a philosophical discussion of psychokinesis and remote viewing in relation to Scientology, and records his claim that he generally shunned publicity. It also records that he was given a personality checklist, an indirect assessment guide, and a further assessment form, and that he promised sketches of the areas discussed and a bill for his expenses. The report section states that the interview was taped, that three cassettes were available for study, and that Price had studied the coordinates given to him on 22 April. The coordinate values themselves appear in the released copy only under a redaction marking, and the pages containing his descriptions are marked as exempt.
