## Premise

The memorandum sets out to give the Assistant Director for Scientific Intelligence a current-status summary of the Central Intelligence Agency's unidentified flying objects project and of related Department of Defense activity, as requested verbally on 30 November 1953. It was produced as an internal status report, not as an investigation of any single sighting.

## Abstract

This declassified Central Intelligence Agency memorandum, dated 17 December 1953 and written by the Chief of the Physics and Electronics Division, summarizes the status of the Agency's unidentified flying objects project after responsibility passed to that division in May 1953. The memorandum states that the Agency's own effort was limited to maintaining awareness of other agencies' work, notably that of the United States Air Force, and to maintaining files. It describes Air Force activity in some detail, reporting that the Air Technical Intelligence Center's Project Blue Book was staffed by one officer, one airman, and a secretary within the Aerial Phenomena Section, that field investigations were no longer conducted by that section itself, and that reports were checked against meteorological, astronomical, aircraft, and balloon data and filed in a cross-referenced system. The memorandum reports that approximately ten percent of reported sightings during the preceding year had been tagged as unsolved. It also reports that the Air Technical Intelligence Center was transferring Project Blue Book to Headquarters, Air Defense Command, because that command had been doing most of the field work. The extracted text ends during that transfer discussion, so the memorandum's closing content is not available in this copy.

## Content Account

The memorandum opens by identifying its occasion: a verbal request of 30 November 1953 from an Executive staff officer for a resume of current unidentified flying objects activities. It then states that the Physics and Electronics Division assumed responsibility for the Office of Scientific Intelligence project on unidentified flying objects following a memorandum of 27 May 1953, and that the project had been confined to maintaining awareness of other agencies' activities and maintaining files.

The body of the memorandum, as extracted, is a status account of Air Force work rather than of Agency investigations. It reports that Air Force interest appeared to be decreasing, that headquarters interest was confined to cursory cognizance of the Air Technical Intelligence Center project, identified as Blue Book No. 10073, and that the project there was carried by Captain Charles A. Hardin, one airman, and a secretary. It reports that project personnel no longer conducted field investigations, which were instead requested from Air Force intelligence officers nearer the sightings, and that the section received and checked reports, requested further investigation where necessary, checked reports against meteorological, astronomical, aircraft, and balloon data, and recorded findings in a cross-referenced system organized by date, location, source, type of observation, and conclusion. It further reports that the section dealt directly with the Air Force public information office on information for public release, and that approximately ten percent of reported sightings in the preceding year had been tagged as unsolved. The final passage in the extraction reports the planned transfer of Project Blue Book to Headquarters, Air Defense Command, with a reason attributed to Lieutenant Colonel Harry Johnston.
