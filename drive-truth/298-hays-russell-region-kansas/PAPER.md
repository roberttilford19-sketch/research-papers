## Premise

The report sets out to consolidate, in one durable record, the geological structure of the Hays–Russell region of Kansas, the area's recent low-magnitude seismic observations, and the practical sensitivity of municipal infrastructure to such events. Its stated purpose is monitoring and awareness: to make sure no relevant structural or geophysical context is omitted from long-term asset-management thinking. It expressly disclaims being a hazard forecast or an attribution of causes.

## Abstract

This report assembles the regional geological context for the Hays–Russell area of Kansas and considers what that context means for infrastructure awareness. It places the area within the Central Kansas Uplift, the adjacent Salina Basin, and associated faulted Precambrian basement blocks, and it includes the Kanopolis Graben as a zone of crustal heterogeneity that provides a plausible setting for occasional low-magnitude basement fault reactivation, without claiming that recent events originate there. Regional gravity anomalies are discussed as evidence of structural complexity only, with an explicit clarification that they do not imply seismic amplification. The report then summarizes seismic observations for 2024–2025: magnitudes of approximately 2.0 to 3.8, depths of roughly five to nine kilometers consistent with basement sources, a modest increase in frequency with no escalation trend, and events that were locally felt without reported damage. Limited well-level variability in the Dakota Aquifer is noted for context only, with common alternative explanations listed and no causal link asserted. The infrastructure sections observe that even small events can reveal pre-existing weaknesses in older water and sewer systems and can warrant routine post-event observation of roads, bridges, and municipal structures, while expecting no structural damage at the observed magnitudes. The report closes with monitoring recommendations that require no changes to codes or emergency planning, and a final assessment that the activity is real, modest, and non-threatening.

2.0 INTEGRATED GEOLOGICAL CONTEXT
2.1 Central Kansas Structural Framework
The Hays–Russell area lies within a region characterized by:
The Central Kansas Uplift (CKU)
Adjacent Salina Basin
Associated faulted Precambrian basement blocks
These features are well documented by the Kansas Geological Survey and represent long-lived crustal structures capable of hosting minor intraplate seismicity under present-day regional stress.
2.2 Kanopolis Graben (Structural Context)
The Kanopolis Graben, a downdropped basement feature east of the CKU, is part of the broader Midcontinent structural fabric.
Relevance:
Represents a known zone of crustal heterogeneity
Provides a plausible structural setting for occasional low-magnitude basement fault reactivation
Does not imply elevated seismic hazard
No claim is made that recent earthquakes originate uniquely from this structure; it is included to complete the regional structural picture.
2.3 Gravity and Density Variations (Contextual, Not Causal)
Regional Bouguer gravity maps show negative anomalies across portions of Ellis and surrounding counties, consistent with:
Variations in crustal density
Basin-uplift transitions
Ancient tectonic features
Important clarification:
These anomalies indicate structural complexity, not seismic amplification or wave focusing. They are included solely to document subsurface heterogeneity relevant to geologic interpretation.
3.0 SEISMIC OBSERVATIONS (2024–2025)
Magnitude range: approximately M 2.0–3.8
Depths: typically ~5–9 km, consistent with basement sources
Frequency: modest increase, no escalation trend
Impact: locally felt, no damage reported
These events are consistent with background intraplate seismicity observed elsewhere in Kansas and the central United States.
4.0 HYDROGEOLOGIC OBSERVATIONS (CONTEXT ONLY)
Limited well-level variability has been reported in portions of the Dakota Aquifer system during the same period.

Such variability is common and may reflect pumping, recharge, barometric pressure, or seasonal effects

## Limits

The report's seismic figures are given as approximate ranges for 2024–2025 without an event list or catalog citation in the text, so individual events cannot be checked from the document alone; the framing attributes the figures to the report. The report names the Kansas Geological Survey and the United States Geological Survey as monitoring sources in general terms but cites no specific publication. Its geological descriptions are summary-level, as the report intends, and its recommendations are advisory. Nothing in the document claims an elevated hazard, and no independent geological review was performed in this staging pass.
