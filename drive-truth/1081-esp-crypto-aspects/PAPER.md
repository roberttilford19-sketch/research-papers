## Premise

The file is a sanitized copy of a National Security Agency introductory memorandum dated September 1970, titled “Research Into the Cryptologic Aspects of ESP.” The memorandum presents the first of a planned series of contributions on factors bearing on threats to communications security. Its concern is specific: psi phenomena, if real and usable, might let a person guess randomized key settings, recover the meanings of code groups or the plaintext of encrypted messages, or influence how keying material is used, to a hostile analyst's advantage. The memorandum sets out a program to survey psi research, identify the aspects most relevant to that threat, build a cryptologically oriented experiment, and develop training methods.

## Abstract

This September 1970 National Security Agency memorandum introduces a study of psi as a possible threat to communications security. It argues that the factor it calls psi is little understood and could considerably enhance a person's ability to guess the correct order of randomized key settings, the relationships between code groups and their meanings, or the underlying plaintext of an encrypted message, or to influence an individual's use of keying material to a hostile analyst's advantage. The study was designed in three parts: a survey of current psi investigations to find which aspects bear on guessing randomized cryptographic information above chance, which environmental characteristics enhance or retard the phenomena, and what sources, including Soviet bloc work, should be followed; the construction, under professional supervision, of a psi experiment using factors known to enhance performance, intended both to isolate talented subjects and to test them; and the development of training methods for cryptologic uses of extrasensory perception. The memorandum reports the steps accomplished on a time-available basis: the survey was made, telepathy and clairvoyance were tentatively identified as the factors most directly related to the threat, and an experiment resembling Dr. Rhine's methods but using NSA-generated random cryptographic material and a double-guess procedure was designed. It also reports a planned psychokinesis experiment on jamming low-voltage communications security equipment, and notes that real target tests were being programmed.

## Content account

The memorandum is headed "INTRODUCTORY MEMO SEP 70" and states that the paper, with its appendixes and inclosures, is the first of a series of contributions toward a more complete knowledge of special factors bearing on threats to COMSEC, the communications security discipline. It defines its object narrowly. The factor known as psi could, in the memorandum's words as OCR'd, enhance a human being's capability to guess the correct order of randomized key settings, the relationships between operations code groups and their meanings, or the underlying plaintext of an encrypted message, or to influence an individual's use of keying material to a hostile analyst's advantage.

The planned study had three elements, restated in the abstract: a survey of psi investigations organized around three questions, covering which aspects of the factor relate most closely to guessing randomized cryptographic information at better than chance expectancy on a continuing basis, which environmental characteristics enhance or retard the manifestations, and which sources of information are most useful for keeping abreast of developments, with special attention to Soviet bloc efforts; a supervised experiment built to use every known performance-enhancing factor and specifically related to guessing randomized cryptologic information, serving both to isolate subjects who appear more efficient than average and to test such subjects; and a method of training talented personnel in the cryptologic uses of ESP.

The accomplished steps are then listed. A survey had been made of work in the field, in inclosures and appendixes. The psi factors most directly related to the COMSEC threat had been tentatively identified as telepathy, defined as thought transference, and clairvoyance, defined as the ability to observe objects not available to normal sensory detection. With assistance from professional psi and psychological experts within the intelligence community, a cryptologically appropriate telepathy and clairvoyance experiment had been designed to select talented subjects and test their dependability. The memorandum notes the resemblance to Dr. Rhine's testing method and lists four differences: the material is cryptographically constructed using NSA random generation; the subject is forced to make a double guess on target material, yielding more performance characteristics for statistical and psychological analysis; the subject receives prior motivational conditioning; and the target material has realistic, lifelike characteristics that can be tailored to the subject's preferences.

The memorandum further reports that current sources of psi research had been located and were being used, noting Soviet bloc material in an inclosure and remarking that Soviet experiments were funded under the ministry of defense and were mainly of the telepathy communications variety, as the memorandum presents it. Real target tests were being programmed using individuals isolated by the research tool, with IBM cryptographic key cards among the materials to be tested, and preliminary tests are said to have indicated the exploration might be more meaningful than originally expected, a claim the memorandum does not document further in these pages. Finally, research into psychokinesis is reported to indicate that spontaneous episodes and controlled experiments have an electromagnetic effect, and an experiment was being designed to test the effectiveness of psychokinesis in jamming modern low-voltage electronic communications security equipment, built around persons with low frustration tolerance, on the memorandum's reasoning that such persons release pent-up force through the psychokinetic channel. The file ends mid-argument at that point.
