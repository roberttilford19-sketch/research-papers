## Premise

The press release was issued to publicize the Parapsychological Association's formal response to a National Research Council committee's assessment of parapsychology, commissioned by the United States Army, which had concluded that there was no scientific justification for the existence of parapsychological phenomena. Its purpose is advocacy: to charge that the committee's conclusion was reached through bias and suppression of evidence, and to direct attention to the Association's rebuttal report and to a panel discussion of it at the Association's 1988 annual convention.

## Abstract

This item is a 1988 press release of the Parapsychological Association, issued under the headline "National Research Council Suppresses Evidence in Assessment of Parapsychology," together with the closing pages of the Association's response letter signed by its president, Dr. Dean I. Radin. The National Research Council committee whose report is at issue had been commissioned by the United States Army and had concluded that there was "no scientific justification from research conducted over a period of 130 years for the existence of parapsychological phenomena." The Association's release makes three charges. It alleges that the two principal evaluators of parapsychology for the committee had a long-standing public commitment against the field, while scientists with more favourable opinions were excluded, contrary to the Council's stated policy of balance. It alleges that the report systematically omitted favourable findings, and that the committee's chairman telephoned the author of a favourable background paper and asked him to withdraw his positive conclusions. And it argues that the report is self-contradictory, since the committee could offer no plausible alternative explanations for much of the research it surveyed, yet still recommended that the Army continue to monitor parapsychological research in the United States and the Soviet Union. Every one of these charges is the Association's allegation, recorded here as such.

## Content Account

### The release and its setting

The release is headed "For Immediate Release" and names the Parapsychological Association's post office box in Research Triangle Park, North Carolina, with Dr. Dean Radin at Princeton University as contact. A note at its end describes the press kit of which the release formed part: the release itself, a letter to Dr. Frank Press, President of the National Academy of Sciences, and the Association's response to the National Research Council report. It also announces the Association's annual convention, held 17–20 August 1988 in Montreal, at which the authors of the Association's response were scheduled to discuss the Council's report in a panel. The Association identifies itself in the release as an affiliate of the American Association for the Advancement of Science.

### The three charges

The release states that the Association's response report, co-authored by Dr. John Palmer of the Institute for Parapsychology in Durham, North Carolina, Charles Honorton of the Psychophysical Research Laboratories in Princeton, New Jersey, and Dr. Jessica Utts of the University of California, Davis, also listed classes of psi effects the authors held to have been repeatedly demonstrated in laboratory studies, and included examples of claimed applications of psi phenomena of potential interest to national security.

The first charge is one of composition: that the two principal evaluators of parapsychology for the Council's committee had a long-standing, public commitment against parapsychology, while scientists with more favourable opinions were excluded from the committee, in what the Association calls direct violation of the Council's stated policy of assigning members to committees "with regard to appropriate balance."

The second charge is one of suppression: that the Council's report systematically omits findings favourable to parapsychology while quoting liberally from two background papers supporting the committee's position, and that the committee's chairman called one author of a favourable background paper and asked him to withdraw his positive conclusions — described in the release as an attempt to delete favourable passages in a paper the Council itself had commissioned.

The third charge is one of internal contradiction: that the committee concluded there was no evidence for parapsychological phenomena, yet by its own admission could offer no plausible alternatives to much of the research it surveyed, and nonetheless recommended that the Army, which the release states paid half a million dollars for the report, should continue to "monitor" parapsychological research being conducted at laboratories in the United States and in the Soviet Union.

### The letter excerpt

The third scanned page carries the closing portion of the Association's response letter. It quotes a passage of the Council's report, at pages 199–200 of that report as cited, using a "dirty test tubes" analogy: the suggestion that an experiment's results remain suspect if its procedures were not carried out to acceptable standards, even where the critic cannot demonstrate that the defect produced the outcome. The letter argues that this amounts to an admission that the committee could think of no scientific explanation for the selected studies it reviewed, and characterizes that admission, given the committee's stated bias, as a compelling endorsement of the existence of anomalous effects. It concludes that the Council's findings about parapsychological phenomena are wrong, and that anomalous effects that cannot be explained by prevailing scientific models continue to be demonstrated, repeatedly, in laboratory studies. These are the Association's arguments, in a letter signed by Dean I. Radin as its president, giving his address at the Department of Psychology, Green Hall, Princeton University.
