## Abstract

This technical proposal, dated 27 August 1992 and written by Edwin C. May and Wanda L. W. Luke of Science Applications International Corporation, responds to solicitation MDA908-92-R-0164. Its stated objective is to pursue the most promising basic and applied research into anomalous mental phenomena. The background section traces government-funded work from a Central Intelligence Agency effort begun in 1973, through application-oriented programmes at SRI International, to a coordinated Army programme begun in fiscal year 1986. The proposal reports that programme's conclusions as follows: an information transfer anomaly for anomalous cognition had been partially established, while the evidence for anomalous perturbation was insufficient, and carefully controlled tests of several physical systems had found no perturbation effect. It then summarises an eighteen-month Defence Intelligence Agency programme at the proposing company begun in February 1991, reporting a correlation between the quality of anomalous cognition data and the total change of Shannon entropy of a target, a finding that a sender need not view the target directly, and a mathematical flaw that left a magnetoencephalograph investigation unresolved. The proposed new work covers brain and skin measurements, personality profiling, several candidate physical theories, a replication of the entropy finding, an applications database, intelligence-like trials, oversight committees, and a rapid-response reserve.

## Content account

The proposal body runs to about twenty pages, followed by a long section of staff résumés. Its objective section divides anomalous mental phenomena into the two categories named in the premise, and its background section gives the history summarised in the abstract. Readers should note the asymmetry in that history as the proposal itself presents it. The existence claim is made, in qualified form, only for anomalous cognition. For anomalous perturbation, the proposal states that algae, single alpha particles, random number generators, and piezoelectric strain gauges were examined, some with experienced subjects, and that no evidence was observed in those carefully controlled experiments.

The recent-programme section reports three findings from the company's own earlier work. The first is the Shannon entropy correlation: the quality of anomalous cognition data correlated with a single physical property of the target, its total change of Shannon entropy. The proposal presents this as its most readily usable result and proposes to replicate it under improved conditions, with monitored sessions, immediate full-colour feedback, and matched static and dynamic target pools, using about five receivers contributing twenty trials each. The second finding is that an individual sender is not required to observe the intended target directly. The third concerns the magnetoencephalograph work, where the proposal candidly reports that a flaw in the mathematical analysis prevented a determination, and that reanalysis with better techniques was under way.

The approach section is organised by the solicitation's statement of work. Under biophysical measurements, the company proposes electroencephalograph work on whether the dominant alpha rhythm is affected by remote and isolated stimuli, reviewing a line of experiments from 1974 onward whose replications the proposal itself describes as inconclusive, with one replication confirming a decrease in alpha power and a second finding an increase. It also proposes work on the electrical properties of the skin, and personality work using the California Q-Set version of the Q-Sort method with about twenty-five people, building on a preliminary study of fourteen individuals in which three known talented receivers clustered together.

Under theoretical issues, the proposal lists candidate approaches and states its selection criterion plainly: approaches should be chosen for their likelihood of producing testable hypotheses. The candidates named include the Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen paradox, thermodynamic and Shannon entropy, general relativity including traversable wormholes, tachyons, and the physical interpretation of potentials, alongside a model called Decision Augmentation Theory. Under applied research, the proposal offers an online database of physical, psychological, and environmental variables for each trial, improved quantitative assessment of free-response data, up to five intelligence-like trials with sponsor-supplied targets and complete ground truth, and participation in intelligence applications at the sponsor's request. It describes three standing oversight bodies, a Scientific Oversight Committee, an Institutional Review Board, and a Policy Oversight Committee, and it reserves about five percent of the effort for quick-reaction tasks such as briefings and unanticipated experiments. A glossary, a reference list of thirty-six items, and the résumés close the document.
