## Premise

The bibliography was produced to catalogue, in one unclassified list, the reports generated by SRI International's psychoenergetics work for government sponsors from the late 1970s through 1989. Each entry gives a classification marking, a title, authors where known, a date, and, for most entries, a one- or two-sentence description of what the report contains, so that a reader can see the shape of the whole program — remote perturbation, remote viewing training and evaluation, target search, screening, and performance investigation — without opening any single report.

## Abstract

This item is an unclassified bibliography of Stanford Research Institute International publications in psychoenergetics, running from 1978 to 1989, preserved in a sanitized declassified copy. It is arranged as tables giving, for each report, a classification marking, a title, authors, a date, and usually a short description of the contents. The entries cover proposed Grill Flame protocols on remote perturbation techniques; the multi-year Special Orientation Techniques and DoD Grill Flame progress reports; electronic system perturbation experiments; remote viewing reliability, enhancement, and evaluation; database management for remote viewing data; coordinate remote viewing training; target search techniques, also called dowsing; personnel screening by the Personality Assessment System; geophysical effects studies; computer-assisted search; fuzzy-set and automated evaluation methods; photon production and neuromagnetic replications; meta-analysis of forced-choice precognition experiments published between 1935 and 1987; and the Enhanced Human Performance Investigation reports of 1986 to 1988. Two closing lists cover unclassified documents and sanitized documents whose originally classified versions were not found. The bibliography's largest single summary entry describes a March 1989 review of SRI psychoenergetic research from 1973 to 1988, whose database comprised 117 documents and over 25,000 trials. The bibliography itself reports no experimental results beyond the brief contents notes attached to each entry.

## Content Account

The bibliography is organized as a sequence of tables under the headings "Class," "Title," and "Author," with most entries followed by a short unclassified description. Its first entries are Grill Flame era proposals and program reports: two versions, dated 8 May 1979 and 15 November 1979, of a proposed SRI protocol for research on remote perturbation techniques, the second describing a procedure and an experiment for remote perturbation, defined as perturbation of an event outcome by mental processes alone; a January 1980 quarterly progress report on NIC techniques by Puthoff, Swann, and Langford; a proposed three-year joint services psychoenergetics program dated 30 June 1980; interim and final reports on Special Orientation Techniques, a one-year program for optimizing the remote viewing capabilities of client-selected individuals; and a September 1980 report on electronic system perturbation techniques, described as considering the possible remote production of physical effects on electronic equipment and as describing 214 separate experiments, some of which show evidence for an anomalous perturbation.

The fiscal-year program reports follow: a data-base management system proposal for operational remote viewing data; a series of DoD Grill Flame progress reports and a final report covering a three-year evaluation program from fiscal 1981 to 1983, described as assessing the potential of remote viewing for intelligence applications; a fiscal 1984 end-of-year report continuing training activities with emphasis on authentication and verification; a January 1982 countermeasures survey, from a literature search on whether countermeasures exist against psychoenergetic intrusions; and the remote viewing reliability, enhancement, and evaluation reports of 1982 to 1984, described as a three-year effort to develop techniques to enhance the accuracy and reliability of remote viewing, apply it to operational tasks, and integrate it into the overall intelligence mix.

A large middle group concerns method. It includes a database management feasibility proposal; a March 1982 report on remote viewing detection techniques applied to antisubmarine warfare, addressing remote viewing as a location aid; an April 1982 investigation of audiolinguistic correlations with the quality of remote viewing sessions, testing whether audio analysis could separate correct from incorrect statements; a May 1982 targeting requirements task; a remote viewing evaluation protocol, finalized in 1983, developing a procedure to assess the relative quality of a set of remote viewing responses; a draft report on coordinate remote viewing technology covering the 1981 to 1983 training program; target search techniques, described in a December 1984 final entry as a psychoenergetic search technique, also called dowsing in Western literature, for determining the location of objects, individuals, and facilities; a November 1983 survey of free world psychoenergetics research covering 233 articles from the open literature; a December 1984 personnel identification and selection effort built on the Personality Assessment System; interim and final geophysical effects studies of 1984, investigating possible effects of ambient geophysical and low-frequency electromagnetic factors on remote viewing performance; database management facility and upgrade reports; a quick reaction tasking entry; Track I training research, evaluating a training procedure that targets sites by geographical coordinates; an automated remote viewing evaluation procedure of May 1985, modifying a Princeton-developed procedure and developing a figure of merit; and a May 1985 investigation of photon production claims, examining psychokinetic effects during remote viewing and their application to intrusion detection.

The later entries, from 1986 to 1989, include work on locating target material in space and time by target search; a suggested remote viewing training procedure of December 1986 developing two new concepts for a testable training method; remote viewing evaluation techniques defining a figure of merit based on accuracy and reliability; computer-assisted search experiments, testing the ability to locate lost or hidden items by dowsing; feedback and precognition experiments; forced-choice remote viewing experiments covering fiscal 1986 to 1988; applications of fuzzy set mathematics to remote viewing analysis; neurological correlates experiments of December 1988; a summary of government-sponsored research in psychoenergetics from 1972 through 1988; a prototype analysis system for special remote viewing tasks; and application-oriented remote viewing experiments of April 1988 and April 1989, described as demonstrating the potential of the technique against a directed energy weapon development. The largest summary entry is the March 1989 review of the psychoenergetic research conducted at SRI International from 1973 to 1988, by May, Utts, and others, described as a review and analysis whose database comprises 117 documents and a total of over 25,000 trials under a variety of protocols.

Two entries concern the Enhanced Human Performance Investigation, described elsewhere in their entries as a five-year program, fiscal 1986 to 1990, to provide research and development in psychoenergetics as a means of enhancing human performance for military applications, with objectives to document that psychoenergetic phenomena are real and reproducible, to determine the mechanisms underlying them, and to bring the field into the mainstream of human performance research. One of those entries, dated December 1987 in the main list, is a fiscal 1987 final report; the closing sanitized list also carries a December 1986 entry of the same title, described as a progress report of related work done at SRI during fiscal 1986. The relation of that December 1986 entry to census item 1184, which is itself an SRI final technical report of that title covering fiscal 1986 work, is recorded in the audit log as an observed relation only.

The bibliography closes with two lists. The first lists unclassified documents: a May 1978 proposal on suggested approaches to psychoenergetic research; a November 1978 description of the standard remote-viewing protocol for local targets; a hardware construction report on a computer-based random number generator; a December 1987 report on fuzzy set applications; a December 1988 meta-analysis of forced-choice precognition experiments published in English-language parapsychology journals between 1935 and 1987; an October 1989 report on a three-year effort to improve remote viewing quality using hypnosis; a December 1989 screening report covering 256 candidates for remote viewing talent; and a December 1989 observation of neuromagnetic fields in response to remote stimuli. The second list covers sanitized documents whose originally classified versions were not found, including a draft on special orientation techniques of April 1980, a feasibility study on using remote viewing detection techniques to locate targets, a May 1985 bacterial mutation study described as a conceptual replication of reported work on psychoenergetic interactions with biological systems, a November 1985 white paper on psi communications experiments, a December 1986 experiment on possible anomalistic behavior of a photon detection system during a remote viewing test, the Personality Assessment System screening report of December 1986, the December 1986 Enhanced Human Performance Investigation progress report, a December 1988 report on the effects of hypnosis on remote viewing quality, and a December 1987 attempt to verify a Chinese claim that light is emitted near a correctly identified remote viewing target.

## Errors and Anomalies

OCR noise is moderate and concentrated in the table furniture, classification markings, and authors' names; entries are described here only where title, date, and description are legible, and author names are given only in the few cases where they are both legible and material. One entry's description states that its experiments extend work "reported in December 1994," a date inconsistent with the entry's own December 1986 date and with the bibliography's range; the reading is flagged as an anomaly in the source text and is not corrected here, and no claim rests on it. Several classification markings are rendered inconsistently by the OCR (for example S/NF and S/NFE variants), so this assessment refers to markings only in general terms. The Drive filename, "remote prebutation," does not describe this document; the only remote perturbation material in it is the pair of 1979 protocol entries.

## Limits

The full extracted text was read: 20,008 characters covering all thirteen pages of the bibliography as extracted, whose internal page numbers run to 14. The bibliography is a catalogue, not the underlying research: none of the reports it lists was read at this desk for this item, and the one- or two-sentence descriptions are the bibliography's own summaries of those reports, recorded here as such. Dates, author attributions, and trial counts quoted from entries are the entries' statements. No entry was checked against the report it describes. Nothing in this staged copy reproduces the full text of the bibliography.
