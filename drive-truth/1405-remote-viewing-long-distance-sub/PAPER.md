## Premise

The report sets out to assess whether remote viewing and related psychoenergetic processes, studied at SRI International under government sponsorship, could constitute a technique of intelligence interest, either as a capability to develop or as a threat to evaluate. It presents the work of one contract period as evidence bearing on that assessment.

## Abstract

This document is the October 1978 final report of SRI Project 5309, titled Advanced Threat Technique Assessment, prepared by Harold E. Puthoff, Russell Targ, Edwin C. May, and Ingo Swann for the period from April 1977 to April 1978. The report defines remote viewing as the reported ability of a person to describe a distant location or object without ordinary sensory access, and it organizes the contract period's work around that definition. Its contents list long-distance coordinate remote-viewing experiments directed at sites in Eastern Europe and the Soviet Union and at Headquarters, Strategic Air Command; an experiment in communication with a submerged submersible near Santa Catalina Island; studies of objects hidden in metal containers; experiments on the role of feedback; work on remote perception of audio signals; and tests for subject-induced equipment perturbation using strain gauges and random-event generators. The portion of the text available to this desk ends during the feedback chapter, at a follow-on experiment in which subjects attempted to identify digits chosen by a computer random number generator, with feedback given on a random half of the trials. The report's summary, conclusions, and appendices, listed in its contents at pages 69 onward, fall outside the portion read here and are not characterized in this assessment. All experimental claims in the document are the authors' claims, reported here as claims and not adopted as findings of this desk.

## Content Account

The report opens with an objective and an executive summary that define psychoenergetic processes and summarize the SRI work, including three worked examples: a remote viewing of a redacted site, communication with a submerged submarine through the remote-viewing channel, and resolution studies using objects hidden in metal containers. The executive summary also offers the authors' account of the state of the art in remote viewing at the end of the contract period.

The third chapter reports long-distance coordinate remote-viewing experiments. It describes trials directed at an East European site, at sites in the Soviet Union, at Headquarters, Strategic Air Command at Offutt Air Force Base, and at underground nuclear test detection. Subject sketches are compared in the report with overhead photography and site diagrams.

The fourth chapter reports long-distance remote viewing from a submersible. The text names the vessel as the Taurus, a five-man underwater vehicle manufactured by International Hydrodynamics Company Ltd. of Canada, operating submerged near Santa Catalina Island off southern California. The protocol paired six prepared messages with six San Francisco Bay Area target locations. A demarcation team visited the location linked to a chosen message for fifteen minutes while a subject aboard the submersible, monitored by an investigator blind to the target pool, recorded impressions of where the team was. The subject then consulted the list of potential targets.

The fifth chapter reports remote viewing of objects, pictures, and symbols, including objects in metal containers, abstract targeting of building interiors, and analytical targets such as alphanumeric and color-and-shape targets. The sixth chapter, the last portion available here, examines the role of feedback. The authors conclude within that chapter that feedback appeared important to their subjects' performance, while noting that psychological factors were not separated from any substantive effect. The available text ends during a follow-on digit-guessing experiment designed to test performance when feedback was withheld on a random half of the trials.
