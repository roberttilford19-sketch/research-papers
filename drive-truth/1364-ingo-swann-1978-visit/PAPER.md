## Abstract

This staged assessment describes a collected United States Army memorandum for record, dated 30 October 1978, reporting the visit of Ingo Swann to AMSAA on 17 October 1978. Swann attended at the request of the installation's Grill Flame participants and, by prior agreement, took part in no experiment; the day was spent in discussion, and the memorandum, signed by Lynne S. Taylor, summarises the major points. Several of those points concern timing and preparation. Swann's concept of rise time holds that a subject prepared for a session at an exact hour loses effectiveness rapidly if the start is delayed. The memorandum calls the interviewer and subject relationship the most important aspect of remote viewing, and it details interviewer discipline: avoiding negative or cueing words, asking questions that contain no object, and distinguishing a subject's basic perceptions of form and light from later interpretation, which the memorandum groups under analytic, memory, and visual overlay. Other points cover quiet time before a session, prompt feedback, disagreement with Stanford Research Institute over how much an outbound experimenter influences a session, and the importance of a comfortable, dedicated, undisturbed room. The document is a compact primary record of how practitioners inside a government programme understood their own method in 1978. This assessment reports its contents as the participants' stated views, not as validated findings.
