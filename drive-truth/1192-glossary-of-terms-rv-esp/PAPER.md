## Premise

The glossary was produced to fix the working vocabulary of the STAR GATE remote-viewing program. By defining its terms in one place — the roles of the people involved, the kinds of projects, the paperwork that moves between them, and the phenomena the program claimed to study — it gave personnel, project officers, and intelligence-community customers a shared language in which taskings, sessions, and evaluations could be written and compared.

## Abstract

This four-page glossary defines the terms of the STAR GATE remote-viewing program, alphabetically from "Beacon Remote Viewing" to "Viewer Identification Number." Its definitions are of two kinds. The first is administrative: a "Customer" is an organizational entity within the Intelligence Community desiring information from remote viewers; a "Project Officer" selects targets, assigns project numbers, tasks viewers by Tasking Sheet, and afterward provides feedback and evaluation; a "Monitor" attends a session and records the data; a "Report" is the viewer's summary, carrying the project number, viewer identification or source number, and tasking and session dates. Projects are classed as Operational, to satisfy customer requests; Proficiency Enhancement, an in-house program measured against the target; and Training, for new personnel and simpler than the other two. Viewers on operational projects carry a constant Source Number and on proficiency projects a Viewer Identification Number, each in place of a name. The second kind states the subject matter in the program's own terms: "Remote Viewing" is the acquisition and description, by mental means, of information blocked from ordinary perception by distance, shield, or time, a term coined, the glossary says, by Stanford Research Institute in the early 1970s; "Psychoenergetics" is a mental process by which an individual perceives, communicates with, and/or perturbs characteristics of a target remote in space and/or time; "Extra Sensory Perception" and "Psychokinesis" are perceptions and physical actions, respectively, that cannot be explained by known means. Each entry carries its original classification marking, from unclassified to SECRET/NOFORN/STAR GATE/LIMDIS.

## Content Account

Read as a whole, the glossary is the program's operating system in miniature. Its administrative definitions describe a complete intelligence-production cycle: a customer states a requirement, a project officer converts it into a numbered tasking, a viewer works a session (alone, as a "Solo," or with a monitor), the viewer produces a report with sketches attached, and an evaluation is written — by the project officer for proficiency work, by the customer for operational work. Feedback flows back to the viewer under stated security conditions, and the glossary is careful to note that operational feedback may not be immediate, because the customer may first have to research or act on the information.

The phenomenon definitions are stated as institutional fact rather than as hypotheses. Remote viewing, psychoenergetics, extrasensory perception, and psychokinesis are defined by what they purport to do, and the conditions that discipline them — "Double Blind," in which the people in session with a viewer have no knowledge whatsoever of the target, and the "Target Pool," a set of targets developed for their distinction and diversity — are defined alongside the claims they were meant to protect. A reader should therefore treat the glossary as evidence of how the program described itself and organized its work, not as evidence that the defined phenomena occur. The glossary makes no argument and presents no data; it legislates vocabulary.

Two features deserve note. The sequence of entries is alphabetical but imperfect: a letter Y entry is absent from the extracted text, and one entry ("Proficiency Enhancement Project") appears nested under the letter J position in the sequence, which may reflect the original layout or an extraction artifact. And the document's handling markings — reproduction requiring originator approval, dissemination only as directed by DIA/PAG or higher authority, not releasable to foreign nationals — show that even the program's dictionary was treated as controlled material in its working life.
