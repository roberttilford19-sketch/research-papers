## Premise

The report was produced to give the sponsoring agency a full final account of a twelve-month study of "remote viewing," the term its authors use for the claimed ability of individuals to describe remote geographical or technical targets by mental imagery alone, with no sensory access to them. It sets out to document both the applied work, in which the ability was tested against sponsor-designated targets including one operational target, and the basic research intended to screen for gifted subjects, identify their measurable characteristics, and look for neurophysiological correlates and possible physical mechanisms.

## Abstract

This document is the final research report of Stanford Research Institute Project 3183, "Perceptual Augmentation Techniques," by Harold E. Puthoff and Russell Targ, dated 1 December 1975 and covering January 1974 through February 1975, in the copy released in 2000 under a different CREST number from the copy held at census item 1176. Its contents list long-distance remote-viewing experiments on sponsor-designated targets, including a West Virginia site, a Urals site, and an operational target at Semipalatinsk in the USSR, an SRI-designated Costa Rica series, a short-distance test using an abacus as an analogue for a cipher machine, a technology series, and an experiment on detecting secret writing. Its basic-research contents list screening tests of natural targets under a standard protocol, a four-state electronic random number generator study, medical, psychological, and neuropsychological evaluation, electroencephalographic work, and instrument experiments with a Geiger counter, a torsion pendulum, and a superconducting magnetometer. The text available here ends within the screening summary, where a series by two sponsor staff members is reported at p = 0.017 by blind rank ordering. All results named are the authors' own reported results in a contractor's final report, recorded here as the document's claims and not as findings verified by this desk.

## Content Account

Only the opening and closing portions of the available text were read for this assessment, and the account below is confined to them and to the report's own contents list. The title and approval pages identify the same report as census item 1176: the same title, the same two authors, the same laboratory, the same project number, the same report date of 1 December 1975, and the same coverage period of January 1974 through February 1975. The cover page of this copy states that the document consists of 153 pages, while the PDF from which the text was taken has 154 pages.

The contents list is the fullest structural evidence available in the pages read. The applied research effort covers long-distance remote viewing of sponsor-designated targets as exploratory research, comprising a West Virginia site described by two subjects, a Urals site, and a summary; a separate section on a sponsor-designated operational target, Semipalatinsk in the USSR; long-distance remote viewing of SRI-designated targets in the Costa Rica series; short-distance remote viewing in a cipher machine analogue experiment and a technology series; and detection of secret-writing target material. The basic research effort covers screening tests, namely remote viewing of natural targets under standard protocol conditions for experienced subjects, learner controls, and sponsor subjects, with a summary, and a four-state electronic random number generator study; identification of measurable characteristics of gifted subjects through medical, psychological, and neuropsychological evaluation; identification of neurophysiological correlates, including a remote strobe experiment and physiological monitoring during remote viewing; and identification of the nature of the phenomena, including experiments with a Geiger counter, a laser-monitored torsion pendulum, and a superconducting differential magnetometer, discussions of perturbation effects and possible mechanisms, a communication-theory approach to channel utilization, a section on Soviet efforts, and conclusions. A program summary, references, and three appendices follow.

The closing portion of the available text falls inside the screening summary. It describes two sponsor staff members who participated as subjects, one in a three-experiment series and one in two experiments, the latter beginning one narrative with a red A-frame building and a large yellow object and going on to describe a lock as laminated steel, both elements marked correct in the report with reference to a figure. For the series of five, the report states that blind rank ordering of the transcripts at each site was significant at p = 0.017, including three direct hits and one second rank for the target-associated transcripts. The summary then states that the descriptions, although containing inaccuracies, were sufficiently accurate to permit the judges to differentiate among the various targets to the degree tabulated in the report's summary table.

Those statements are the report's own reported results, presented by its authors in a final report to their sponsor. This desk has not verified them, and the portions of the report that would describe the operational target and the later basic-research sections lie beyond the text read here, as the Limits section states.
