## Abstract

This document is a Defense Intelligence Agency question-and-answer briefing paper on Project Grill Flame, dated 20 August 1981. It contains eight numbered questions, each framed by the same statement of the program's purpose, with the agency's answers. The first answer describes Grill Flame as a planned three-year joint program between the Defense Intelligence Agency and the Army, then in its first year, fiscal year 1981, investigating paranormal phenomena such as remote viewing and psychokinesis judged to have potential military applications. That year's emphasis, it states, was on evaluating the application potential and limitations of remote viewing, defined as the ability of individuals to mentally collect data at a distance, and on evaluating foreign work as a possible threat to the United States. The second answer states that the agency knew of no present CIA program of a similar kind, while keeping the CIA informed. The fifth answer gives fiscal year 1982 funding as approximately 140,000 dollars from the Army and 335,000 dollars from the Defense Intelligence Agency. The eighth answer addresses the Army INSCOM program, stating that INSCOM ran an in-house effort directed against real intelligence targets, coordinated under a joint memorandum of understanding signed in March 1981, with funding of 130,000 dollars in fiscal year 1981 and 200,000 dollars in fiscal year 1982. The document is an administrative record of what the agency said about the program in 1981, reported here as the agency's statements, not as findings of this desk.

## Content Account

The document consists of eight question-and-answer pairs on a repeated template. The questions, as preserved in the recognized text, ask for a description of the project and the phenomena under study; whether the CIA was conducting similar studies and what interface existed; the level of funding requested for fiscal year 1982; how the work was organized; and whether the Army INSCOM study of paranormal phenomena was separate from Grill Flame and what its total funding was.

The first answer, quoted in substance in the abstract, defines the program's scope and its first-year emphasis. Handwritten annotations appear in the recognized text beside this answer, but they are rendered as fragmented characters and cannot be read reliably from this copy.

The second answer states that although the CIA could best answer for itself, the Defense Intelligence Agency was not aware of any present program at that agency, and that the agency maintained its interface by keeping the CIA informed of progress in the Grill Flame effort.

The fifth answer gives the fiscal year 1982 funding plan: approximately 140,000 dollars from the Army and 335,000 dollars from the Defense Intelligence Agency.

The eighth answer, the most detailed in the portion preserved, states that INSCOM had an in-house Grill Flame program, described as an experimental effort directed against real intelligence targets and problems. It states that INSCOM's efforts were coordinated closely with the Defense Intelligence Agency under a joint Grill Flame memorandum of understanding signed by the Deputy Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency and the Assistant Chief of Staff for Intelligence, Department of the Army, in March 1981. It states that INSCOM's Grill Flame contracts were included in the overall Defense Intelligence Agency contract with Stanford Research Institute International, and it gives INSCOM's total Grill Flame funding as 130,000 dollars for fiscal year 1981 and 200,000 dollars for fiscal year 1982. The answers to the remaining questions are present in the document but are partly obscured in the recognized text by the handwritten annotations described below, and they are not characterized beyond the questions they answer.
