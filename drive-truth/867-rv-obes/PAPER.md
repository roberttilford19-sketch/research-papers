Remote viewing (RV) and out-of-body experiences (OBEs) are interrelated manifestations of non-local consciousness.

1. Shared Mechanism of Non-Local Perception 
   Both phenomena access information beyond sensory or spatial limitations. RV achieves this via a "synthetic copy" of consciousness projected to a target, while OBEs involve direct consciousness projection via the crown chakra. Critically, both operate outside spacetime constraints, accessing historical events or distant locations through universal records (e.g., akashic records) .  

2. Identical Altered States of Consciousness  
   RV and OBEs require suppression of analytical cognition through:  
-Theta/alpha brainwave dominance 
-Sensory gating reduction (diminished default mode network activity)   
   - Induction via identical protocols: meditation, hypnosis, or coordinate focusing (e.g., Swann's CRV method for RV ↔ Yoga Nidra/Tressoldi's hypnosis for OBEs) .  
3. Neurocognitive and Phenomenological Overlap  
   - RV sessions often spontaneously trigger OBE-like sensations (e.g., "sensing through an extended body" – Ingo Swann)  
 - Military viewers like Joseph McMoneagle reported OBEs in 65% of operational RV tasks .  
   - A 2018 study found 14% identical data between RV and OBE-RV sessions, confirming perceptual crossover .  
4. Historical and Cultural Integration 
   Ancient practices treated them as complementary:  
   - Egyptian/Greek oracles used OBE-like states for strategic remote perception .  
   Tibetan dream yogis directed OBEs for intelligence gathering, mirroring RV's applied use .  
5. Unified Theoretical Frameworks 
   Models like the "Spiritual Eye" hypothesis (US20060072226A1 patent) posit RV and OBEs as two modes of the same faculty:  
   - RV: Receptive tuning of consciousness modules  
   - OBE: Active projection of consciousness modules . 
RV and OBEs represent complementary expressions of a singular capacity: consciousness unbounded by biological and spatiotemporal limits. Their separation into distinct categories is largely artificial, stemming from methodological preferences rather than ontological differences.
