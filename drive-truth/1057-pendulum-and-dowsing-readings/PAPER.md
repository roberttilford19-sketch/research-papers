## Abstract

This report, dated 24 April 1984, is Joseph R. Jochmans' second report to the Fetzer Foundation on site selection for its planned Kalamazoo World Center. Jochmans reports that pendulum and dowsing readings favor the "590 site" across from the Fetzer radio and television station over other Kalamazoo locations, and he sets out two guiding principles: the center should gather, communicate, and fund research while doing no clinical work, and it should be as self-sufficient as possible. He records that stones from Saqqara and Giza were placed at energy-line crossing points on the property on 16 April 1984, with thirteen Arkansas quartz crystals to follow, and he recommends clearing dumped concrete, fencing the property, and acquiring adjoining facilities. Tarot readings assign the High Priestess, the Lovers, and Temperance to the site's spiritual, educational, and administrative centers. The report also assesses the Clovelly property as a retreat site and two alternate locations, recommending acquisition of all 56 acres of the Ray Anderson estate at Dustin Lake. It insists that any research clinic be located in the Bahamas, and it closes with a six-part plan for a research communications network, including a journal, conferences, an expanded library, and computerized cross-referencing.

## Content Account

The report opens with its conclusion. After considering other sites around Kalamazoo, Jochmans reports, both dowsing and pendulum readings indicate that the 590 site, across from the Fetzer radio and television station, is still by far the best, in his words energy-wise and otherwise. Two guiding principles follow. The Kalamazoo center must devote itself exclusively to gathering research information, communicating and forming a research network, and funding research, while doing no clinical application work whatsoever; clinical work must be done elsewhere, out of the country, preferably in the Bahamas. And the center must become as self-sufficient as possible in all its aspects, including communications and energy needs.

He then records work already done on the property and work proposed. On 16 April 1984, stones from Saqqara and Giza in Egypt were placed at specific energy-line crossing points on the 590 property, which he states helped to raise the overall energy levels and create new energy vortices. His next steps are to acquire thirteen quartz crystals in Arkansas for placement along the property's energy lines, especially at their entrances and exits, with a target of May; to clean up the dumped garbage on the property, above all the piles of concrete blocks he describes as weighing on the spirit and energy of the place; to protect the property against future dumping, possibly by a fence; and, when possible, to acquire the AAA facilities at the property's southern end so that the whole property would be complete.

Tarot readings for the three major sites on the property are given with their attributes. The Spiritual Center drew the High Priestess, glossed as Isis, the Universal Mother, with secret wisdom to be revealed, balance, fertility, emotions under control, and the beginnings of initiation. The Educational and Conference Center drew the Lovers, glossed with left and right brain balance, the Tree of Life and the Tree of Immortality, universal service, and logic guided by intuition. The Administration Building drew Temperance, spelled "Temperence" in the report, glossed with the fire of the heart, gold in the third eye, the mixing of the waters of inner alchemy, and the beginning of the path toward enlightenment.

The report then turns to other properties. Preliminary dowsing of the Clovelly property, across from the Fetzer home, indicates to him an ideal secluded retreat area: the Fetzer home would become a guest house, small cottages or cabins could be built for visiting researchers, and a small meditation chapel could stand on the energy-line crossing point on the property's southern side; he lists rezoning and map and field dowsing to stake out the buildings as next steps, and notes two energy lines running through the property with indications of a third to enter in the near future. Of two alternate sites for the center, the Leithauser property, at D Street and Route 131, has a high concentration of energy patterns but sits near the Kalamazoo Speedway, a real problem of noise pollution. His first choice among alternates is the Ray Anderson estate in Oshtemo, near KL Avenue and Fourth Street, with shoreline on Dustin Lake: dowsing readings show potentially healing energy properties, pendulum readings indicate that all fifty-six acres should be acquired as soon as possible, and an addendum reports a more recent reading suggesting the property will become a minor node point after the next shift in global energy patterns. He is candid that the property's eventual purpose cannot yet be clearly defined, while insisting that acquiring the land now is vital nonetheless.

The report's remaining positions are stated firmly. The pendulum readings are, in his word, adamant that the Research Clinic Center be located in the Bahamas, with specific focus on the Bimini Islands or possibly the purchase of an entire island; he plans a special report on the Cayce readings regarding Bimini as an Atlantean and future healing site, followed by contact with Bahamian government officials about acquiring land. The major emphasis for the foundation at this time, he argues, should be a research communications network of six parts: a professionally edited bimonthly or quarterly journal as an open forum for research ideas; conferences, seminars, and workshops at Fetzer Hall; a greatly expanded library of healing materials, organized and catalogued for visiting researchers; computer processing and cross-referencing of the information so gathered; expanded publication and sponsorship of research books, developing the foundation's own printing and bindery facilities; and expanded efforts to find and fund research individuals and groups, for which he is preparing a listing to be ready by June. Every recommendation in this account is Jochmans' own, resting on the readings he reports; the readings' validity is his claim, and this assessment adopts none of it.
