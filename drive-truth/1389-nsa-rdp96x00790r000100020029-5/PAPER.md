## Abstract

This staged assessment describes a collected United States Air Force scientific report, 'Communication by Electroencephalography', by E. M. Dewan, issued in November 1964 as Special Report No. 12 of the Air Force Cambridge Research Laboratories, report number AFCRL-64-910, and released under the CREST reference NSA-RDP96X00790R000100020029-5. The report asks whether the electrical activity of the brain can itself carry deliberate messages. Its method rests on the contrast between alpha rhythm and alpha blocking: under the conditions described, a subject can learn to turn the alpha state on or off, and apparatus including an integrating threshold switch and filtering can register which state is present with, the report says, no ambiguity under ideal experimental conditions. A sequence of such states forms a series of information bits. The report then considers extensions: driving an electromechanical typewriter or a modified teletype, controlling devices that manipulate objects in the environment such as a crude grasping instrument, increasing output by using the two cerebral hemispheres independently, and the possibility that a person could learn to modulate alpha frequency itself, supported by work on conditioning brain wave activity through conditioned responses. Its stated conclusion is measured: under certain circumstances, a person can manipulate electroencephalographic activity in a way that permits communication without motor activity.
