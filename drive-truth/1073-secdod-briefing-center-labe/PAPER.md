## Abstract

This briefing script presents the United States Army Intelligence and Security Command program known as Center Lane, a special access program that applied remote viewing and other psychoenergetic methods to intelligence collection. The speaker traces government interest in psychoenergetics from 1972, through early research at SRI International, the Army programs Gondola Wish and Grill Flame, and the transfer of the Army effort to INSCOM, where it continued as Center Lane after congressional action ended its funding within the National Foreign Intelligence Program at the close of fiscal year 1982. The briefing reports, as the program's own figures, 700 operational missions in support of 89 collection projects over five years at a total cost of 703,000 dollars, with more than 200 missions devoted to the Iran hostage situation, and it states that about 85 percent of missions produced target-relevant information and about half produced information of true intelligence value. It describes training, including orientation at SRI, hemispheric synchronization work associated with Robert Monroe, and coordinate remote viewing, and it closes with plans for fiscal year 1984 spending, an expanded mission statement reaching toward telepathic communication and psychokinesis, and proposed research into countermeasures. All figures and success claims are the briefing's own, presented to justify continued support.

## Content account

The briefing is organized in two parts. The first part gives background. It states that since 1972 the United States Government, including the Department of Defense, had examined psychoenergetics, meaning remote viewing, psychokinesis, and remote communication or telepathy, and that early impetus came from remote viewing research by two laser physicists at SRI International, whose subjects are said to have described a secret NSA facility and a Soviet research facility at Semipalatinsk in detail later confirmed, according to the briefing, by human and technical sources.

The script then follows the Army's institutional path. Army interest is dated to 1972 studies of Soviet bloc work; a Missile Intelligence Agency interest in psychokinesis led to an 80,000 dollar SRI contract in August 1977. In September 1977 an INSCOM element began Gondola Wish, an operations security program. In 1978 the Army Chief of Staff for Intelligence cancelled Gondola Wish, placed the subject under a security envelope, and created Grill Flame, tasking INSCOM in October 1978 to build a remote viewing training and collection program. The briefing records a 75,000 dollar SRI orientation training contract by April 1979, formal approval of the INSCOM effort on 13 January 1981, a joint services Grill Flame committee of DIA, the Air Force, and the Army in 1981, and the end of Army participation in the joint program after fiscal year 1982, when the effort continued under the name Center Lane with security and investigative activities funding.

On operations, the briefing states that INSCOM conducted 79 missions in 1979, 386 in 1980, and 153 in 1981, with 47 in the first nine months of 1983, totaling 700 missions across 89 collection projects for several government sponsors, at a stated cost to date of 703,000 dollars. It states that over 85 percent of missions produced information relevant to the target site, while approximately 50 percent produced information of true intelligence value, and that Joint Chiefs of Staff users rated the Iran reporting qualitatively equal to other available sources. These are the program's self-reported figures, offered in a funding and continuation context, and the briefing itself supplies no independent evaluation.

The second part looks forward. It lists proposed fiscal year 1984 expenditures totaling 496,500 dollars in program funds, supplemented by 235,000 dollars of INSCOM funds, covering biomonitoring equipment, a psychological screening and selection profile study at 120,000 dollars, research on extremely low frequency electromagnetic radiation as a possible countermeasure at 65,000 dollars, a new technique for search problems at 175,000 dollars, and continued and new coordinate remote viewing training. It presents a new Center Lane mission statement expanding the work beyond information collection into psychoenergetic communication and psychokinesis, discusses personnel shortages, and states plainly that at the time there was no known reliable means to detect or prevent intrusion by psychoenergetic collectors. The text ends at the heading of its conclusion in the OCR copy.
