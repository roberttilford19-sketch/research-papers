## Premise

The document is a research proposal bearing the assertion, as its title, that direct brain perception and direct brain action exist. It was submitted by Captain Edgar D. Mitchell of the United States Navy and prepared and edited by Andrija Puharich, M.D., and it was released under CIA-RDP96-00787R000100190001-2. The preface states that the proposal collates the thoughts of some forty-two men and women from many disciplines who had met at two conferences, at Wainwright House, Rye, New York, in November 1970 and at the twentieth annual international conference of the Parapsychology Foundation at St. Paul de Vence, France, in August 1971. It was produced to mobilise the resources for a controlled laboratory test of one subject's claimed abilities and, beyond that, for a two-year pilot programme and an eventual permanent institute.

## Abstract

The proposal defines direct brain perception as the acquisition of information without stimulation of the sensory channels, and direct brain action as the initiation of a physical effect without any known physical interaction. It argues that these powers, if real, are of basic importance, that Soviet researchers had recognised the importance of the question whether thought can move an object while American science had largely treated the subject as taboo, and that two events made a decisive test timely: the Apollo 14 extrasensory perception experiment conducted unofficially by Mitchell, and the emergence of Uri Geller, a twenty-four-year-old Israeli who, the proposal states, had repeatedly demonstrated direct brain action under rigid scientific control. The bulk of the text read reports Puharich's field investigation of Geller in Israel across three visits in 1971 and 1972, with frequencies and repeatability percentages for breaking metal objects, moving clock hands, repairing watches, moving a compass needle, and the reported dematerialisation and rematerialisation of objects. It then sets out a Phase II laboratory design in which a metal ring would be manipulated, if at all, under sealed, instrumented, and continuously monitored conditions, with automatic handling, closed-circuit recording, computerised data collection, and a unanimous independent review board. The proposal anticipates using a confirmed result to test whether basic laws of physics are violated.

## Content Account

### Definitions and framing

The introduction contrasts ordinary perception and action, which work through sensory and muscular transducer systems, with direct brain stimulation, a laboratory technique it describes as state of the art, and with the rarer claimed effects that are its subject. It states that there are people, perhaps in the hundreds, who claim direct perception of information and direct bioenergy action on material systems, that empirical as well as some experimental evidence is offered for these powers, and that a clear resolution of the question would do more than any other approach to mobilise scientific and philosophical interest. The framing is explicitly comparative: the proposal states that scientists in the Soviet Union had recognised the pressing importance of the question, that the United States had lagged because of a taboo in the scientific establishment, and it supports that claim with a sequence of broken taboos, instancing hypnosis, the scientific study of sex, survival in space, and heart transplantation.

The preface is unusually candid about the document's status. It states that the proposal is a first draft attempt by the editor, circulated for review, criticism, and consensus, that the editor takes sole responsibility for it at this stage, that the scientific advisory and research committees were not yet fully formed, and that if the editor had taken undue liberties in reporting, formulating concepts, or using names or institutions, he had done so solely to expedite the formulation of a research programme. Every claim in the document should be read with that admission in view.

### The proposed programme

Section III proposes a comprehensive two-year pilot programme, to be carried out in sequential phases so as to limit the rate of expenditure, with an explicit caution that early failures should not prejudice the comprehensive character of the studies. Teams of scientists would travel to investigate alleged cases of talent around the world; experiments would be carried out and data correlated; a full plan would be developed for the following five to ten years; and a report would be prepared to serve as a reference book on the talent and the ongoing studies. The survey list names fifteen areas, including Israel for Geller, Brazil and the Philippines for healers, India for the Bhrigu Samhita manuscripts and holy men, Indonesia for communities said hereditarily to practise telepathy, Mexico for plants and drugs used in direct brain perception, Russia for biocybernetics and psychokinesis research, Czechoslovakia, China and Taiwan, Romania, the Netherlands for Gerard Croiset's precognitive powers, Germany, England, Canada for Bernard Grad's work on the transfer of a healing effect to animals and plants via water, and the United States. If the results were positive, a strong case could then be made, the proposal argues, for a permanent institute for advanced study of the phenomena, which would also serve as a clearing house and a laboratory. Mitchell is named as project leader for the organising effort, with a small full-time research and administrative staff.

### The Geller investigation as reported

Section V reports the results of Program Alpha, Phase I, the field investigation of Geller. The biographical account states that his development began at the age of seven, that he worked in Israel as a translator and photographer's model, and that he became a stage performer in April 1970, his act consisting of telepathy with numbers, words, and drawings, breaking metal objects by will power, and moving clock hands. Puharich studied him during three visits, in August 1971, September 1971, and from November 1971 to April 1972, the last both in the laboratory and during stage appearances. The proposal lists, for the first visit, the breaking of a gold ring held in another person's clenched fist, selective temperature rises of six to eight degrees Fahrenheit on one or the other of a pair of bimetal thermometers, the starting of broken clocks and watches, the moving of watch hands without contact, and ninety per cent accuracy in telepathy tests with three-digit numbers. For the third period it gives frequencies and repeatability percentages, including breaking metal objects 102 times at a stated repeatability of ninety-four per cent, moving clock hands fifty-six times at ninety per cent, and repairing broken watches thirty-six times at seventy-seven per cent, together with compass-needle movement and the reported dematerialisation and rematerialisation of metal objects. It concludes, in the principal investigator's words as rendered in the recognised text, that there is no question in his mind that Geller possesses a power of true action at a distance. These are the proposal's own reports of its editor's field investigation. They are not independent findings, and this assessment does not adopt them.

### The Phase II design

Section VI sets out the research design for the laboratory phase, oriented at this time, as its summary states, to mobilising the resources to carry out the experiment. The design is built around the manipulation of a metal ring. No observer would handle any of the rings; the rings would always be under seal control and handled by automatic devices before, during, and after each trial; selection of ring sets and of the target ring would be automatic and random; filmed records would be displayed on a synchronised split screen; each instrument observer would be assigned to one station only and continually monitored for violation of the control conditions; and all instrument data would be fed into an on-line computer for instant analysis and storage. Any one violation of the control conditions by any participant would be sufficient reason to nullify a test, with the final decision made by the chief investigator. A ring would count as manipulated if there were a deformation of shape, a significant mass change, or a fracture in the metal. The data from each experiment would be reviewed and judged by an independent review board, and any decision on the null hypothesis must be unanimous. The summary records that the design had not yet been put into final form by the scientific advisory committee and the research committee, that it would undoubtedly be continuously upgraded until the experiment commenced, and that the experiment was anticipated before the end of 1972.

### Anticipated outcome

Section VII states that the preliminary findings with Geller in Israel and Germany suggest a high probability of a successful outcome. Should direct brain action be established, the proposal projects tests directed at classically established laws of physics, including the conservation of energy, the fundamental unit of charge, the laws of motion, gravity, the principle of indeterminacy, and charge-parity symmetry, in order to find out whether any basic law is violated and, if none is, to define the phenomenon within the known framework. The appendices begin with references and footnotes listing the editor's own earlier lectures and reports on extrasensory perception, reaching back to a 1952 paper on the possible usefulness of extrasensory perception in psychological warfare presented to a Department of Defense conference at the Pentagon, as that list is rendered in the recognised text.

## Errors and Anomalies

The document's title is an assertion, not a description. "Direct Brain Perception and Direct Brain Action Exist" states as settled the very question the proposed research was designed to resolve unambiguously, and the Phase II design's own summary acknowledges that the experiment had not yet been performed and its design not yet finalised. This assessment therefore treats the title and the Phase I conclusions as the proposers' claims throughout.

The recognised text is severely affected by optical character recognition errors, including misread words on nearly every page, garbled conference programmes, and a title page on which the editor's first name is rendered in a form that appears to read "Henry (Andrita) Puharich" where the preface signature reads Andrija Puharich. Names are given in this assessment only where the recognised text renders them legibly and consistently; the editor's name is given in the form used in his own signature to the preface.

The document contains a duplicated release number in one stamp, rendered with a different prefix digit sequence from the number used consistently elsewhere in the file. The number used in this assessment is the one that recurs across the pages read.

Percentages and frequencies quoted from the Phase I report are the proposal's figures as rendered in the recognised text. Several are accompanied in the original by counts in parentheses that do not in every case correspond exactly to the stated percentage of the stated frequency, and the recognised text is too degraded at those points to determine whether the discrepancy is the document's or the recognition's. No conclusion in this assessment rests on those figures.
