## Premise

The document is the tasking sheet for a single remote-viewing utility assessment. It was produced inside the government remote-viewing program to state, in advance, exactly what a remote viewer would be asked to obtain about a shielded document, so that the viewer's output could later be scored against a fixed list of questions.

## Abstract

This one-page declassified document sets out "Project N-1 X," described in its own text as a utility assessment to determine a remote viewer's ability and propensity to collect foreign intelligence against documents shielded from normal perception. The sheet states that Document N-1 X is the first in a series of projects, numbered one through five, to be conducted. It gives encrypted coordinates and lists the essential elements the viewer is to obtain by accessing a specified document, identified by a document number. The requested elements are bibliographic and physical: the document's main themes, purpose, principal sections and subsections, author, publisher, extent of dissemination, security classification, language, value as viewed by customers, title, date published, number of pages, size, a description of its front cover, and its use of charts, photographs, and graphs. The viewer is further asked to identify the date and principal sources of the information the document contains, and to describe the container used to store the document, the building in which it is stored, and the room in which it is stored. Finally, the viewer is asked to provide an overhead view of the geographical area of the building's location, including recognizable geographical features and easily recognizable or historical sites. The sheet closes with the handling caveat "HANDLE VIA SKEET CHANNELS ONLY" as rendered by optical character recognition. The document is a tasking instrument only; it records no session, no viewer output, and no evaluation of results.

## Content Account

The document's background paragraph frames the exercise narrowly. Project N-1 X is a utility assessment, which in the program's usage meant a test of whether remote viewing could produce usable intelligence, and the target is not a place or a person but a document shielded from normal perception. The assessment therefore tests document access specifically: whether a viewer, given encrypted coordinates, can report the contents and circumstances of a paper record he or she cannot see.

The essential elements of information fall into three groups. The first and largest group concerns the document as a publication: themes, purpose, structure, authorship, publication data, classification, language, dissemination, and assessed value to customers. The second group concerns physical description: page count, size, front cover, and the presence of charts, photographs, and graphs. The third group moves outward from the document to its storage environment: the container, the room, the building, and finally an overhead geographical view of the surrounding area with recognizable features. This outward progression, from content to container to landscape, is characteristic of the program's tasking style, in which a single session was expected to move between levels of detail.

The document contains no findings. It does not report what any viewer produced, whether the information obtained was accurate, or how the assessment was scored. Any account of the outcome of Project N-1 X would have to come from a different record.
