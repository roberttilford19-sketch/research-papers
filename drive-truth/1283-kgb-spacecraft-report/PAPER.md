## Premise

The item reports, at several removes, an alleged incident in Siberia in which Soviet soldiers were said to have shot down an unidentified craft and to have been killed by its occupants in return. Its own headline is careful in a way the body of the story is not: it describes the material as alleged evidence of a mishap involving a UFO, published by a newspaper.

## Abstract

This single-page Foreign Broadcast Information Service item, serial AU3003152893, transmits an article published in the Kiev newspaper Holos Ukrayiny on 27 March 1993, which itself reprints an article titled "Cosmic Revenge" from the newspaper Ternopil Vechirniy. The reprint states that after Mikhail Gorbachev dissolved a KGB intelligence administration in 1991, material from that department reached the Central Intelligence Agency, and it attributes to a magazine it names as Canadian Weekly World News the report that United States intelligence obtained a 250-page file on a UFO attack on a military unit in Siberia. According to the story as transmitted, a low-flying saucer-shaped craft appeared over a unit on training manoeuvres, a surface-to-air missile was fired at it, and five short humanoids with large heads and large black eyes emerged from the wreckage. Two surviving soldiers are quoted as testifying that the beings merged into a single glowing sphere which exploded, turning twenty-three watching soldiers into stone. The story adds that the remains were taken to a secret research institution near Moscow, and it quotes an unnamed CIA representative on the danger the case would pose if the file corresponded to reality.

## Content account

The chain of transmission is the most important fact about this document, and it is fully visible in its own header and text. The Foreign Broadcast Information Service monitored and translated a Ukrainian newspaper. That newspaper reprinted another newspaper. That newspaper cited a magazine as its authority for the existence of a KGB file. The alleged file itself is not reproduced, quoted at length, dated, or identified by any number. No participant is named. No unit, place, or date for the alleged Siberian incident is given anywhere in the item.

Within that chain, the story's specific claims are these. A 250-page file is said to contain documentary photographs, drawings, and testimony from participants. An unnamed CIA representative is quoted calling the case "a horrific picture of revenge on the part of extraterrestrial creatures, a picture that makes one's blood freeze." The craft is described as low-flying and saucer-shaped. The missile launch is described as unexpected and unexplained. The two survivors are said to have survived because they stood in shade and were less exposed to the luminous explosion. Specialists are said to assume that an unknown energy source changed the soldiers' living tissue into a substance whose molecular composition is no different from limestone. A CIA representative is quoted a second time, conditionally: "If the KGB file corresponds to reality, this is an extremely menacing case."

The conditional in that final quotation is the only qualification inside the story itself, and it deserves to be preserved, because the story's own framing elsewhere treats its material as established. The transmitting service makes no judgement of its own. Foreign Broadcast Information Service items of this kind record what a foreign publication printed. They do not endorse it, and the presence of this item in a released CIA file shows only that the item was collected and retained.
