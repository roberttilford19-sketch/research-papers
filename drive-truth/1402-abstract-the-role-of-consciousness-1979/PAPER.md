## Abstract

This single-page document is the typed abstract, submitted on a conference form for an invited paper at a 1979 symposium entitled "The Role of Consciousness in the Physical World," of a paper by Harold Puthoff, Russell Targ, and Edwin C. May of SRI International, entitled within the abstract itself "Experimental Psi Research; Implications for Physics." The authors state that experimental laboratory work continues to provide evidence for so-called psi processes, which they define as a class of interactions between consciousness and the physical world as yet unexplained, comprising both the acquisition of information not presented to any obvious sense and the production of physical effects not mediated by any obvious mechanism. They state that their laboratory concentrated on the former, investigating remote viewing, the claimed ability of certain individuals to access and describe, by mental processes, information blocked from ordinary perception by distance or shielding. They describe a database of more than one hundred experiments, with targets ranging from objects in nearby light-tight canisters to geographic sites at transcontinental distances, viewed from locations including shielded Faraday cages and a submerged submarine. They argue that any model of psi must account for the characteristics they report, and they anticipate that much of the data will be accounted for within physics as presently understood or by conservative extrapolations proposed for other data, and that the psi database may in turn shed light on current problems in physics.

### What the abstract claims

The abstract divides psi processes into two classes: the acquisition of information not presented to any obvious sense, and the production of physical effects not mediated by any obvious mechanism. It states that the SRI laboratory concentrated primarily on the first class, under the name remote viewing. Its empirical claims are that the laboratory's database consisted of more than one hundred remote viewing experiments; that targets ranged from objects in nearby light-tight canisters to geographic sites at transcontinental distances; and that viewing locations included shielded Faraday cages and a submerged submarine. From these observations, the authors state, models proposed to explain psi processes must account for three reported characteristics: a bit rate and a spatial resolution, the apparent ineffectiveness of ordinary electrical shielding, and relative insensitivity to distance up to approximately ten thousand kilometres. The numerical values for the bit rate and resolution are garbled in the recognition of the scan, as described below, and are therefore not restated as figures in this assessment.

### The authors' expectation for physics

The abstract closes with the authors' stated anticipation: that although such phenomena might appear to conflict with the laws of physics, much of the data will in all probability be accounted for either within the framework of physics as presently understood, or on the basis of conservative extrapolations that have been proposed to account for other, non-psi, data; and that, conversely, the psi database may shed light on some of the current problems in physics. This is the authors' expectation as stated in 1979, recorded here as their position and not as a finding.
