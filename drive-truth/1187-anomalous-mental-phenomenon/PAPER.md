## Premise

The plan proposes a long-term, systematic, and comprehensive investigation of anomalous mental phenomena, integrating basic and applied research with standard human-intelligence practice so that, if the research succeeded, anomalous cognition data could be incorporated into the intelligence community's existing infrastructure as an additional form of human-intelligence reporting. It was submitted by the Cognitive Sciences Laboratory of Science Applications International Corporation to the Defense Intelligence Agency in response to a stated government requirement.

## Abstract

This 59-page plan, dated 7 June 1991, by Edwin C. May and Wanda L. W. Luke of the Cognitive Sciences Laboratory at Science Applications International Corporation, was presented to the Defense Intelligence Agency under request for proposals number MDA908-91-R-0133. Its objective is a long-term, systematic, comprehensive investigation of anomalous mental phenomena, combining basic and applied research with standard human-intelligence practices for intelligence-community applications. After historical background and an overview, the plan sets out definitions, objectives, and a method organized around anomalous cognition, acquisition of information by mental means, and anomalous perturbation, mental interaction with physical systems, divided into macroscopic and microscopic forms. Applied workstreams address receiver selection, training, target selection, protocol development, search investigation, and analysis; basic-research workstreams address the source and transmission of anomalous cognition information, including target entropy analysis, four-dimensional calculations drawing on general relativity, and vector and scalar potentials in electromagnetic theory, plus neuroscience approaches. The work is phased across fiscal years 1992 through 1996. The plan contemplates answering within two years how anomalous cognition data should be integrated as additional human-intelligence data into existing infrastructure, and allows for a training school for analysts as early as fiscal year 1994 if research warranted a permanent operations unit. A rationale section argues each item, including communications applications using redundancy coding and countermeasures through jamming and deception; recommendations, references, a definitions annex, and a bibliography complete it. The plan is a proposal: it reports no new experimental results, and its schedules are intentions, not outcomes.

## Content Account

The plan's organizing idea is integration. Anomalous cognition is treated throughout as a prospective data source to be engineered into an intelligence system: receivers are to be selected and trained the way other specialist personnel are, targets and protocols standardized, output analyzed and evaluated, and the finished product delivered into the same infrastructure that handles human-intelligence reporting. The document states the integration question explicitly — how anomalous cognition data should be integrated as additional human-intelligence data into the existing infrastructure — and assigns it a two-year horizon, with downstream institutional consequences (an analyst training school, field evaluations, possibly a permanent operations unit) all expressly conditional on the research succeeding.

Its basic-research program is candid about how little mechanism was established. The transmission workstreams are theoretical calculations with decision points: four-dimensional calculations suggesting a possible backward flow of information consistent with general relativity, with definitive results expected by the end of fiscal year 1993, and calculations on the vector and scalar potentials of electromagnetic theory, initiated in awareness of the controversy surrounding those potentials. The anomalous perturbation section acknowledges a definitional problem in its own text: anomalous perturbation is defined negatively, as what remains after all known sources of perturbation are eliminated, and isolating a system completely is very difficult; it further reports that the former program's analysis of ten years of literature could not reach a definite conclusion about the existence of macroscopic anomalous perturbation. Claims carried over from prior work — for example, that redundancy coding in one experiment raised a binary hit rate from 60 percent to 100 percent across fifty bits — are the plan's citations of earlier research, attributed here to the plan and not verified by this desk.

The document's classification history is part of its content. It was classified SECRET/NOFORN when issued, with declassification on OADR, and the released copy mixes classified plan sections with an unclassified definitions annex and bibliography, so that the program's working vocabulary — receiver, sender, session, search, second sight, and dozens of related terms — is preserved in the same file as its schedules. Science Applications International Corporation's recommendations close the plan's argument, but the document remains what its title says: a plan, whose value as a record lies in showing what the laboratory proposed, in what order, and on what stated assumptions.
