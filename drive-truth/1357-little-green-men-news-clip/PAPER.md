## Premise

The letter was written to bring what its author regarded as extremely important information about unidentified flying objects to the attention of the newspaper's readers, and to argue for open investigation of the subject. Its stated burden is that official secrecy and censorship were concealing the truth, that congressional hearings then expected would end that policy, and that contact with what the author calls a superior civilisation would transform religion, government, science, the military establishment, and above all the human mind.

## Abstract

This single-page item is a clipping from the Amarillo Globe-Times, filed under the heading of letters from readers, of a letter signed John Laval of Matawan, New Jersey, under the heading "Little Green Men." The letter is arranged as five numbered assertions. It asserts first that unidentified flying objects are unknown superior machines under intelligent control from an extraterrestrial source, which have been entering the atmosphere in great numbers since 1944 to observe and map the planet, and that whether their motives are friendly or hostile remains to be seen. It asserts second that the United States Air Force, with the help of the Central Intelligence Agency and the National Security Council, has conducted a propaganda campaign to persuade Americans that the objects are misidentified natural phenomena, illusions, or hallucinations. It asserts third, addressing the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena and its director, that the committee has fought for open investigation, and that hearings the author expected in 1964 would end the policy of secrecy. It asserts fourth that when the truth comes out and contact is achieved, a radical change will follow across human institutions. It asserts fifth that the American and Soviet manned space programmes are a propaganda front, that large rockets are already obsolete for space exploration, and that advanced nations are working to duplicate the objects' anti-gravity propulsion system. Every assertion summarised here is the letter writer's own claim, recorded as such and not verified or endorsed by this desk.
