## Premise

The memorandum sets out to audit the public release of the DOW-UAP-D32 mission report on administrative and disclosure grounds alone. It compares what the declassified record documents against what it withholds, and it expressly declines to infer anything about the origin or physical nature of the phenomenon the report describes.

## Abstract

This memorandum, dated May 10, 2026, reviews the declassified DOW-UAP-D32 mission report and its associated imagery. According to the memorandum, the underlying record documents a 45-minute observation, from 1559Z to 1644Z, by an MQ-9 Reaper using an AN/DAS-4 sensor over MGRS sector 37SFU in Syria, near the Euphrates Volcanic Field. The aircrew narrative quoted in the record describes a “misshapen and uneven ball of white light,” while the structured metadata classifies the phenomenon as “Plasma.” The memorandum arranges these points in a tiered evidentiary table, then identifies three administrative inconsistencies: the classification in the metadata is more specific than the narrative and is not supported by any spectral or electromagnetic data in the release; the documented light and glare halo effects show sensor saturation without resolving the source; and only about five to six seconds of footage were released from a 45-minute observation, which prevents independent analysis of motion or stability over time. It closes with a list of six categories of withheld data whose release would be required to resolve the classification, including raw spectral telemetry, the complete video record, the payload manifest, range-to-target metadata, radar logs, and any internal after-action or AARO assessments. The memorandum draws no conclusion about what the phenomenon was.

3. IDENTIFIED ADMINISTRATIVE INCONSISTENCIES

3.1 Classification Dissonance: The aircrew narrative provides a visually descriptive account (“misshapen
and uneven ball of white light”). In contrast, the structured metadata applies the specific classification
“Plasma.” The declassified record does not contain spectral, electromagnetic, or other supporting sensor
data that would substantiate this technical classification.

3.2 Sensor Saturation and Imaging Artifacts: The report documents light/glare halo effects. Such
effects are consistent with intense luminous sources in AN/DAS-4 electro-optical/infrared imagery.
Released images show diffraction and blooming, confirming high radiant intensity relative to the sensor,
but do not resolve the source’s underlying nature.
3.3 Media and Data Gap: The mission log records 45 minutes of observation, yet only approximately 5–
6 seconds of footage have been publicly released. This substantial gap prevents independent analysis of
the phenomenon’s motion profile, stability, or changes over time.

4. GEOSPATIAL CONTEXT

The event occurred in MGRS Sector 37SFU in the vicinity of the Euphrates Volcanic Field. The mission
report establishes this location but contains no documentation of physical interaction between the
observed phenomenon and the ground.

5. REQUIRED DATA FOR RESOLUTION

1. Raw and processed spectral telemetry supporting the “Plasma” classification.
2. Complete sensor data package, including full FMV from 1559Z–1644Z.
3. Platform payload manifest listing all sensors employed.
4. Range-to-target metadata and associated geolocation data.
5. Radar logs and any Radar Cross Section (RCS) information.
6. Any internal after-action reports or AARO assessments.

6. CONCLUSION

The DOW-UAP-D32 report documents a persistent, high-intensity aerial event that was officially
classified in part as “Plasma.” While core observational facts are established, the supporting technical data
remain withheld. This memorandum is submitted solely to identify these administrative and disclosure
gaps.
