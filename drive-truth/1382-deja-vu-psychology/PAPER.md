## Abstract

This staged assessment describes a collected journal extract from Psi Research, dated September and December 1984, released under the reference CIA-RDP96-00792R000700810001-1. Its principal content is Dr. Harvey Irwin's review of Vernon M. Neppe's The Psychology of Déjà Vu: Have I Been Here Before?, published by Witwatersrand University Press in Johannesburg in 1983. Irwin explains the phenomenon as a subjectively inappropriate impression of familiarity, and he follows Neppe in distinguishing related terms for experiences that are heard or met rather than seen. The review weighs Neppe's chosen definition, which Irwin considers possibly too broad because it does not require that the situation be genuinely new to the person. It also examines Neppe's attempt to separate déjà experiences from precognitive ones according to how precisely an earlier dream can be recalled, a distinction Irwin calls partly arbitrary but necessary at an early stage of research. The review's strongest criticism concerns Neppe's survey of epileptic, schizophrenic, paranormally inclined, and otherwise ordinary respondents: Irwin doubts the representativeness of the samples and objects to statistical tests that presume random sampling, while crediting the work as a pilot study that operationalised the experience and produced interview schedules and questionnaires for later researchers. A second review, of a near-death experience textbook edited by Bruce Greyson and Charles Flynn, begins on the final page and is cut off in this extract.

## Content Account

Irwin places the book in a field with a sizeable literature but little recent theoretical or empirical progress, and he notes its systematic treatment of definitions and of the qualitative characteristics of the experience. He finds the treatment of underlying mechanisms too condensed, regrets the absence of the distinction between episodic and semantic memory, and describes the book's style as close to a doctoral thesis despite its stated aim at the intelligent lay reader. He nevertheless concludes that it is a substantial publication and essential reading for psychologists and parapsychologists contemplating empirical study in the area, while dissenting from the foreword's praise of the book as a major work of scientific and imaginative genius.
