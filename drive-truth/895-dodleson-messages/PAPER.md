## Errors and anomalies

The document is internally inconsistent about the century of the past correspondent. It variously places Lucas in the 1500s, calls the communications fifteenth-century, dates the first message to March 28, 1521, and describes a gap of over 400 years running "from the 15th to the 21st century," although the 1980s recipients were in the twentieth century. These cannot all be right, and the document does not notice the conflict. The college given for Thomas Harden is spelled "Braznos College," an error for Brasenose College, Oxford. The star coordinates passage says the object was "discovered to be a quazar," a misspelling of quasar, and presents the identification as a confirmation without a source. The document states that Fermat's Last Theorem "was indeed solved in Ken's lifetime" and elsewhere that it "was proven correct in 1995": the proof was completed by Andrew Wiles in 1994 and published in 1995, so the broad point stands, but the document treats a prediction attributed to 2109 as validated without showing that the message predates the proof in any checkable record. The February 9, 1985 message is reproduced with a long passage duplicated word for word inside itself, which may be a feature of the source message or a transcription fault; the document does not remark on it. The dramatic claim that both Society for Psychical Research investigators disappeared and that the Society denied any record of the case is relayed from the programme with no independent support in the document.
