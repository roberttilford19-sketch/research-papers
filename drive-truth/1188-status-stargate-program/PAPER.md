## Premise

The memorandum sets out the Central Intelligence Agency's planned actions, in response to Congressional direction, for accepting transfer of the STAR GATE program from the Defense Intelligence Agency, commissioning an independent retrospective and technical review of the program's twenty-year history, and surveying the program's records for declassification.

## Abstract

This three-page declassified memorandum describes the Central Intelligence Agency's response to Congressional direction to transfer the STAR GATE program from the Defense Intelligence Agency to the CIA. The Agency is working with the Defense Intelligence Agency on the transfer and has coordinated on that agency's report, whose review and analysis will feed into an independent CIA review. For the retrospective review of STAR GATE's twenty-year history and analysis of its results, the Agency will work with the National Research Council; tasking statements have been exchanged to establish a Blue Ribbon Panel of parapsychology experts so the review will be comprehensive and unbiased. The Council will be asked to review the program's research and development studies, fewer than fifty, with unclassified reports available; meet program officials; evaluate all program data, including designs, results, and proposed future research, recommending which areas merit further work and which should not be pursued; and draft the final report and help brief Congress. The review is to be unclassified but unreleased until Congress and the agencies are briefed, and could take up to six months. The memorandum also describes cooperation requested from the United States Army Research Institute and a declassification effort: certain aspects of past CIA sponsorship will be declassified immediately, a DCI classification review committee will examine documents, and the Defense Intelligence Agency will be asked to declassify its sponsorship, transfer its documents to the CIA, and assist. A comprehensive management strategy for transferred positions and funding is to follow.

## Content Account

The memorandum is a planning document, not a report of results. Its findings, such as they are, are administrative. First, the transfer itself is treated as settled Congressional direction: the Agency is executing it, not debating it. Second, the review mechanism is external. Rather than evaluate STAR GATE internally, the Agency proposes to route the retrospective and technical assessment through the National Research Council and a Blue Ribbon Panel, and it specifies that the panel's product will be unclassified. Third, declassification is presented as a deliberate, staged process, with some sponsorship facts declassified immediately and the remainder — including actual operational targets, customers, and resources — reserved for a DCI classification review committee.

The document's own claims are procedural throughout. It asserts that fewer than fifty research and development studies have unclassified reports available, that the study could take up to six months, and that the program's history spans twenty years. These figures are the memorandum's own statements and are reported here as such; this desk has not verified them against any other record. The memorandum does not evaluate whether remote viewing works, and it records no conclusion about the program's scientific merit; that judgment was the task assigned to the panel it describes.
