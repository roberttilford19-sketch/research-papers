## Premise

The report was produced to account, to the contracting officer's technical representative, for the first year of a five-year program (fiscal 1986 to 1990) of research and development in psychoenergetics run by SRI International with a network of subcontractors and consultants. It sets out to give a progress update task by task, with the finances for each task, and its stated program aims are to document that psychoenergetic phenomena are real and reproducible, to determine the mechanisms underlying them, and to bring the field into the mainstream of human performance research with a scientific foundation equivalent to the rest of that field.

## Abstract

This document is the final technical report for fiscal year 1986 of SRI International's Enhanced Human Performance Investigation, by Edwin C. May, dated December 1986 and covering work from 1 July to 15 November 1986 under a five-year psychoenergetics program. It defines its field as direct interactions between human consciousness and the environment, divided into information acquisition, called remote viewing or extrasensory perception, and production of physical effects, called remote action or psychokinesis. The report describes a Scientific Oversight Committee that reviewed seventeen protocols in advance and returned year-end assessments, which are reproduced with SRI's responses in an appendix. It reports progress and spending task by task: a figure-of-merit system for scoring free-response remote viewing, a subcontracted parapsychology literature database that entered some sixteen years of journal abstracts in its first year, medical examinations begun for experiment participants, personality assessment using the Personality Assessment System, and remote action experiments. On those experiments its reporting is notably self-critical: the Princeton random number generator data were judged not collected in a form that could test SRI's Intuitive Data Sorting model, the Syracuse distance experiment recorded no data because of subcontracting delays, and a skin-parameters result reported at p = 0.019 is stated to be uninterpretable because of two procedural defects the report itself identifies.

## Content Account

The introduction defines the field and the program. Psychoenergetic phenomena are defined as direct interactions between human consciousness and the environment which, although the mechanism is unexplained, can be observed and recorded, falling into two categories: acquisition of information, called remote viewing or extrasensory perception, and production of physical effects, called remote action or psychokinesis. The three program objectives are stated as documenting that psychoenergetic phenomena are real and reproducible, determining the mechanisms underlying them, and bringing psychoenergetics into the mainstream of human performance research. The research hierarchy runs from explanatory mechanisms and phenomenological properties through physical, physiological, and psychological correlates to optimal strategies for applications. Resources combine an in-house SRI core group, described as studying psychoenergetics for over a decade, with subcontractors and consultants then including personnel from Princeton University, Syracuse University, John F. Kennedy University, the Palo Alto Medical Clinic, MARS Measurements Associates, the Parapsychology Sources of Information Center, the Mind Science Foundation, and the University of Delaware.

The progress chapter opens with the status of subcontracts. A table lists the fiscal 1986 agreements by type — purchase orders, consultant agreements, services contracts, and full subcontracts — with funding amounts from $16,000 to $109,000; one contract, with the Palo Alto Medical Clinic at $109,000, and a full subcontract with Time Research Institute at $107,000, are the largest shown. The final contracts with Syracuse University, the Mind Science Foundation, John F. Kennedy University, and the University of Delaware were signed by the end of the third quarter, and the Time Research Institute contract was terminated on 19 May 1986 after extensive review of the subcontractor's statement of work and qualifications.

Oversight is a major theme. The Scientific Oversight Committee met first on 6 January 1986 to review protocols in advance, to volunteer members to monitor specific experiments in detail and review final reports as if they were journal articles, and to recommend future directions. Seventeen protocols were submitted; the committee found the SRI protocols in order, with comments seeking clarification, except for one subcontractor protocol whose approach it questioned. The committee reconvened on 6 and 7 November 1986 for year-end a posteriori assessments of the experimental designs and protocols, which are reproduced in an appendix with SRI's explanatory commentary appended.

Among the substantive tasks reported in the portions read, the remote viewing analysis task presents the figure-of-merit system: target and response materials are coded as yes or no answers to a set of descriptor questions, accuracy is defined as the proportion of target material correctly described, reliability as the proportion of the response that is correct, and the figure of merit is the product of the two; mean chance expectations had been calculated, and extensions were recommended for fiscal 1987. Of $189,000 budgeted for that task, $194,700 was spent. The resource library task, subcontracted to the Parapsychology Sources of Information Center under Rhea A. White, completed the first year of a multiyear effort to build a database of parapsychological literature: hardware and software were in place, a first working version implemented, and approximately sixteen years of abstracts for each of the major parapsychological journals entered, with an eventual goal of covering the entire literature including books, dissertations, reports, proceedings, and non-English material in English abstract. Health assessments began in the third quarter: participants selected from the larger subject pool undergo a medical examination including height, weight, pulse, blood pressure, blood analysis, and urinalysis, and eleven individuals had been examined by the close of fiscal 1986, at $52,500 spent against a reduced budget after it was decided that only actual participants, rather than all one hundred potential participants, needed examination. Personality assessment work continued to center on the Personality Assessment System, which interprets profiles from the Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scales, with the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator as the principal self-report instrument under examination.

The remote action modeling work is reported with unusual candor. SRI had been developing a model of psychoenergetic functioning called Intuitive Data Sorting and applied it to a Princeton Engineering Anomalies Research random number generator experiment. The report states that the analysis was consistent with Princeton's own — the effects are small but persistent and statistically robust — but that the data were not collected to provide a specific test of the model: sixty-five percent of the total data were collected at a single sequence length, which was the only sequence length that scored at mean chance expectation, and because the data were not collected uniformly as a function of sequence length it is difficult to interpret the analysis, and premature to speculate on model forms that could fit the data.

Two further remote action tasks show the same pattern. The Syracuse University subcontract, to determine the effects of distance between remote action subjects and random number generators and performance differences across noise diode, beta-decay, and pseudorandom sources, was not in place until 24 June 1986 because of human-subjects questions, cost-accounting differences, a publication approval dispute, and a change of principal investigator; as a consequence no experimental data were recorded as of 15 November 1986, though the hardware, software, and the SRI–Syracuse telecommunications link had been developed and test data transmitted, and the Syracuse staff volunteered to complete the pilot and confirmation work in fiscal 1987. The Mind Science Foundation experiment on remote action influences on electrodermal activity reported evidence for a psi effect in the remote action condition at p = 0.019, no evidence in the Intuitive Data Sorting condition, and a superiority of the former over the latter approaching significance at p = 0.08, two-tailed. The report then states that two problems in the procedure render these results uninterpretable: a rest period whose duration was determined by the key participant made the two conditions indistinguishable, and the order of conditions was set by the timing of a computer keyboard carriage return while the experimenter entered subject information, giving the experimenter the first opportunity to sort the data. Alternative protocols are recommended.

The appendix reproduces Scientific Oversight Committee reviewers' comments verbatim, with SRI responses. In the portions read, reviewers criticize the Personality Assessment System work, including a prediction that good remote viewers should be "bottom-up" rather than "top-down" processors, and one reviewer objects that a study titled as neuropsychological assessment is a reanalysis of the assessment system; SRI responds that performance will be reported as effect-size estimates rather than p-values and that a position paper evaluating all screening methodologies is contemplated. On the electrodermal study, one reviewer objects that a p-value of 0.08 is nonsignificant and should not be reported as significance, disputes the statistical analysis, and commends SRI staff's detection and reporting of the two serious artifacts that render the results uninterpretable; SRI's responses concede the deleted phrase and answer the methodological points in detail. A statistical reviewer of an interim report on remote action effects on the velocity of the alga Dunaliella argues that the data are clearly non-normal and fat-tailed, that the regressions are poor, and that the experiment is looking for an effect that lies in the noise. Reviewer comments on a piezoelectric strain gauge experiment, addressing shielding and isolation, appear at the end of the text available here.

All experimental results in this account are the report's own reported results, and the report itself qualifies or withdraws several of them, as recorded above. The financial figures are the report's own task-level statements.
