## Abstract

The review describes Rietdijk's book as three parts of five chapters each, every chapter preceded by its own abstract. The first part, on the new physics, presents the Bohr-Einstein controversy, the EPR paradox, the Bell inequalities, and Aspect's experiments as establishing the non-local character of quantum mechanics, then expounds relativity and the four-dimensional space-time continuum, Rietdijk's claimed proof of a retro-active effect, and his concept of action-distance, which is zero for non-locally related events. The second part turns to parapsychology and destiny, adopting Walker's observational theory, treating the tendency of coincidences to form meaningful patterns as fundamental, and proposing super-local co-ordination by integrative forces, together with hypotheses about consciousness as primary alongside matter, God as a pattern-forming agent, and groups of people acting as integrated supersouls. ### The reviewer's judgment

The reviewer's verdict is favourable but not uncritical. The reviewer finds Rietdijk generally careful in distinguishing fact and inference from hypothesis and speculation, describes the position reached as an enlightened form of Christian belief, and praises the clarity of the assumptions and the accessibility of the arguments to an interested lay reader. The reviewer hopes the book will be translated into English soon, suggests that an index would be a welcome addition, and closes that strand of the review by borrowing Giordano Bruno's saying, "Si non e vero, e bene trovato."

Three criticisms are stated. Moral judgments cannot logically be derived from existential judgments, and on that point the reviewer finds Rietdijk unclear, remarking that despite his own rule of no water in the scientific wine, the attempt to convince the reader that scientific and religious world views are part of the same pattern becomes invincible only with difficulty. The introduction of consciousness as a primary element is, in the reviewer's opinion, unnecessary, and the associated hypothesis is described as shaky. Most substantially, the reviewer takes issue with the hypothesis of realism implicit in Rietdijk's proof of the retro-active effect, namely the realism of unobserved quantum-mechanically random events, and sets against it the reviewer's own advocated hypothesis of indefinite reality, from which a very different world view might be developed. The reviewer treats the existence of that unobvious choice, which might be settled by experiment, as the book's most valuable provocation, and notes that the crucial experiment for a related question, an omniscient observer, has yet to be invented.
