## Abstract

This item is a two-page printout of an automated database search, of the kind produced by the DIALOG service, preserved in a declassified CIA file. The searcher first built a set for parapsychology combined with remote viewing, which the printout numbers at 31 records, then combined that set with Russia, the USSR, or Soviet terms, which narrowed it to three records. The printout then displays two of the resulting items in keyword-in-context form. The first, displayed under the Academic Index file, is a November 1993 Phi Delta Kappan article by Irving M. Klotz criticizing multicultural science-education essays used in Portland, Oregon schools; its keyword hits come from passages quoting material that urges students toward psychoenergetics, precognition, psychokinesis, and remote viewing, and from its discussion of the suppression of genetics in the Soviet Union. The second is a Time magazine item of 23 January 1984 on extrasensory perception research and psychic weapons, shown through excerpts concerning a clairvoyant's attempt to predict Soviet submarine movements, official denials of Pentagon interest, and claims of a multimillion-dollar remote-viewing project. The printout shows the search logic, the set counts, and partial displays only. It is a retrieval record, not a study, and it contains no findings of its own beyond what the retrieved items themselves say.

## Content Account

The first page records the search itself. A set numbered S1 combines parapsychology with remote viewing in proximity; intermediate counts shown on the printout include 18,750 for one term, 242,168 for another, and 304 for a remote-viewing proximity expression, with the combined S1 set at 31 records. A second set, S2, gathers the geographical and political terms, with counts of 27,052 for Russia, 15,396 for USSR, and 59,725 for Soviet shown on the printout, totalling 17,234 records as displayed (the arithmetic of the displayed counts is the printout's own and is not reconciled here). A third set, S3, combines S1 with S2 and returns three records. The searcher then asked for a display of S3 in a keyword-in-context format, and the system replied that the KWIC option was not available in files 46 and 86, before displaying items from file 88, the Academic Index.

The first displayed item is an article by Irving M. Klotz, "Multicultural perspectives in science education: one prescription for failure," published in Phi Delta Kappan, volume 75, number 3, page 266, in November 1993. Its displayed abstract states that the Baseline Essay on Science is a misleading text used in the Portland, Oregon public schools that overrates ancient Egyptians as pioneers of modern science, and that the essay's incorrectness leads students not to take science seriously. The keyword passages displayed around the search terms include a passage, apparently quoted from the material Klotz criticizes, urging that a budding astrophysicist should concentrate on psychoenergetics, defined there as the multidisciplinary study of the interface and interaction of human consciousness, and seeking to acquire talents in precognition, psychokinesis, remote viewing, and related modes. A further displayed passage concerns the suppression of Mendelian genetics in the Soviet Union under Stalinist Marxism. On the face of the display, therefore, the first hit is a false drop for the search's apparent purpose: an education-policy article in which the search terms occur inside quoted material and a historical discussion, not a report of Soviet parapsychology research.

The second displayed item is a Time magazine piece of 23 January 1984, displayed under the title "An E.S.P. gap" with a subtitle referring to psychic weapons research. The excerpts shown describe a clairvoyant, named in the excerpt as Madame Zodiac, looking at top-secret photographs and charts and attempting to predict the movements of Soviet submarines off the East Coast, for a payment of four hundred dollars in cash. They record that the Pentagon denied any interest in parapsychology, with a Defense Department spokesman saying officials had scrutinized budgets. They also carry a claim, attributed in the excerpt to a writer named McRae, that there was a multimillion-dollar project part of which focused on remote-viewing experiments, and a statement attributed to Targ that in the Soviet Union psychic research was taken seriously at the highest levels, with a fragment concerning his going to Grant's Tomb in New York City. The display is fragmentary: sentences are cut at the keyword windows, and the item's full text, its author line, and its argument cannot be reconstructed from this printout.

No third item is displayed in the pages available. The document therefore establishes only that the search was run, what its sets and counts were as printed, and what the first two displayed items contained in excerpt. It contains no evaluation of either item by the searcher.
