## Abstract

This staged assessment describes a collected institutional newsletter, issue number two of the Bulletin of the William C. Menninger Dream Laboratory, dated summer 1969 and released under the reference CIA-RDP96-00787R000400040026-8. The laboratory was based at Maimonides Medical Center in Brooklyn, New York. The bulletin announces the first Gardner Murphy Fellowships, funded by Gardner Murphy, a former president of the American Psychological Association, to let college students take part in research; the first recipients were Donadrian Rice of Wofford College and Mildred Carbone of Hofstra University, and several other summer students and part-time workers are named. It reports that laboratory staff, including Montague Ullman, Stanley Krippner, and Charles Honorton, would present six papers at the Parapsychological Association's annual meeting in New York in September 1969, and it summarises Krippner's report of fifty-five experimental and seventy-nine pilot nights of dream telepathy work judged by outside judges. It announces the arrival from England of Malcolm Bessent, who served as a research subject in target picture, psychometric, plant psychokinesis, and eight-night precognitive dream studies. Finally it describes Honorton's new alpha and extrasensory perception project, in which higher alpha activity accompanied more correct card guesses, a difference the bulletin calls statistically significant. This assessment reports the bulletin's announcements as its own record of the laboratory's activities.

Readers wishing to receive future bulletins were asked to write to Charles Honorton at the laboratory's Maimonides address.
