## Premise

The file was assembled as working reference material around the Gemini 4 (GT-4) mission of June 1965. Its core is the post-flight experiment debriefing in which the GT-4 crew, James McDivitt and Edward White, discussed their scientific observations and experiment results with investigators. Around that core the file gathers the mission operations report that defined GT-4's objectives, administrative correspondence about the handling and analysis of the mission's photography, and press clippings. Read as a whole, the file documents how the Gemini program planned, flew, and then processed the scientific return of an early long-duration crewed flight.

## Abstract

The file opens with NASA routing slips and the Mission Operation Report for GT-4, dated June 1, 1965, with its May 24, 1965 base report. That report records the mission's objectives — a flight of about 97 hours and 50 minutes over 62 revolutions, evaluation of crew performance and flight control systems on a long-duration mission, an extravehicular activity (EVA) period, and a program of thirteen scientific experiments — together with descriptions of the G4C pressure suit, the Titan II launch vehicle, program costs, the tracking network, and recovery arrangements. The centerpiece of the file is the experiment debriefing transcript, in which McDivitt and White answer investigators' questions experiment by experiment: the structure of the airglow, meteors seen entering the atmosphere below the spacecraft, the southern lights over Australia, electrostatic and radiation measurements, systematic terrain and weather photography, and the particles observed drifting around the spacecraft. The remaining material is administrative: 1965 correspondence about duplicating and analyzing GT-4 film, requests for voice-tape transcripts, typed notes summarizing points from the debriefing, flight-plan charts, and newspaper clippings. The transcript's optical character recognition is poor in places, and several handwritten notes in the file are illegible in the downloaded text.

## Content

### The mission operations report

The Mission Operation Report No. M-913-65-04, issued as a memorandum dated June 1, 1965 from the Associate Administrator for Manned Space Flight, added extravehicular activity, an extravehicular propulsion unit evaluation, and a booster rendezvous exercise to the GT-4 flight plan. The attached base report of May 24, 1965 describes the planned mission in detail. The crew was James A. McDivitt as command pilot and Edward H. White II as pilot, with Frank Borman and James Lovell as backups. The planned flight was 62 revolutions lasting about 97 hours and 50 minutes. Thirteen experiments are listed by their program codes: D-1, D-6, D-8, D-9, M-3, M-4, M-6, MSC-1, MSC-2, MSC-3, MSC-10, S-5, and S-6. The report also describes the G4C suit developed for the EVA, the Gemini Titan II launch vehicle, a program cost table giving total research and development cost as $1,353.9 million, the worldwide tracking network, and primary and secondary recovery zones. These figures are the document's own and are reported here as the document states them.

### The experiment debriefing transcript

The debriefing transcript is organized by recording "belts" (Belts 11 through 19) and consists largely of an extended exchange in which an investigator questions the two crew members about visual and photographic observations. The topics, in the order the file presents them, include the following.

The crew discussed the airglow layer at length: its banded structure, its appearance at twilight, and the color bands at the horizon. The transcript records the investigator's remark that the structured appearance the crew described had reportedly not been seen before; that characterization belongs to the speaker in the transcript and is not adopted here. The crew also reported meteors entering the atmosphere below the spacecraft. White estimated roughly ten to twenty bright meteors, and the transcript makes clear this was not a systematic count, because the crew was not watching continuously. They described the southern lights over Australia, zodiacal light, the appearance of the Sun and Moon, and the difficulty of defining the horizon for navigation purposes.

On the instrument experiments, the crew reported that the MSC-1 electrostatic charge experiment and the MSC-2 and MSC-3 instruments produced no performance the crew could report, while the D-8 radiation experiment was carried out with one-minute readings taken at six positions in the cabin. On photography, the crew discussed MSC-10 and the S-5 and S-6 systematic photography programs, including camera malfunctions that produced black frames and shutter and mirror faults. They recommended that both pilots work together on sequence photography rather than one pilot attempting it alone. They also described particles drifting around the spacecraft, including frozen droplets from urine dumps that the crew called "snowflakes," and the glove that floated out of the cabin during White's EVA. Near the end of the transcript, a speaker sums up the experiment program by saying the experiments showed no anomaly; that assessment is the speaker's, recorded in the transcript.

### Supporting and administrative material

Typed notes headed "Notes from the De-Briefing transcript" summarize points on meteors, the visibility of Venus in a mission photograph (print S-65-34771), zodiacal light, and the condition of the windows. The notes record that Gemini's windows were judged superior to Mercury's, and that White's sleeve smudged McDivitt's window during the EVA, partly removing a silicone film. Flight-plan timeline charts and a table of nominal translational maneuvers follow.

The correspondence section, all dated 1965, concerns the analysis of GT-4 photography. A memorandum of July 2, 1965 from the Deputy Director of the Gemini Program addresses the technical analysis of GT-4 photography. A teletype message of July 16, 1965 from Willis B. Foster to Robert O. Piland requests the release of S-5 70 mm film — spool 3 of magazine 8 — to Leo Childs for resolution measurements on a microsensitometer at the Data Corporation in Dayton, Ohio. A letter approves Dr. F. Saiedy of the U.S. Weather Bureau to conduct a "Spectrophotography of Clouds" experiment. A letter of June 24, 1965 from Karl G. Henize of the Dearborn Observatory at Northwestern University to Dr. Jocelyn Gill apologizes for missing the astronaut debriefing conference. Several memoranda from Gill, dated June 17 and July 14, 1965, request duplicate films, prints, and voice-tape transcripts. A list of film-handling questions is attributed to "Chas. Matheus."

## Errors and anomalies

The Drive filename dates the file to 1967, but every dated item inside it is from 1965, and one routing-slip form is the April 1969 edition of NASA Form 26. The filename's date therefore does not describe the contents and should not be relied on. The file is also not a single debriefing document: it is a bundle containing an operations report, a debriefing transcript, administrative correspondence, charts, and press clippings. The clippings are from the Washington Post of Thursday, August 26, 1965, and they concern Gemini 5, not Gemini 4: they report Cooper and Conrad tracking a Minuteman missile launched from Vandenberg and a water and power problem aboard Gemini 5. Their presence inside a Gemini 4 file is an anomaly of assembly, not of content. The optical character recognition of the transcript section is poor, with typewritten-over pages rendering many words uncertain, and the handwritten notes throughout the file are largely illegible in the downloaded text.
