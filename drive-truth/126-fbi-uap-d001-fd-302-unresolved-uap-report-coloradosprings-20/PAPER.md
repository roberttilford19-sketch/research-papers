## Premise

The form records a Federal Bureau of Investigation interview with a witness to an unresolved unidentified anomalous phenomenon report, in order to preserve the witness's account of a February 2022 sighting near Fort Carson, Colorado. It was produced as an investigative record; the form itself states that it contains neither recommendations nor conclusions of the Bureau.

## Abstract

This Federal Bureau of Investigation FD-302 records a March 2025 interview, conducted by video conference, with one of five soldiers who reported seeing an object over Cheyenne Mountain, Colorado, in early February 2022. According to the account recorded in the form, the witness and four others from his unit were walking toward a building entrance at Fort Carson at about 1:00 or 2:00 in the afternoon, under clear skies, when all five noticed a motionless, silent, matte white or off-white oval object hovering in a low saddle of the mountain. The witness described it as bean-shaped, with a curved indentation on the bottom and a surface covered in intersecting lines or ridges forming an abstract polygon pattern, and estimated its size by comparison with the mountain saddle. The account states that the group watched for approximately three to five minutes, that none of them had a telephone with them, and that when they looked away briefly to decide about retrieving one, the object had vanished. It further records that the five men separately drew what they remembered and found the drawings consistent, that a report was believed to have been made to the Army, and that the witness was contacted by the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office in early 2024 but was not interviewed by it.

## Content Account

The form records an interview conducted in March 2025 by a special agent and a supervisory special agent, with identifying details redacted throughout. The witness's account, as recorded, places the sighting on a workday in early February 2022 at approximately 1:00 or 2:00 p.m. He and four other persons from his unit drove a short distance at Fort Carson, parked, and walked west toward a building entrance, looking toward Cheyenne Mountain. All five, the account states, noticed a strange object hovering just over the mountain in a low point or saddle.

The description recorded is specific: matte white or off-white, apparently non-metallic, oval and horizontal, with a curved indentation on the bottom, characterized by the witness as bean-shaped, completely motionless, and silent. The surface was described as covered in intersecting lines or ridges forming an abstract polygon pattern across the whole object. Its size was estimated at about ten percent of the saddle of the mountain, or about the size of a pool cue at arm's length. The account states the object did not drift, which the witness offered as his reason for not believing it was carried by the wind.

The closing portion of the account records that none of the five had a telephone, that they looked away simultaneously to decide whether one of them should return to the vehicle for one, and that the object was gone when they looked back. It records that they later compared independent drawings, all showing a horizontal white bean-shaped object with abstract crossing lines, that the witness believed an official report was made to the Army, and that he was contacted by the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office in early 2024 and responded, but was never interviewed.

## Errors and Anomalies

No internal contradiction was identified in the account as recorded. Two points are matters of record rather than error: the interview took place roughly three years after the sighting, so the account is a recollection; and this form gives the time as about 1:00 or 2:00 p.m. and the duration as three to five minutes, while companion records of the same incident in this collection give different times and durations, as noted in the limits. Redactions remove all names, the exact interview date, and the locations within Fort Carson.

## Limits

This is one witness's account in one interview form; the other four witnesses' accounts are not in this document. The date is given only as early February 2022, while the companion intelligence analysis in this collection dates the incident to 15 February 2022 and gives a morning time and a duration of 30 to 180 seconds. Those discrepancies between records are stated here and are not resolved by this document. No photographs or sensor data are included.
