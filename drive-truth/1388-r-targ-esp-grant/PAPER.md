## Abstract

This staged assessment describes a collected Public Health Service grant application, form PHS-398, submitted by Stanford Research Institute with Russell Targ, a senior research physicist in its Electronics and Bioengineering Laboratory, as principal investigator and Harold E. Puthoff among the professional personnel. The proposal, titled 'Techniques To Enhance Extraordinary Human Perception', covers a project period from 1 October 1972 to 1 October 1973. Its research narrative argues that the standard card-guessing paradigm works against the ability it tests. Because feedback is delayed until the end of a run of trials, the applicant compares the paradigm to extinction training in learning psychology, and notes the familiar decline of initially high scores to chance with repeated testing. The proposed remedy is an extrasensory perception teaching machine that gives immediate feedback on each trial, together with work on reinforcement schedules, on the reported amplifying effect of Faraday cage shielding, and on hypnosis. The application includes the investigators' biographical sketches and publication lists in laser and plasma physics, administrative and budget pages, an extensive bibliography drawing on the parapsychology literature, and figure captions for a precognition experiment reporting a correlation the applicant presents as statistically significant. This assessment describes the application's argument and contents as a proposal. A proposal states what its authors intended to test and hoped to show; it is not a report of results, and no outcome is claimed here on its behalf.

## Content Account

Sampled sections of the narrative develop the theoretical framing: that science progresses by interaction of theory and data, that the field is trapped in a circle in which weak effects discourage theory and weak theory prevents control, and that raising the level of the effect would open a more fruitful level of investigation. A sampled passage discusses Henry Puharich's privately circulated Faraday cage material, in which breaking the cage's shielding or disconnecting its earth connection reportedly returned subjects' scores to ordinary levels, an effect the application treats as a physical amplification worth systematic study. The closing sampled pages give the significance section, arguing that demonstrating learning on a random target selector would show that the ability can be enhanced.
