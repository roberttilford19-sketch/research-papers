## Premise

The memorandum sets out to derive physical constraints from a reported missile-deflection event off the coast of Yemen. ## Abstract

This memorandum, version 4.2 and dated May 9, 2026, presents Robert Tilford's forensic physics analysis of an event he designates MB-772. As the memorandum presents it, MQ-9 Reaper multi-sensor footage from October 30, 2024, off the coast of Yemen shows an AGM-114 Hellfire missile deflected from a stationary glowing orb without its warhead detonating, its airframe remaining intact through a near-90-degree trajectory reversal, and the orb showing no measurable displacement. From a stated impact energy of approximately 40 kilojoules, the memorandum derives a field stiffness constraint above ten to the eighth newtons per metre and an inertial anchor ratio above ten to the sixth. It organises the argument in tiers: direct observations first, then a table of classical-physics expectations against the derived constraints, then provisional mechanism analysis favouring a passive repulsive boundary layer, offered in both deductive and inductive form. An operational section argues that fragmentation and blast pressure would be nullified by such a boundary and that the useful intelligence would lie in an electromagnetic or gravitational signature shift at the moment of deflection. A closing audit resolution matrix lists four sceptical explanations and the evidence the memorandum says each would require, and the document ends with an action line asking that all MB-772 missile telemetry and associated AEGIS tracks be secured for spectral decomposition.
