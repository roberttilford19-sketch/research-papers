## Abstract

This file contains United States Army staff papers from March and April 1949 concerning an evaluation study of the flying saucer phenomenon. A dispatch memorandum of 22 March 1949, from the Executive Branch of the Plans and Operations Division, records that the evaluation study was prepared at that division's request to determine whether various reports on the subject stemmed from natural phenomena or whether their origin could be traced definitely to the activities of a foreign power, and directs that the division be kept informed if future developments varied with the evaluation placed on the study. A filing form dated 5 April 1949 records the subject as an evaluation study of the phenomenon of flying saucers. A memorandum for record of 5 April 1949 states that the Intelligence Division had submitted, at the Plans and Operations Division's request, a study of the flying saucer phenomenon, and that the study intimated that of all cases investigated there was no foreign nation implication in these flying saucers; the same memorandum directs a comment to the Intelligence Division requesting verification of the accuracy of Walter Winchell's statement of 3 April 1949 concerning flying saucers. A further memorandum for record of 20 April 1949 traces the sequence: reports received by radio in February 1949 prompted the request of 24 February, the study was forwarded on 7 March, and the comment cycle following Winchell's 3 April broadcast produced the Intelligence Division's answer. The evaluation study itself is not reproduced in the extracted text.

## Content Account

The earliest paper in the extraction is an outbound dispatch memorandum dated 22 March 1949 from the Executive Branch, Plans and Operations Division, signed in the extraction by a lieutenant colonel. It states that the evaluation study was prepared at the request of Plans and Operations to determine whether various reports on the subject stemmed from natural phenomena or whether the origin could be traced definitely to the activities of a foreign power. It further states that the Intelligence Division is to keep Plans and Operations informed if future developments in the field are at variance with the evaluation placed on the study, that the Pentagon staff had been informed, and it directs circulation as indicated and then filing.

The filing form, classified under decimal classification P&O 350.05 and dated 5 April 1949, records the subject as the evaluation study of the phenomenon of flying saucers, with an action line referring to a first comment to the Director of Intelligence and a note that no reply was required.

The memorandum for record of 5 April 1949, from the Executive Branch, records that the Intelligence Division had submitted, at the request of Plans and Operations, a study of the flying saucer phenomenon, and that this study intimated that of all cases investigated there was no foreign nation implication in these flying saucers. It records that a staff officer of the Intelligence Division said the division would be glad to take action at the request of Plans and Operations, and the action directed is a comment to the Director of Intelligence requesting verification of the accuracy of Walter Winchell's statement of 3 April 1949 regarding flying saucers.

The memorandum for record of 20 April 1949, by a major of the Executive Branch, gives the fuller chronology. As a result of a number of radio reports in February 1949 on flying saucers, Plans and Operations requested the Intelligence Division on 24 February 1949 to evaluate these phenomena. On 7 March 1949 the Intelligence Division forwarded the attached evaluation. As a result of Walter Winchell's broadcast of 3 April 1949, a comment was sent to the Intelligence Division requesting verification of the accuracy of the broadcast, and a second comment from the Intelligence Division constitutes its answer. The memorandum directs circulation as indicated and then filing, and notes that one staff element was not informed.
