## Abstract

This staged assessment describes a collected fragment of a United States intelligence special report on Soviet bloc science and parapsychology, released in sanitised form under the reference NSA-RDP96X00790R000100030054-6. The surviving text is headed Inclosure D and dated 12 April 1970. It argues that the calibre of researchers in a field predicts how quickly practical applications emerge, notes that a fairly large number of Soviet scientists were investigating aspects of extrasensory perception, and proposes a comparative study to estimate how much time remained before Soviet science produced practical applications from parapsychology. The enclosure reports that Soviet field research aimed at isolating a postulated psi energy claimed significant discoveries in 1969 despite official and scientific opposition, and it singles out filmed experiments with the telekinetic claimant Nelya Mikhailova, also rendered in the text as Kalugina. It relays Western observers' view that the best Soviet bloc work was being done in Bulgaria by Dr. George Lozanov, who is described as enjoying high-level support and as sponsor of the clairvoyant Vanga, said to be a nationalised psychic. Two appendices list Soviet and bloc researchers with brief notes on their work, and a short bibliography of underlying reports dated 1968 and 1969. This assessment treats the enclosure as a period intelligence product whose reports about bloc research are attributed claims, not established findings.

## Content Account

Appendix B lists four underlying items, including a report on opposition to parapsychology research and filmed experiments dated 14 February 1969, and a report on the validity of Mikhailova's claim to telekinetic power dated 29 April 1969.
