## Abstract

This staged assessment describes a collected paper by Dr. Kenneth A. Kress, 'Parapsychology in Intelligence: A Personal Review and Conclusions', released in sanitised form under the CREST reference NSA-RDP96X00790R000100010031-3. Kress writes as a participant: he was involved in many aspects of the Agency's later investigations, and he states that his intent is not historical completeness but a record of data and opinions likely to benefit future evaluators. The paper confines its attention to psychokinetics and remote viewing. It recounts how anecdotal reports reached national security agencies from the Second World War onward, how Agency sponsorship of Stanford Research Institute work developed in the early 1970s, and how sensitivity, fear of ridicule, and congressional scrutiny constrained the programme. Its most detailed episode concerns Pat Price's remote viewing of a Soviet research site designated URDF-3: some descriptions impressed analysts, including an account of a crane, but much of the material was judged wrong or unevaluable, and Kress records the overall result as inconclusive and not operationally useful. Elsewhere the paper describes embassy targeting tests, an outside physicist's more favourable critique, and repeated friction between contractor claims and government advisers over experimental design. Kress concludes that tantalising but incomplete data exist, that parapsychology is not a proven intelligence tool, and that only long-term basic research could settle the question. This assessment attributes every judgement to Kress or to the officers he quotes.
