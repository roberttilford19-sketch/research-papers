## Abstract

This document is a set of compiled notes summarizing a video interview with Ava Glass, who describes working with British intelligence officers. The notes are organized in three successive passes over the same material. The first pass lists five methodologies: a covert vetting process in which a colleague who was herself a spy spent weeks befriending and questioning the speaker before she was cleared to meet other officers; the Prevent approach of thinking like an attacker to identify likely targets such as stadiums, stations, and bus stops, and hardening them with measures such as street closures and bollards; countryside training games in which officers simulated attacks, including explosions at old chemical factories and the kidnapping of a school bus, with soldiers playing the public and indicating casualties; strict anonymity, in which the speaker never learned any officer's real name; and the rule that officers are never left alone on duty. The second pass expands the secrecy material, adding observation as an ingrained habit, the security rationale for non-disclosure, legal obligations, the avoidance of political discussion, and controlled disclosure through whistleblowing channels. The third pass gathers concrete details: blast-proof glass and biometric locks, bollards appearing around infrastructure, the signing of the Official Secrets Act, inter-agency attendance at training games, and the unassuming appearance of real officers. All content is the speaker's account as summarized in the notes.
