## Premise

The file is a small Central Intelligence Agency correspondence packet from October 1958. A private citizen wrote to Director Allen W. Dulles to bring a magazine article about the Dutch psychic Peter Hurkos to the Agency's attention, and an Agency officer replied on the Director's behalf with a brief letter of thanks. ## Abstract

This file contains three connected pieces from October 1958. The first is a letter dated 16 October 1958 to Allen W. Dulles, Director of the Central Intelligence Agency, from Janet Hutchinson, who wrote that every possible resource should be made known to the Agency and enclosed a reprint of an article about Peter Hurkos, a Dutch man credited with extrasensory perception. Hutchinson noted that Hurkos was respected by similar agencies in Europe, had returned to the United States after a summer in Holland, intended to become a citizen, and was working on an autobiography with Emeline K. Paige, the article's author. The second piece is the reply, dated 27 October 1958, written on behalf of Mr. Dulles by an executive officer of the Agency, thanking Hutchinson for her letter and the enclosure and expressing appreciation for her writing. The third and largest piece is the enclosure itself, Paige's Yankee Magazine profile, which recounts anecdotes of Hurkos locating a missing girl's body, predicting oil depths, and recognizing a hospital patient and a nurse under names he could not ordinarily have known, and which presents his claimed abilities, police work in many countries, and scrapbooks of testimonials as matters of record. The file records a courteous acknowledgment, not an Agency evaluation of Hurkos.

## Content account

The incoming letter is dated 16 October 1958 and is headed "PETER HURKOS." Hutchinson tells Dulles that she is sending a reprint of an article concerning Hurkos because she feels every possible resource should be made known to the Central Intelligence Agency. She states that Hurkos is widely known and highly respected by similar agencies in Europe, that he has returned to this country after spending the summer in his native Holland, that he intends to become a United States citizen, and that Miss Emeline K. Paige, who wrote the article, is working with them on Hurkos's autobiography.

The reply is dated 27 October 1958. It is signed by an executive officer whose name is withheld in the released copy under a sanitization marking, writing on behalf of Mr. Dulles. Its whole substance is thanks: the writer thanks Hutchinson for her letter of 16 October and the enclosed article on Peter Hurkos, and says the Agency appreciates her taking the time to write about the matter. A distribution list on the letter shows copies to internal Agency addressees. Nothing in the reply endorses, tests, or comments on Hurkos's claimed abilities.

The enclosed article, Emeline K. Paige's "The Man Who Knows" from Yankee Magazine, occupies the remaining pages. Its editor's note frames the piece cautiously, presenting it for what it is, no more, no less. The article tells a series of stories: Hurkos, telephoned in Florida about a missing Philadelphia girl, directing police to a river near a boathouse where, the article says, the body was found; Hurkos placing stones on a map in a Texas office and giving oil depths, with one depth wrong in the drilling; and an origin story in a Dutch hospital, where Hurkos, recovering from a fall from a ladder and three days in a coma, is said to have addressed a fellow patient by a name that was not his own but matched a driver's license in the man's possession, and to have warned a nurse, whose name he also produced, that she would lose her valise on a train, which the article says she had already done that morning. The magazine presents Hurkos as a large man, six foot three and 228 pounds, a former Dutch house painter living at Glen Cove, Maine, who received as many as 1,200 letters a week, and it repeats a claim that in certain tests an electronic brain put the odds against his perfect score by chance at one in a very large number. All of these are the magazine's claims, reproduced in the file as the enclosure Hutchinson sent; the Agency file itself verifies none of them.
