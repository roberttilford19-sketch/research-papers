## Premise

## Abstract

This four-page intelligence analysis assesses the airborne object observed by five United States Army service members at Fort Carson, Colorado, over Cheyenne Mountain on 15 February 2022. The analysis states, in its subject line and opening assessment, that the object was possibly the backscattering of sunlight, and it expressly assigns that assessment low confidence, citing uncertainty in each witness's field of view, the amount of snow cover, and the exact elevation and amount of cloud cover. It states that no anomalous data or characteristics were recorded or assessed, and that the event did not represent an unknown adversarial capability. Summarizing the witness accounts, it describes an object roughly the size of a large jet, resembling an angular, non-symmetrical potato made of uneven panels, stationary about 300 to 500 feet above the mountain for approximately 30 to 180 seconds, slightly translucent and shimmering white, which suddenly disappeared while the witnesses watched. The analysis then sets out the conditions on which its explanation depends: the sun's position that morning, snow depth on the mountain likely between six and twelve inches, and weather reports indicating partly to mostly cloudy skies despite the witnesses' report of clear skies. It explains that backscattered sunlight reflecting off snow-covered ground could illuminate low-level clouds, which might account for the object's visibility and its sudden disappearance.

## Content Account

The analysis opens with its subject: an airborne object over Cheyenne Mountain in February 2022 was possible backscattering of sunlight. Its summary paragraph, which appears on page three of the extraction, states that the object was observed at 0935 Mountain Time on 15 February 2022, approximately six miles to the west over and slightly behind the Cheyenne Mountain silhouette, for approximately 30 to 180 seconds. It describes the object, according to the five service members' accounts, as roughly the size of a large jet and resembling an angular, non-symmetrical potato made of uneven panels, completely stationary about 300 to 500 feet above Cheyenne Mountain while slowly changing shape, slightly translucent and shimmering white with defined edges and a milky shimmer that reflected sunlight. It records that the witnesses reported clear skies and that, despite keeping their eyes on the object, it suddenly disappeared.

The supporting discussion gives the sun's position at 0945 Mountain Standard Time on 15 February 2022 as an altitude of about 27.5 degrees in the southeast at an azimuth of about 125 degrees, casting shadows toward the northwest. It reports snow depth on Cheyenne Mountain likely ranging from six to twelve inches according to the National Water and Climate Center, and it states that although witnesses reported clear, blue skies, multiple weather reports, including Air Force Weather Agency and Weather Underground reporting, indicated clouds, suggesting partly to mostly cloudy skies that morning. The proposed mechanism is that sunlight backscattering off snow-covered ground could illuminate low-level clouds in the vicinity, accounting for the visibility of the object and its sudden disappearance when the clouds or the sun shifted slightly. Two analyst notes explain altostratus clouds and backscattering. The analysis closes by stating that no aircraft or balloons were noted active in or around Cheyenne Mountain at the time, and it repeats that confidence in the assessment is low, based on uncertainty in the witnesses' fields of view and the exact level of cloud cover.
