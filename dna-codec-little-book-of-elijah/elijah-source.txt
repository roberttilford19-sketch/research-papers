The little book of ELIJAH
oral tradition
Preserved in the Gospel of Barnabas, pages 185–186 (chapter 145)

* words of Elijah IN RED

---

Introduction

I came by this little book the long way around, and the long way is part of the story.

Years ago I was reading the Gospel of Barnabas, and it stopped me in my tracks. Here was a Jesus I had never met in church: fully human, a prophet of the Most High, refusing every attempt to make him into a god. His teaching would not allow it. And in the middle of that book, chapter 145, Jesus is arguing with the Pharisees about who a true Pharisee is — and he quotes a whole little book by Elijah.

---

Introduction

So I went hunting for it. I searched this way, that way, every which way, for years. There was no standalone book to find, no library shelf with its name on it. I was about ready to forget the whole thing. Then one of those half-awake nights came, pen beside me, and it landed on me: I had to back-engineer it. I went back to my only source material — and it was there all along. If it was a poisonous viper, it would have bit me.

---

Introduction

That is the first thing to understand about this little book: there never was a “book” to lose. In those days people actually talked to each other. A rule of life like this lived mouth to ear, father to son, master to disciple, for hundreds of years. Writing it down is the brand-new part. Elijah was head of the sons of the prophets — the schools at Gilgal, Bethel, Jericho, and Mount Carmel — men separated from the world to seek God. His disciple Elisha begged a double portion before the master was taken up, and the old words say a chariot of fire and a whirlwind did the taking. Orders of monks still live in caves and on Carmel in imitation of Elijah to this day. The true Pharisee, in Jesus’ telling, was never the villain of the story. “Pharisee” meant simply: one who seeketh God.

---

Introduction

The second thing: the advice is not museum glass. It works on a man today.

“Guard therefore your senses and guard your heart, because God is not found outside of us, in this world wherein he is hated.” We live in a world engineered to grab our eyes. Elijah’s answer is almost insultingly practical: “When he walketh, let him not look save at his own feet.” Not because the world is beneath you, but because your attention is the gate of your heart, and God is not found out there. He is inside here.

And it is short. Eight sayings and seven daily rules, and you can carry the whole thing in your wallet. I know, because I have — copied out by hand on little cards, red ink for the words of Elijah, carried for years. This printed copy is those cards, with the wording checked against the published text so no word of the master is mine and none is missing.

I am not asking you to settle what scholars argue about the Gospel of Barnabas. I am asking you to read these short pages and see if they don’t hunt you down the way they hunted me. The teachings are the thing. I am only the fellow who went looking, and only an afterthought at the back of this book.

— Robert Tilford, Hays, Kansas

Where This Lives

Jesus, speaking to the Pharisees:

“Elijah, the friend of God, at the prayer of his disciple Elisha, wrote a little book wherein he included all human wisdom with the law of God our Lord.”

Barnabas heard it, and wrote it down.

---

Elijah, servant of God

“to all them that desire to walk with God their creator, writeth this.”

(for so beginneth the little Book)

---

1. Against Learning Much

“Whoso desireth to learn much, they fear God little, because he who feareth God is content to know only that which God willeth.”

---

2. Against Notoriety

“They that seek fair words seek not God, who doth naught but reprove our sins.”

---

3. Against Venturing Outside

“They that desire to seek God, let them shut fast the doors and windows of their house, for the master suffereth not himself to be found outside his house, in a place where he is not loved.”

---

4. God Within Us

“Guard therefore your senses and guard your heart, because God is not found outside of us, in this world wherein he is hated.”

---

5. Mind Your Own Business

“They that wish to do good works, let them attend to their own selves, for it booteth not to gain the whole world and lose one’s own soul.”

---

6. Live Better

“They that wish to teach others, let them live better than others, because nothing can be learned from him who knoweth less than ourselves. How, then, shall the sinner amend his life when he heareth one worse than he teaching him?”

---

7. Flee the Conversations of Men

“They that seek God, let him flee the conversation of men; because Moses being alone upon mount Sinai found him and spake with God, as doth a friend who speaketh with a friend.”

---

8. Seek God First

“Those who seek God once only in the city days shall they come forth where be men of the world, for in one day can be done works for two years in respect to business of him who seeketh God.”

---

Advice On How To Live

WALK
“When he walketh, let him not look save at his own feet.”

TALK
“When he speaketh, let him not speak save that which is necessary.”

---

Advice On How To Live

EAT & THINK
“When they eat, let them rise from the table still hungry; thinking every day not to attain to the next; spending their time as one draweth his breath.”

WEAR
“Let one garment, of the skin of beasts, suffice.”

---

Advice On How To Live

SLEEP
“Let the lump of earth sleep on the naked earth; for every night let two hours of sleep suffice.”

CONDEMN NO ONE
“Let him hate no one save himself; condemn no one save himself.”

PRAYER
“In prayer, let them stand in such fear as if they were at the judgment to come.”

---

Conclusion

“Now do this in the service of God, with the law that God hath given you through Moses, for in such wise shall ye find God that in every time and place ye shall feel that ye are in God and God in you.”

This concludes the Little Book of Elijah

---

About Elijah — a short summary

Not part of the little book — only to say who is speaking.

Elijah stands at the head of the sons of the prophets, the schools at Gilgal, Bethel, Jericho, and Carmel. Elisha, his over-zealous disciple, begged a double portion before the master was taken — and the old words say a chariot of fire and a whirlwind carried him up; some today would use other words for the same light, wind, and ascent.

Orders and monks still live in caves and on Carmel in imitation of Elijah, and the true Pharisee of that day was simply the one who seeketh God. For hundreds of years this little book lived the same way he did: passed mouth to ear, not written down.

---

An Afterthought

I have this little book of Elijah. I read of it in the Gospel of Barnabas, then searched for it high and low, every which way, for years — and it was there all along. If it was a poisonous viper it would have bit me. As far as I can tell it survived hundreds of years in one form: oral. People used to actually talk to each other. I copied it out by hand, card by card. The teachings are the thing; I am only an afterthought. God is not found out there. He is inside here. — R.T.
