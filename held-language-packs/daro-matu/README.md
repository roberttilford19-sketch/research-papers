# Daro-Matu — A Held-Language Research Pack

This pack gathers everything assembled at this desk about Daro-Matu in the course of Robert Tilford's Quran translation project. It is a research pack, not a translation. No Daro-Matu Quran translation exists, and none is claimed here or anywhere in this project. What follows is the record of an honest search: what was looked for, what was found, and the thing that was not found, named plainly so that no one mistakes this shelf for a finished work.

## The language

Daro-Matu (ISO 639-3: dro) is a Melanau language of Central Sarawak, Malaysia, spoken in the Matu and Daro country of the Rajang delta coast. It sits inside the wider Melanau record — and that is precisely its difficulty at this desk: the Melanau record that exists in print belongs mostly to other varieties, above all Mukah, and a dictionary of a sister variety is not a dictionary of this one.

## The base, and why the lane is held

No Daro-Matu dictionary was found in any lawful digital source. What is in hand is partial, and the pack names it exactly. Calvin R. Rensch's *Melanau and the Languages of Central Sarawak* (SIL Electronic Survey Reports 2012-011, SIL International, 2012) treats Matu-Daro explicitly and prints cognate sets with Matu forms throughout — material drawn largely from Grace Chou's 2002 thesis, *A Reconstruction of Proto-Melanau*, whose eleven varieties include Matu; the thesis itself has no lawful digital copy. Behind those stand short historical lists: Sidney H. Ray's 1913 wordlist material and a short ASJP list. The Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka Melanau dictionary that does exist — *Kamus Bahasa Melanau Mukah – Melayu Dewan* — covers Mukah, which is ISO mel, not dro, and must not be relabelled. The online Melanau Ajang–Malay dictionary covers Ajang, Matu's closest sister — a neighbour's dictionary, honestly recorded as such, not this language's base. A volume titled *Kamus Bahasa Melanau Matu Daro* circulates only as an upload of unverified provenance on a document-sharing site; it was excluded, and is noted here only so the desk's successors know the title exists in the wild and was weighed and set aside. No Daro-Matu scripture text was found either, and no God-term is attested in any source searched. Cognate sets and a sister's dictionary cannot ground a scripture draft, and this house does not mislabel. The lane is therefore HELD.

## The open seat

The pack's status can be stated in one line, and it is stated here verbatim so that it travels with the pack wherever the pack goes:

**Open seat: "God-term unverified — community usage not yet located."**

The base carries a second, named condition beside it: the desk's Daro-Matu base is PARTIAL — a survey's cognate sets and two short lists, with the one thesis that treats Matu in depth resting in library holdings only.

## The one road not taken

What would move this lane is a base of its own: Chou's Proto-Melanau thesis read in a library, or a Matu-Daro dictionary if one is ever published — mentions of a Mato-Daro–Malay dictionary project exist in secondary sources, but no published version was found. Those are library roads and another day's decisions. Until a real base is in hand, this pack is the lane's honest resting place: everything known, gathered in one place, with the gap named rather than papered over.
