# Sources — Jah Hut (jah)

Pointers only. This file cites the published sources and catalogues behind the Jah Hut held-language pack; it reproduces none of their text. Anything quoted in the pack's documents is quoted there, briefly, with its source named in the same breath.

## The published Bible

- *Bukuq Haba Agan Jahut* — the complete Bible in Jah Hut, Bible Society of Malaysia, launched January 2024. The desk's attestation of its God-term rests on John 3:16 as printed in the Bible and quoted verbatim in Wikipedia, "Bible translations into the languages of Indonesia and Malaysia" (read 2026-11-17): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_translations_into_the_languages_of_Indonesia_and_Malaysia
- PeopleGroups.org, PG009254 (Jah Hut) — independently lists the Bible Society of Malaysia Jah Hut Bible (video + audio) and points to Bible.com.
- Scripture Earth — Jah Hut page: https://scriptureearth.org — carries no local Jah Hut text files; its only resource is a link onward, "Read, Listen, and View on Bible.is — Bible Society of Malaysia" (checked 2026-11-17).
- Bible Brain (Faith Comes By Hearing) — the Bible.is text store; its public API is key-gated (answered 403, key required, 2026-11-17). The text's machine-readable road runs through this door.
- Faith Comes By Hearing, "Jah Hut Bible" Android app (package org.fcbh.jahbsm.s2.s) — holds the text with synchronized audio; not readable by a text fetch.
- YouVersion / Bible.com — no Jah Hut language page exists (404, checked 2026-11-17).

## The scholarly base

- Diffloth, Gérard. "Jah-Hut, an Austroasiatic Language of Malaysia." In *Southeast Asian Linguistic Studies* Vol. 2, Pacific Linguistics C-42, The Australian National University, 1976, pp. 73–118. DOI: 10.15144/PL-C42.73. Read whole in the open ANU edition (online edition licensed 2015 CC BY-SA 4.0, with permission of PL): https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/253109/1/PL-C42.73.pdf — the pack's scholarly base of record. (The SEAlang addresses earlier records carried for this paper answered 404 on 2026-11-17; the ANU copy is the same paper, lawfully open.)
- Aslian etymological database (Starling) — https://starlingdb.org, database "asliet"; the Jah-Hut column, all 304 records read 2026-11-17.
- SEAlang Mon-Khmer Database — holds only 18 Jah Hut items (Shorto 2006 tradition): http://sealang.net/monkhmer/database/byLanguage.htm
- Sidwell, Paul (2015), Austroasiatic dataset, Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.34092 — carries a ~200-item Jah Hut list sourced to Burenhult's manuscript spreadsheet.
