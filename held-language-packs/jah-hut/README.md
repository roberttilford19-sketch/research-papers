# Jah Hut — A Held-Language Research Pack

This pack gathers everything assembled at this desk about the Jah Hut language in the course of Robert Tilford's Quran translation project, including the gate re-run of 2026-11-17 that changed this lane's ground. It is a research pack, not a translation. No Jah Hut Quran translation exists, and none is claimed here or anywhere in this project. Jah Hut is the one lane among the ten held Malaysia wickets whose God-term seat is closed — and the pack says plainly why the lane is nevertheless still held, and exactly what single step would move it.

## The language

Jah Hut (ISO 639-3: jah) is an Aslian language — Austroasiatic, not Austronesian — spoken by an Orang Asli people of Peninsular Malaysia, in Pahang. Sources put the speaker count around five thousand. Its scholarly record is thin by the record's own account: Gérard Diffloth, whose 1976 grammar sketch is the language's principal published description, wrote that all Jah Hut vocabulary printed before his paper "probably does not exceed two hundred items," and his paper adds glossed examples rather than a dictionary. What changed this lane is not scholarship but Scripture: in January 2024 the Bible Society of Malaysia launched a complete Bible in Jah Hut, *Bukuq Haba Agan Jahut* — the whole canon, translated by a mother-tongue team, in the community's own words.

## The seat — closed

The desk's seat rule is that the God-term must be the term the published translation itself uses, quoted from the text, never inferred and never invented. For Jah Hut that rule is met. John 3:16 as printed in *Bukuq Haba Agan Jahut* reads: "Alaq sayèng lalu jah kay duniyaq doh sampèy Iyeh ok Iwãq Eh hak tunggal, supayaq ne'dec jah hak pechayaq kay Iwãq nin het hengkãk tapiq dapat rés hak ley han neng." The word for God stands at the head of the verse, and the pack states the seat in one line, verbatim, so that it travels with the pack wherever the pack goes:

**Seat: CLOSED — the God-term is *Alaq*, attested in John 3:16 of the published Bible (*Bukuq Haba Agan Jahut*, Bible Society of Malaysia, launched January 2024).**

The same verse attests a small core around the God-term, aligned against the verse's known sense with Diffloth's glosses as the check: *sayèng*, "love"; *duniyaq*, "world" (an Arabic–Malay loan); *Iwãq*, "Son" — Diffloth independently glosses the same root, ʔiwaʔ, "someone's offspring"; *pechayaq*, "believe" (a Malay loan); and *het*, "not." Beyond the verse, the scholarly base's religious shelf is three words deep: *pkaʔ*, "a spirit"; *kmoc*, "ghost"; and the compound *bes kraʔboʔ*, "the food-mixing taboo spirit" — plus *hɔm*, "heart," from the etymological database. Lord, heaven, hell, angel, pray, mercy, prophet, soul, bless, holy, sin and forgive are each NOT ATTESTED in the base in hand, and this pack leaves those seats empty rather than filling them. The sky is attested (*sʔwiʔ*, "skies") and is counted as sky, not as heaven, because a word for the visible sky is not evidence for the Bible's heaven.

## The count, and why the lane is still held

The gate re-run counted the base in hand — Diffloth's paper read whole, the Jah-Hut column of the Aslian etymological database (all 304 records), and the verse's attested words — against the project's 1,887 template keys, by the generous matching rule every landed lane was counted by. The base holds 213 distinct attested meanings, and they ground **163 keys against the floor of roughly 600**. On the base in hand, the gate DOES NOT CLEAR.

But that is not the whole verdict, and the pack states the rest plainly. The commissioned form of the re-run — the published Bible itself as control corpus — CANNOT YET RUN, for one named reason: the text of *Bukuq Haba Agan Jahut*, though published and real, is not in hand in any machine-readable form the desk could lawfully take. Scripture Earth's Jah Hut page carries no local text files; its only resource is a link onward to Bible.is, where the text sits in Faith Comes By Hearing's Bible Brain behind an API key. YouVersion has no Jah Hut language page. The free FCBH "Jah Hut Bible" app holds the text with synchronized audio, but an app's innards are not a page a text fetch can read. The desk obtained one verse verbatim, the full scholarly base, and no bulk Bible text.

So the lane's status is HELD — live candidate. The extraction step is named, and it is the only step missing: the day the Bible text is lawfully in hand — an API key granted, the app package walked by an agent that can fetch files, or a Bible Society of Malaysia digital edition — the same count can be run against the whole Bible, whose 31,000 verses would ground the core list many times over what a 46-page grammar sketch can. Until that day, no translation is claimed, and this pack is the lane's honest resting place: the seat closed, the arithmetic on the record, the blocker named rather than papered over.
