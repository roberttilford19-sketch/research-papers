## Jah Hut — the God-term seat is closed, the count does not clear

The God-term is **Alaq**. John 3:16 as printed reads: "Alaq sayèng lalu jah kay duniyaq doh sampèy Iyeh ok Iwãq Eh hak tunggal, supayaq ne'dec jah hak pechayaq kay Iwãq nin het hengkãk tapiq dapat rés hak ley han neng." The word stands at the head of the verse in the God position, and the desk's seat rule — the term as the published translation itself uses it — is met by the printed Bible's own usage. Genesis 1:1 in Jah Hut could not be reached to add a second verse, so the confirmation rests on this one verse, stated as such; but one verbatim verse of the published Bible is attestation, not inference, and the seat is CLOSED.

The same verse attests, by alignment against its known sense with Diffloth's glosses as the check, a small core around the God-term: **sayèng**, "love" ("Alaq sayèng lalu jah kay duniyaq doh" — God so loved the people of this world); **duniyaq**, "world," an Arabic–Malay loan; **Iwãq**, "Son" ("Iwãq Eh hak tunggal" — His only Son; Diffloth independently glosses the same root, ʔiwaʔ, "someone's offspring"); **pechayaq**, "believe" ("jah hak pechayaq kay Iwãq nin" — the person who believes in that Son), a Malay loan; and **het**, "not," matching Diffloth's hat / hǝt. The rest of the verse is carried unglossed in the companion term list, because no published word-level gloss for those forms was in hand and the desk does not guess glosses.

Beyond the verse, the religious shelf of the scholarly base is three words deep. Diffloth glosses **pkaʔ**, "a spirit," in his phonology section's list of cluster-initial roots ("/p kaQ/ a spirit"); **kmoc**, "ghost," in his comparative vocabulary ("ghost (SB: G18): Jah Hut /kmoc/"); and the compound **bes kraʔboʔ**, glossed in his syntax examples as "the food-mixing taboo spirit." The etymological database adds **hɔm**, "heart." That is the whole of it. Lord, heaven, hell, angel, pray, mercy, prophet, soul, bless, holy, sin and forgive are each NOT ATTESTED in the base in hand: a search of Diffloth's full text for "god" returns nothing at all, and no gloss in either source carries any of those meanings. The sky is attested — **sʔwiʔ**, "skies," in Diffloth's noun lists — and the desk counts it under the template's "sky" key and not under "heaven," because a word for the visible sky is not evidence for the Bible's heaven, and the seat stays honestly empty.

The count itself was run this way. The template key set was taken from the lane lexicon file dict_mbf.py in the Baba Malay lane, whose header states it mirrors "the Bajaw lane engine's 1,887 keys (the Locked English Master's core vocabulary)"; the file was parsed mechanically tonight and holds exactly 1,887 keys. Against those keys the desk matched, generously and by the Kiput precedent (derived forms count for their root concept, plurals for their singular, an attested loan counts as an attested form), every meaning attested in the combined Jah Hut base: the etymological database's Jah-Hut forms, every form Diffloth glosses in the paper read whole, and the verse's attested words. The base holds 213 distinct attested meanings, and they ground **163 of the 1,887 template keys** — 163 against a floor of roughly 600. A machine pass first returned 165; two of those were false positives of the matching rule itself ("is" matched off the pronoun "I," "news" off "new"), were struck by hand, and the corrected figure is the one reported. The full attested list, with its source marked word by word, is in the companion file JAHHUT-TERMS-2026-11-17.md.

So Jah Hut's verdicts are these. God-term seat: CLOSED, on Alaq, from the printed Bible. Gate count on the base in hand: DOES NOT CLEAR — 163 of 600. And the commissioned re-run in its specified form, the Bible text as control corpus: CANNOT YET RUN, and what is missing is exactly one thing — the text of *Bukuq Haba Agan Jahut* in machine-readable form. That text exists; it sits in Bible Brain behind an API key and inside the FCBH app package, and the day it is lawfully in hand — an API key granted, the app package walked by a browser agent, or a Bible Society of Malaysia digital edition — the same count can be run against the whole Bible, whose 31,000 verses would ground the core list many times over what a 46-page grammar sketch can.
