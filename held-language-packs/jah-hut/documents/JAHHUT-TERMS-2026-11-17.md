# Jah Hut (jah) — extracted term list, gate re-run of 2026-11-17

Three sources, all read at the desk on 2026-11-17:

- **ASL** — the Aslian etymological database (Starling, starlingdb.org, database "asliet"), all 304 records read through; the Jah-Hut column.
- **DIF** — Gérard Diffloth, "Jah-Hut, an Austroasiatic Language of Malaysia" (Pacific Linguistics C-42, 1976, pp. 73–118), read whole in the open ANU edition (openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au, online edition licensed CC BY-SA 4.0). The paper is a grammar sketch with no vocabulary appendix; the forms below are the words Diffloth glosses in his examples and phonology lists. A few interlinear glosses in the scanned text were too garbled to read and were left out rather than guessed, so this list is a floor for the paper, not a claim of its last word.
- **JN 3:16** — John 3:16 as printed in the Jah Hut Bible (*Bukuq Haba Agan Jahut*, Bible Society of Malaysia, launched January 2024), quoted verbatim in Wikipedia's "Bible translations into the languages of Indonesia and Malaysia." Word alignments from the verse are marked as alignments (the desk's reading against the verse's known sense, with Diffloth's glosses as the check), not as published glosses.

213 distinct attested meanings in total across the three sources.

## From the printed Bible (John 3:16)

The verse as printed: "Alaq sayèng lalu jah kay duniyaq doh sampèy Iyeh ok Iwãq Eh hak tunggal, supayaq ne'dec jah hak pechayaq kay Iwãq nin het hengkãk tapiq dapat rés hak ley han neng."

| Meaning | Jah Hut form | Note |
|---|---|---|
| God | Alaq | first word of the verse; the Bible's God-term |
| love | sayèng | alignment: "Alaq sayèng lalu jah kay duniyaq doh" — "God so loved the people of this world"; cf. Malay sayang |
| world | duniyaq | Arabic/Malay loan (dunia) |
| Son | Iwãq (Eh = his) | alignment: "Iwãq Eh hak tunggal" — "His only Son"; cf. Diffloth ʔiwaʔ "someone's offspring" |
| believe | pechayaq | alignment: "jah hak pechayaq kay Iwãq nin" — "the person who believes in that Son"; Malay loan (percaya) |
| not | het | in "het hengkãk" — "shall not perish"; cf. Diffloth hat / hǝt "not" |
| people | jah | in the verse; also Diffloth's gloss throughout |

The remainder of the verse (sampèy, Iyeh, ok, supayaq, ne'dec, hengkãk, rés, ley, han neng) is carried unglossed: no published word-level gloss for those forms was in hand, and the desk does not guess. The phrase "dapat rés hak ley han neng" carries the verse's "have eternal life" sense at phrase level only.

## Religious and near-religious terms from Diffloth 1976

| Meaning | Jah Hut form | Where Diffloth gives it |
|---|---|---|
| spirit | pkaʔ | phonology section, in a list of cluster-initial roots: "/p kaQ/ a spirit" |
| ghost | kmoc | affiliation section, comparative vocabulary: "- ghost (SB: G18): Jah Hut /kmoc/" |
| taboo spirit | bes kraʔboʔ | syntax section, example (50): "the food-mixing taboo spirit" |
| heart | hɔm | ASL (see below) |

No word for God, Lord, heaven, hell, angel, prayer, mercy, prophet, soul, blessing, holy, sin or forgiveness is glossed anywhere in Diffloth's paper — a search of the full text for "god" returns nothing, and the desk records each of those seats NOT ATTESTED in this base. Diffloth also glosses one Expressive, /ʔaʃʔhlEp/, as "sound of breathing, of the creation of the world" (phonetics section) — an iconic sound-word, not a creation noun, and it is not counted as one.

Two cautions ride with this list. First, Diffloth's own introduction states that all Jah Hut vocabulary printed before his paper "probably does not exceed two hundred items," and his paper adds glossed examples rather than a dictionary — the thinness of this base is the paper's own account of the record, not the desk's complaint. Second, several forms above are Malay or Temuan loans and are flagged as such where Diffloth flags them; the desk counts an attested loan as an attested form (the Kiput precedent counted the English loan bup, "book," the same way), and the flags keep the count honest.
