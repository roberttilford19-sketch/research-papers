# Grok prompt — Kiput God-term hunt (written 2026-11-17, for Robert to hand-carry)

RESEARCH TASK — Kiput language (ISO 639-3: kyi), Sarawak, Malaysia

You are helping research a translation project. We are preparing a translation into Kiput, a small Berawan–Lower Baram language spoken by roughly 450 people (community ~1,400–2,000) around Long Kiput / Kuala Tutoh on the Baram River, Sarawak. One thing blocks the work: we do not know the word Kiput speakers themselves use for God, and we will not invent one. Your job is to find what the community actually uses. Search only — do not contact anyone.

WHAT WE ALREADY KNOW (do not re-report these as findings):
- The only published description is Robert Blust, "A Short Morphology, Phonology and Vocabulary of Kiput, Sarawak" (Pacific Linguistics 546, 2003), ~931 vocabulary items. It contains NO word for God, heaven, hell, angel, prayer, mercy, prophet, or soul. Its only spirit word is "akaaŋ" = ghost, spirit of the dead.
- Ethnologue catalogues Kiput as "Unwritten"; its whole recorded language development is that one dictionary/grammar.
- Blust's 1971 field notebook (Kaipuleohone RB2-003-B) is the same elicitation the book was made from — same material, not a new source.
- Sunday worship is held in Kiput at Kuala Tutoh (attested in a March 2026 ethnography), and much of the Long Kiput community has been Catholic since the mid-20th century. So a God-term exists in spoken use; it has simply never been found in print we can reach.

FIND, IN THIS ORDER:
1. The Kiput word for God as used by the community itself — in Catholic prayer, hymns, liturgy, or catechism connected to Long Kiput / Kuala Tutoh / the Diocese of Miri: hymn books, prayer books, liturgy sheets, parish or diocese publications, recorded services, community videos or posts in Kiput. Also what Muslim Kiput speakers (e.g., Kampung Benawa) use — Allah, Tuhan, or another term.
2. Any Kiput religious vocabulary from ANY source (thesis, journal, SIL, university repository, PARADISEC, Kaipuleohone, archive.org, Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka): God, Lord, heaven, hell, angel, spirit, pray/prayer, mercy, prophet, soul, church, priest, bless, holy, sin, forgive.
3. Joshua Project lists Kiput Bible translation as "started" but names no text. Find what stands behind that flag: which agency, what portions if any, any published term list.
4. Any Kiput-language printed or recorded material at all beyond Blust: school materials from Sarawak Orang Ulu / Baram-area programs, community-produced texts, materials from the 2025 Adiit Mucu'ut Kiput heritage festival.
5. SUPPORT ONLY — God-terms used in Christian or Islamic contexts in the closest related languages: Belait, Narum, Miriek (Miri), Berawan. Label these clearly as related-language evidence, never as Kiput.

RETURN FORMAT — for every find, give ALL of these:
- The term exactly as written in the source (copy the spelling letter for letter)
- A verbatim quote of the line or entry it appears in
- The source: title, author, year, publisher or repository, and a URL or catalogue number where one exists
- One label: ATTESTED IN KIPUT / RELATED LANGUAGE / MALAY AS USED BY KIPUT SPEAKERS / UNVERIFIED

RULES:
- Never guess, construct, or "reconstruct" a God-term. If a source does not state it, it does not count.
- Report negative results too: name the catalogues and sites you searched where nothing was found. A documented empty shelf is a useful answer.
- If you find nothing for item 1, say so plainly rather than filling the gap with related-language terms.
