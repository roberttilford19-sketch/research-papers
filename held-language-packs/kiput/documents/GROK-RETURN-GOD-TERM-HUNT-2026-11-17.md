**Research findings: Kiput word for God**

No attested Kiput-language term for God (or equivalent religious terms) was located in any accessible published or digital source. The community is known to hold Sunday worship in Kiput at Kuala Tutoh and to have been Catholic since the mid-20th century, but the actual spoken term has not appeared in print or recording that can be reached through public search.

### 1. Kiput community usage (Catholic or Muslim)

**No attested term found.**

- Searched: Diocese of Miri materials, Borneo Post and Utusan Borneo coverage of Kuala Tutoh and Long Kiput events, Joshua Project, Global Recordings Network, YouTube community videos, festival references (Adiit Mucu'ut), and general web results for Kiput + Tuhan / Allah / God / doa / hymn / liturgy.
- Result: Multiple sources confirm Catholic worship and prayer sessions at Kuala Tutoh (e.g., Diocese of Miri blog on the 106th anniversary; Borneo Post 2009 and 2017 reports of Christmas prayer sessions and church events). None quote or spell any Kiput-language term for God.
- Muslim communities (Kampung Benawa, Lubok Nibong) are documented, but no Kiput-specific term is given; sources simply note conversion to Islam and use of Malay cultural/religious practice.

### 2. Any Kiput religious vocabulary

**Only the previously known item:**

- Term: **akaaŋ**
  Verbatim: "akaaŋ = ghost, spirit of the dead"
  Source: Robert Blust, *A Short Morphology, Phonology and Vocabulary of Kiput, Sarawak*, Pacific Linguistics 546, 2003.
  Label: **ATTESTED IN KIPUT**

No other religious vocabulary (heaven, hell, angel, prayer, mercy, prophet, soul, church, priest, bless, holy, sin, forgive) appears in Blust or in any other located source.
