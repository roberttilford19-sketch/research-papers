# MALAYSIA WICKETS — BASE HUNT (2026-11-17)

**Commission:** for each of the 20 sticky-wicket lanes of the Malaysia 39 wave (BUILD-LOG.md), find where the lane's own named published lexical base — dictionary / grammar / corpus — can lawfully be obtained. Lawful sources only: archive.org, SIL Language & Culture Archives, Pacific Linguistics / ANU Open Access (incl. SEAlang), university repositories, Google Books (preview only), national-library and Badan Bahasa digital collections, and digital scripture repositories where a Bible corpus is the base. No purchases made, no pirate sources counted (Scribd / DocsLib / academia.edu mirrors encountered were excluded and are flagged where they were the only trace).

**Headline:** 11 of the 20 bases are lawfully in hand digitally today; 4 are partially in hand; 2 are library-only; 1 is purchase-only; 2 were not found in any lawful source. The single richest vein is the Pacific Linguistics open-access programme at ANU (2015 CC BY-SA online editions) — it clears Jahai, Kristang (grammar *and* dictionary), and Kiput outright, and carries the Sabah survey chapters for Ida'an and Tidong.

**Corrections surfaced by the hunt (they change lane records, not just access):**
1. **Kristang:** the dictionary is **Baxter & de Silva (2004)**, not "Baxter & de Kleine" — no Baxter & de Kleine Kristang work exists.
2. **Berawan:** the build log's "West Berawan (zbc)" conflates two codes — **zbc = Central Berawan** (Batu Belah, Long Teru); **zbw = West Berawan** (Long Terawan). Scripture portions and most digital wordlists attach to zbw; Blust's open notebooks attach to zbc lects. Any Berawan source must be checked by village/lect name, never by "West/East" alone (Burkhardt 2014 uses yet another labelling).
3. **Tidong:** ISO `tid` is the older inclusive code; the current split is **itd** (Nunukan/Southern Tidung — the Nonukan side) and **ntd** (Northern Tidung/Sesayap).
4. **Penan:** Sercombe's dictionary work is on **Eastern** Penan (Sukang/Long Lamai), not mainly Western as the lane record assumed.
5. **Semelai:** no standalone Kruspe Semelai dictionary exists — her published dictionary is Mah Meri (2010); the Semelai lexical base is the grammar's vocabulary appendix and its SEAlang extraction.
6. **Temiar:** the dictionary is **Nathalie Means** (ed. Gordon P. Means), Hamline University Press, 1998 (some catalogues/sales pages give 1999).

## 1. Jah Hut (jah) — PARTIAL

The named Diffloth base is in hand, but the hunt's finding is that the wicket here is existence, not access: no Jah Hut dictionary of substance exists in any lawful source checked.

- **In hand:** Gérard Diffloth, "Jah-Hut, an Austroasiatic Language of Malaysia," in *Southeast Asian Linguistic Studies* Vol. 2, Pacific Linguistics C-42, ANU, 1976, pp. 73–118 (DOI 10.15144/PL-C42.73). Full text, CC BY-SA 4.0 online edition: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/253109/1/PL-C42.73.pdf — a grammar sketch with scattered vocabulary; Diffloth himself notes total printed Jah Hut vocabulary before his work "does not exceed two hundred items."
- **Fragments:** SEAlang Mon-Khmer Database holds only **18 Jah Hut items** (Shorto 2006 tradition): http://sealang.net/monkhmer/database/byLanguage.htm · Sidwell 2015's Austroasiatic dataset (Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.34092) carries a ~200-item Jah Hut list sourced to Burenhult's manuscript spreadsheet. RWAAI (Lund) holds a Jah Hut collection record; item-level access unverified.
- **Not found:** any DBP "Kamus Jah Hut" or other published Jah Hut dictionary/glossary — targeted Malay and English searches returned none.

## 2. Jahai (jhi) — IN HAND DIGITALLY

- **Named base:** Niclas Burenhult, *A Grammar of Jahai*, Pacific Linguistics 566, ANU, 2005 (ISBN 9780858835542; DOI 10.15144/PL-566). Full PDF, CC BY-SA 4.0 online edition, ANU Open Research: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/146729/1/PL-566.pdf
- **Bonus machine-usable form:** SEAlang Mon-Khmer Database carries a **1,780-item Jahai lexicon** extracted from Burenhult 2005: http://sealang.net/monkhmer/database/byLanguage.htm
- Google Books is snippet-only (PARTIAL, not needed). The wicket clears on the ANU PDF alone.

## 3. Kristang (mcm) — IN HAND DIGITALLY

The strongest lane of the twenty: both named works are lawfully downloadable in full from ANU.

- **Grammar:** Alan N. Baxter, *A Grammar of Kristang (Malacca Creole Portuguese)*, Pacific Linguistics B-95, ANU, 1988 (DOI 10.15144/PL-B95). Full PDF, CC BY-SA 4.0: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/92f5478e-a864-46a5-be89-4e0bcc4bb662/full
- **Dictionary:** Alan N. Baxter & **Patrick de Silva**, *A Dictionary of Kristang (Malacca Creole Portuguese)–English, with an English–Kristang finderlist*, Pacific Linguistics 564, ANU, 2004 (DOI 10.15144/PL-564; ~2,429 words). Full PDF, CC BY-SA 4.0: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/146269/1/PL-564.pdf
- **Supplement:** the Pinchah Kristang / Open Kristang Wordnet (Kodrah Kristang project) collates Baxter & de Silva into an open online dictionary; the project is dormant since 2023 and the live endpoint was not verified in this pass — a documented supplement, not a verified source.

## 4. Mah Meri (mhe) — LIBRARY-ONLY

- **Named base:** Nicole Kruspe, *A Dictionary of Mah Meri as Spoken at Bukit Bangkong*, Oceanic Linguistics Special Publication 36, University of Hawai'i Press, 2010 (~4,000 entries, Mah Meri–English–Malay). Paper ISBN 978-0-8248-3493-7.
- **No lawful open full copy found.** archive.org holds it as a **controlled-lending** item only ("Access-restricted-item: true" — borrow with an IA account, not a download): https://archive.org/details/dictionaryofmahm00nico — class PARTIAL by that route. The eBook sits behind JSTOR Books / Project MUSE / De Gruyter institutional authentication (LIBRARY-ONLY). Print is in print from UH Press at $40 paper (report only; no purchase made): https://uhpress.hawaii.edu/title/a-dictionary-of-mah-meri-as-spoken-at-bukit-bangkong/
- SEAlang lists a "Betise' (Mah Meri, Besisi)" lexicon but no Kruspe dictionary text; ELAR (SOAS) holds Kruspe Mah Meri documentation materials — a corpus lead for a follow-up pass, not the dictionary. No DBP Mah Meri dictionary found.

## 5. Semelai (sza) — PARTIAL

- **In hand:** SEAlang Mon-Khmer Database carries **"Semelai, all 1336 items (Kru2004)"** — the searchable digital extraction of Kruspe's Semelai vocabulary: http://sealang.net/monkhmer/database/byBranchAndLang.htm
- **The grammar itself is not lawfully free:** Nicole Kruspe, *A Grammar of Semelai*, Cambridge University Press, 2004 (the Semelai–English Vocabulary is its appendix, p. 448 ff.). Google Books gives a limited preview only; Cambridge Core is paywall/institutional; no archive.org copy found. Class for the book: PARTIAL (preview) / LIBRARY-ONLY / PURCHASE-ONLY.
- Kruspe's field data are archived in RWAAI (Lund); the collection is a recordings/texts lead, not a verified dictionary download. No standalone Kruspe Semelai dictionary exists (see correction 5).

## 6. Temiar (tea) — IN HAND DIGITALLY

- **Named base:** Nathalie Means (ed. Gordon P. Means), *Temiar–English, English–Temiar Dictionary*, Hamline University Press, 1998 (2,600+ root words, 3,800+ derivatives, plus a basic grammar guide). Full text on archive.org via the Rosetta Project: https://archive.org/stream/rosettaproject_tea_ortho-1/rosettaproject_tea_ortho-1_djvu.txt
- **Also in hand:** SEAlang extraction of the same dictionary — **2,736 items**: http://sealang.net/monkhmer/database/byBranchAndLang.htm · Iskandar Carey, *Tengleq Kui Serok: A Study of the Temiar Language*, DBP Kuala Lumpur, 1961 (~1,000-word glossary), Rosetta copies on archive.org (e.g. https://archive.org/details/rosettaproject_tea_vertxt-1 ).
- Geoffrey Benjamin's Temiar grammar outline (1976) is LIBRARY-ONLY. No DBP "Kamus Temiar" exists as such — Carey 1961 is the DBP Temiar book. A newer Kelantan Temiar dictionary (Quinton, 5,000+ definitions) is PURCHASE-ONLY.

## 7. Bahau (bhv) — IN HAND DIGITALLY

Unblocked by a newer and better base than the survey wordlist the lane was stopped on.

- **Named base, in hand:** *Kamus Bahasa Bahau — Bahasa Indonesia*, Kantor Bahasa Provinsi Kalimantan Timur (Badan Bahasa), Samarinda, Edisi Pertama, July 2023, xiv + 164 pp., ISBN 978-623-194-589-1. Full PDF on the official Balai/Kantor Bahasa Kalimantan Timur site: https://balaibahasakaltim.kemendikdasmen.go.id/file/kamus/KamusBahau-Indonesia.pdf — covers Bahau of Mahakam Ulu and Kutai Barat, with word class, phonetics, and example sentences.
- **Supplement (PARTIAL):** ABVD (MPI) Bahau wordlist, 84 filled items from 2020 fieldwork: https://abvd.eva.mpg.de/austronesian/language.php?id=1705&sort=cognacy
- Negative results recorded: no SIL Bahau lexical resource (OLAC holds only the Glottolog page for bhv); no Perpusnas or older Badan Bahasa Bahau volume found; no archive.org copy. The 2006 Indonesian *Kamus Bahasa Kayan* (Tanjungpura) is LIBRARY-ONLY and is Kayan, not Bahau — cross-check only.

## 8. Kayan Baram (kys) — IN HAND DIGITALLY

## 12. Berawan (zbc lane; zbc = Central, zbw = West) — PURCHASE-ONLY

- **The dictionary proper:** Jürgen M. Burkhardt, Jey L. - **Scripture:** zbc — NOT FOUND (none reported). zbw — portions dated 2005–2011 exist in the record, but **no lawful digital text was found** on ebible.org, Bible.is, YouVersion, or Scripture Earth.

## 15. Ida'an (dbj) — IN HAND DIGITALLY

Stronger than the named base: a full published grammar with a 2,300+ word list is lawfully free, on top of the SIL survey material.

- **Primary base:** Nelleke E. Goudswaard, *The Begak (Ida'an) Language of Sabah*, LOT Dissertation Series 107, Utrecht, 2005 (PhD, Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam). Full-text PDF, free from LOT: https://www.lotpublications.nl/Documents/107_fulltext.pdf — complete grammar, texts, and a wordlist of over 2,300 words. Caveat: it describes the **Begak** dialect (Ulu Tungku, Lahad Datu); ch. 1.1.4 covers Ida'an–Begak differences.
- **Named SIL survey base:** "Ida'an (dbj) wordlist – Sapagaya LD 79," SIL Archives entry 80367 (15 pp., elicited Sapagaya, Lahad Datu, 1979; draft, posted as-is): https://www.sil.org/resources/archives/80367 · John E. - Print-only items recorded (LIBRARY-ONLY): the 1998 Ida'an picture dictionary (*Kamus akay gambar*), the 1989 trilingual phrase book, Moody 1993 on Ida'an phonemics (Sabah Museum Monograph 4).
