## The language

Belait (ISO 639-3: beg) belongs to the Berawan–Lower Baram branch of the North Sarawak languages. Its centre is Brunei — the Belait and Tutong districts — with speakers across the border in northern Sarawak. It is a distinct North Sarawak language, however Malay-influenced its speakers' daily life, and its documentation has been made almost entirely from the Brunei side, by Brunei-side scholars. That geography is the shape of this lane's problem: the Malaysia-side base is small, and the real record sits in Brunei's institutions.

## The base, and why the lane is held

The named base is Noor Alifah Abdullah's *Struktur Bahasa Belait* — a Universiti Brunei Darussalam thesis of 1992, published as a book by Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka Brunei in 2004. It exists in print. No lawful digital copy of it was found anywhere checked: not in the UBD repository, not on archive.org, not in the endangered-language archives. Adrian Clynes's standard sketch of Belait (in Adelaar & Himmelmann, *The Austronesian Languages of Asia and Madagascar*, Routledge, 2005) is likewise purchase-or-library only. What is in hand digitally is partial and honest about its limits: Holly Drayton's ELAR collection of Lower Baram varieties — more than thirty hours of Belait audio-visual data, some sixteen hours transcribed and five glossed, under item-level access conditions — together with glossed Belait data in Clynes (2012) and Drayton (2025), and a comparative wordlist of some 211 items in the Austronesian Basic Vocabulary Database. Fragments and field recordings are not a lexicon a scripture draft can stand on. And across every source searched, no Belait God-term and no Belait scripture portion of any kind was located. A draft built today would rest on another language's lexicon and would have to invent its God-word, and this house does not invent, and does not mislabel. The lane is therefore HELD.

## The open seat

The pack's status can be stated in one line, and it is stated here verbatim so that it travels with the pack wherever the pack goes:

**Open seat: "God-term unverified — community usage not yet located."**

The base carries a second, named condition beside it: the desk's Belait base is LIBRARY-ONLY. One development is recorded here because it changes the lane's future: a Belait–Malay dictionary — *Kamus dialek Belait – Melayu*, Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka Brunei, first edition 2025 (ISBN 9789998400832) — now exists in print, found by catalogue record in an independent research return after the desk's own hunt had closed. It is not in hand, and this pack does not pretend otherwise; but a modern dictionary exists in the world to be acquired, which was not known when the lane was first held.

## The one road not taken

The roads to a Belait base are a library road — the 2004 *Struktur Bahasa Belait* and the 2025 *Kamus* in Brunei-side or research-library holdings — and, for the dictionary, a purchase. Spending is Robert Tilford's decision alone, and it has not been taken. Until a base is in hand by one of those roads, this pack is the lane's honest resting place: everything known, gathered in one place, with the gap named rather than papered over.
