**Berawan religious vocabulary — exploration results**

Berawan is the closest major relative of Kiput (both inside Berawan–Lower Baram). Documentation is better than for Kiput, but still limited on Christian/Islamic terminology. The main sources are ethnographic rather than lexicographic.

### Key sources

| Source | Type | Religious content |
|--------|------|-------------------|
| Peter Metcalf, *Where Are You, Spirits: Style and Theme in Berawan Prayer* (Smithsonian, 1989) | Full study of traditional prayer | Multiple prayer texts with Berawan terms |
| Peter Metcalf, *A Borneo Journey into Death* (1982) | Funeral rites and eschatology | Soul/spirit terminology |
| Burkhardt, Burkhardt & Ang, *Comparative Berawan Glossary* (UM Press, 2023) | Modern comparative lexicon (~1,200 items × 4 lects) | Focus on flora, fauna, traditional lifestyle; limited religious material |
| Earlier wordlists (Ray 1913, Proctor 1977–78, Blust) | Short vocabularies | Minimal religious coverage |

### Attested traditional religious terms (from Metcalf)

These are traditional (pre-Christian/Islamic) terms, clearly labeled as such:

- **bili / bill / bilis**
  Glossed consistently by Metcalf as "spirit."
  Appears frequently in prayer formulas (e.g., "bili sadt" = spirits of the grandfathers/ancestors).
  Source: Metcalf 1989 and 1982.
  Label: **ATTESTED IN BERAWAN** (traditional)

- **papi'**
  The genre term for "prayer" itself (the spoken invocation style).
  Source: Metcalf 1989.
  Label: **ATTESTED IN BERAWAN**

- **sadt**
  Grandfather / ancestor (used in ritual address).
  Source: Metcalf 1982.
  Label: **ATTESTED IN BERAWAN**

- **ukun, dupun / tepun**
  Terms for ancestors / forefathers (some borrowed from Kenyah).
  Source: Metcalf 1982.
  Label: **ATTESTED IN BERAWAN**

No full native monotheistic "God" term equivalent to Tuhan/Allah is attested in the published prayer texts or glossaries reviewed. Traditional Berawan prayer addresses multiple ancestral and place spirits rather than a single high God.

### Modern religious terminology

- No published Christian or Islamic term list for any Berawan lect was located.
- The 2023 *Comparative Berawan Glossary* prioritizes endangered traditional vocabulary (flora, fauna, daily life) and does not appear to include a dedicated religious section with God/Lord/heaven terms.
- Berawan communities have experienced both Christian and Muslim influence (similar to Kiput), but the spoken modern terms have not entered accessible published sources.

### Negative results

- No Berawan Bible portions, hymnals, or catechisms located.
- Joshua Project and related mission databases list Berawan varieties with limited or no scripture resources.
- Searches of academic repositories, regional news, and language archives returned no additional religious lexicon beyond Metcalf's traditional prayer material.

### Summary

Berawan has better-documented **traditional** spirit and prayer vocabulary than Kiput (especially *bili* "spirit" and the *papi'* prayer genre), thanks to Metcalf's work. However, like Kiput, there is still no attested community term for the monotheistic God (Christian or Muslim) in reachable published sources. The 2023 comparative glossary is a useful modern resource but is not focused on religious terminology.

**Next practical step**: The *Comparative Berawan Glossary* (2023) and Metcalf's prayer texts are the highest-value printed sources. Direct community consultation remains the only reliable route for the contemporary God-term, exactly as with Kiput.
