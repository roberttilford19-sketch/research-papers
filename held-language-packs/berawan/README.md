# Berawan — A Held-Language Research Pack

This pack gathers everything assembled at this desk about the Berawan languages in the course of Robert Tilford's Quran translation project. It is a research pack, not a translation. No Berawan Quran translation exists, and none is claimed here or anywhere in this project. What follows is the record of an honest search: what was looked for, what was found, and the thing that was not found, named plainly so that no one mistakes this shelf for a finished work.

## The language

Berawan (ISO 639-3: zbc, Central Berawan; zbw, West Berawan; with Long Jegan under zbe) belongs to the Berawan–Lower Baram branch of the North Sarawak languages, spoken along the Baram and Tinjar rivers in Sarawak, Malaysia, in four lects: Batu Belah and Long Teru (Central, zbc), Long Jegan (zbe), and Long Terawan (West, zbw). Berawan is the closest major relative of Kiput, whose held pack stands beside this one. A correction of record rides with the lane: it was opened in the Malaysia wave as "West Berawan (zbc)," but zbc is Central Berawan and zbw is West — the portions and wordlists split across the codes, and any future Berawan work must name its lect from the hunt file first, never by "West" or "East" alone.

## The base, and why the lane is held

The dictionary proper exists and is recent: Jürgen M. Burkhardt, Jey L. Burkhardt & Ang Lay Hoon, *Comparative Berawan Glossary / Glosari Komparatif Bahasa Berawan* (Penerbit UM, University of Malaya Press, 2023) — some 4,800 items, roughly 1,200 per lect across all four lects, in a unified community orthography. It is the dictionary base for zbc and zbw alike, and it is PURCHASE-ONLY: it was out of stock at the seller checked, it has not been bought, and a purchase is a spending decision that belongs to Robert Tilford alone. What is in hand digitally is partial: open-access UNIMAS journal papers, including the unified-orthography paper (2020) and a 2025 glossary paper reproducing an excerpt; a 216-item ABVD list for Long Terawan (zbw); scattered Austronesian Comparative Dictionary entries; and Blust's 1971 field notebooks at Kaipuleohone, which carry wordlists for the zbc lects. On scripture: none was found for zbc, and although portions dated 2005–2011 stand in the record for zbw, no lawful digital text of them was found on any scripture platform checked. The religious register is documented better than Kiput's — and what it documents is the traditional one. Peter Metcalf's ethnographies record *bili*, "spirit"; *papi'*, the genre term for prayer itself; *sadt*, grandfather and ancestor; *ukun*, *dupun/tepun*, the ancestors. Traditional Berawan prayer addresses many ancestral and place spirits, and no monotheistic God-term — no native equivalent of Tuhan or Allah in community use — is attested in any published source reached. A traditional spirit-word is not a God-word, and this house does not promote one into the seat. The lane is therefore HELD.

## The open seat

The pack's status can be stated in one line, and it is stated here verbatim so that it travels with the pack wherever the pack goes:

**Open seat: "God-term unverified — community usage not yet located."**

The base carries a second, named condition beside it: the desk's Berawan base is PURCHASE-ONLY — the 2023 comparative glossary is the dictionary of record, and it has not been purchased.

## The one road not taken

The road here is a purchase, and it is named as what it is: the Burkhardt glossary, bought when and if Robert Tilford decides the spending. Community consultation — the only reliable route to the contemporary God-term, here as with Kiput — is outreach, likewise his call alone. Neither has been taken. Until one is, this pack is the lane's honest resting place: everything known, gathered in one place, with the gap named rather than papered over.
