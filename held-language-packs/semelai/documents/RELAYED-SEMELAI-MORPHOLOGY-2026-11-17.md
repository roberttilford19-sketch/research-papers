**Semelai verbal morphology**
(based primarily on Nicole Kruspe, *A Grammar of Semelai*, Cambridge University Press, 2004)

Semelai has one of the most thoroughly described verbal systems in the Aslian branch. Its morphology is notable for the coexistence of two distinct systems: an inherited non-concatenative Austroasiatic system and a concatenative system borrowed from Malay.

### Dual morphological systems

| System | Origin | Formal character | Typical functions |
|--------|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| Non-concatenative | Inherited Mon-Khmer/Aslian | Prefixes or infixes whose placement depends on root syllable count; includes coda copy and template reduplication | Imperfective, causative, comparative, intensive |
| Concatenative | Borrowed from Malay | Fully specified prefixes, one suffix, one circumfix; allomorphy by syllable weight | Middle voice, collective, imperfective, causative, applicative/iterative, happenstance |

The two systems operate side-by-side and can apply to both native and Malay-derived roots.

### Non-concatenative (inherited) processes

- **Coda copy / imperfective**: Copies the final consonant of the root into a preceding position. Marks imperfective/progressive aspect, especially on transitive verbs.
  Example pattern: root → C(final)-root.
- **Causative markers**: Several forms including *par-*, *tar-*, and infix *-r-*.
  Choice and placement depend on the root's syllabic structure.
- **Comparative**: *raʔ-* (prefix on monosyllables) or infix on disyllables.
  Example: *sey* 'be thin' → *raʔ-sey* 'be thinner'; longer roots take internal placement.
- **Intensive**: Light-syllable reduplication.

Placement rule (shared with other Aslian): the affix attaches to the left edge of the prosodic head (stressed final syllable). On monosyllabic roots it appears as a prefix; on disyllabic roots it is usually infixed.

### Concatenative (Malay-derived) processes

- *br- / b-* — middle voice (also used on nouns for 'have X').
- *p-* — causative (alternative to the native causative markers).
- *m(N)-* — imperfective (especially with certain roots or loans).
- *tr-* — happenstance ('happen to V').
- *-(ʔ)iʔ* — applicative with intransitives; iterative with transitives.
- *b-…-(ʔ)an* — collective activity (circumfix).
- *-an* — nominalizer (less frequent on verbs).

Allomorphy is conditioned by the number of syllables in the base: monosyllabic roots take heavy-syllable allomorphs; disyllabic roots take light-syllable allomorphs.

### Verb structure and constraints

- Minimal form: bare root.
- Maximal form: root + up to two derivational affixes + peripheral clitics.
- Trivalent verbs generally do not undergo further valency-changing derivation.
- Stative intransitive verbs (including many property and odour terms) participate in the same derivational processes as other intransitives.
- Serial verb constructions (especially manner and motion) are common and supplement morphological marking.
- Direct quotation is the primary strategy for reporting speech; it is introduced by a quotative of verbal origin.

### Summary of major verbal categories marked morphologically

| Category | Primary markers | Notes |
|----------|-----------------|-------|
| Imperfective / progressive | Coda copy; *m(N)-* | Both native and borrowed strategies |
| Causative | Native *par-/tar-/-r-*; borrowed *p-* | Multiple options coexist |
| Middle | *br-/b-* | Borrowed |
| Applicative / iterative | *-(ʔ)iʔ* | Borrowed; dual function |
| Collective | *b-…-an* | Borrowed circumfix |
| Comparative | *raʔ- / -raʔ-* | Native |
| Happenstance | *tr-* | Borrowed |

Semelai verbal morphology is therefore a clear case of contact-induced dual systems: the older Austroasiatic non-concatenative patterns remain productive for aspect and some valency changes, while Malay-derived concatenative affixes have been fully integrated and now apply productively across the lexicon.

**Primary source**: Kruspe, N. (2004). *A Grammar of Semelai*. Cambridge University Press (especially Chapters 3 and 5). Secondary summaries appear in reviews and comparative Aslian studies.
