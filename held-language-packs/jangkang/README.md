# Jangkang — A Held-Language Research Pack

This pack gathers everything assembled at this desk about the Jangkang language in the course of Robert Tilford's Quran translation project. It is a research pack, not a translation. No Jangkang Quran translation exists, and none is claimed here or anywhere in this project. What follows is the record of an honest search: what was looked for, what was found, and the thing that was not found, named plainly so that no one mistakes this shelf for a finished work.

## The language

Jangkang (ISO 639-3: djo) is a Land Dayak language whose main base lies on the Kalimantan side — West Kalimantan, Indonesia — with a border community on the Malaysia side. It entered the Malaysia wave as a border lane, and its record, such as it is, lives with the Indonesian institutions. That record proved to be nearly empty.

## The base, and why the lane is held

No Jangkang lexical resource was found in any lawful source searched, and the searches were the thorough kind: Badan Bahasa and the Balai Bahasa Kalimantan Barat lists, Perpusnas-style national catalogues, SIL Indonesia, archive.org, Google Books, SEAlang, WorldCat. No *Kamus Bahasa Jangkang*, no *Struktur Bahasa Jangkang*, no Balai Bahasa volume of any kind surfaced. OLAC's page for the language lists only Glottolog and Ethnologue as its descriptions — no lexical resource at all. What exists is fragments from university journals: Universitas Tanjungpura wordlists, among them 41 Bokidoh Dayak Jangkang rice terms with forms and meanings, published open access — a genuine fragment, honestly recorded, and forty-one rice terms are not a lexicon. On scripture the record is equally plain: WorldBibles states that the Bible is not yet translated into Jangkang; Joshua Project records translation "Started" with portions "None Reported"; only Global Recordings Network audio teaching exists. No God-term is attested in any source reached. There is no base to ground a draft on, and no neighbouring cluster's lexicon may be borrowed under this language's name. The lane is therefore HELD — like Uma' Lasan, held at the most basic level: the base does not yet exist to be obtained.

## The one road not taken

There is nothing here to buy, borrow, or download; the roads that exist are the slow ones — an Indonesian university or Balai Bahasa publication coming into being, or documentation work on the Kalimantan side reaching print. If a Jangkang dictionary or grammar ever surfaces in a catalogue, this pack is where its arrival will be recorded and weighed. Until then, this pack is the lane's honest resting place: everything known, gathered in one place, with the gap named rather than papered over.
