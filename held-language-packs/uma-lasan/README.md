# Uma' Lasan — A Held-Language Research Pack

This pack gathers everything assembled at this desk about the Uma' Lasan language in the course of Robert Tilford's Quran translation project. It is a research pack, not a translation. No Uma' Lasan Quran translation exists, and none is claimed here or anywhere in this project. What follows is the record of an honest search: what was looked for, what was found, and the thing that was not found, named plainly so that no one mistakes this shelf for a finished work.

## The language

Uma' Lasan (ISO 639-3: xky) is a Kenyah-cluster language of North Sarawak, Malaysia. Ethnologue lists it as "Unwritten" and records some 80 percent lexical similarity to the Badeng and Lepu Kulit dialects of Mainstream Kenyah (xkl) — a close kinship that matters to this lane's future, and that does not make the neighbour's lexicon this language's base.

## The base, and why the lane is held

This is the emptiest shelf in the wave, and the pack says so without dressing it up. No Uma' Lasan dictionary, grammar, or scripture text exists in any lawful source found — not in the SIL archives, not in the Glottolog bibliography, not in the Malaysian repositories, not in PARADISEC or Kaipuleohone, not on archive.org, SEAlang, or WorldCat. What exists is fragments. Sidney H. Ray's 1913 comparative lists in "The Languages of Borneo" run to 211 words and explicitly include Uma' Lasan, under the older spelling "Kinjin/Kindjin." Antonia Soriente's 2003 classification of the Kenyah languages is the only modern source treating Uma' Lasan with wordlist data, and it is library-only. Global Recordings Network holds audio Bible-teaching for the language — teaching audio is not a lexicon, and no scripture in any form is reported anywhere: portions, New Testament, and Bible all stand at "None Reported," and no God-term is attested in any source reached. Two hundred and eleven words from 1913 cannot ground a scripture draft, and a draft built on the Mainstream Kenyah scaffold without this language's own record would mislabel the book. The lane is therefore HELD — held, in this case, at the most basic level there is: the base does not yet exist to be obtained.

## The open seat

The pack's status can be stated in one line, and it is stated here verbatim so that it travels with the pack wherever the pack goes:

**Open seat: "God-term unverified — community usage not yet located."**

The base carries a second, named condition beside it, the starkest in the wave: the desk's Uma' Lasan base is NOT FOUND — no dedicated lexical resource was located in any catalogue searched.

## The one road not taken

There is no road of the usual kinds here — nothing to buy, nothing to borrow, nothing to download. What could ever move this lane is a base coming into existence: new documentation of the language, or a builder pass made with the Kenyah-cluster record and the Ray and Soriente fragments open and every Uma' Lasan form checked against them. The Mainstream Kenyah lane has landed in this project since the wicket was recorded, so a scaffold exists for that day; a scaffold is not a base. Until then, this pack is the lane's honest resting place: everything known, gathered in one place, with the gap named rather than papered over.
