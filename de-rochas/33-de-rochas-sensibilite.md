## The story

For nearly thirty years, Albert de Rochas (1837–1914) wrote about the borderland where the nineteenth century's science met its mysteries: hypnotism, telekinesis, "magnetic emanations," reincarnation, and spirit photography. He began with *Les Forces non définies* in 1887. Telekinesis means movement at a distance, and magnetic emanations, in the language of his time, were influences supposed to pass from a person outward. These were his subjects, pursued in book after book.

The book at the centre of this dossier is *L'Extériorisation de la sensibilité*, published in 1895, a study, experimental and historical, of the externalisation of sensibility. Its claim, documented in its pages, is a strange one stated plainly here as the dossier records it: a hypnotised subject feeling a stimulus applied at a distance from the body. Sensibility — the capacity to feel — was said to be exteriorised, placed outside the body itself, so that what was done at that distance was felt by the subject.

A companion volume followed. *L'Extériorisation de la motricité*, of 1896, which documents the Palladino committee work, concerns the externalisation of motricity, of movement rather than feeling. Its fourth edition runs to 630 pages.

De Rochas has not lacked writers about him in English. What he has lacked is translation. English-language writing about him summarises him at second hand, citing the French volumes as French. A reader can therefore learn what others say de Rochas found, but cannot read his own experimental and historical account in English in any edition found by this research.

## The work and what survives

*L'Extériorisation de la sensibilité* is available in reprint at 278 pages, in French, and is stated to be public domain in the United States. Its early editions are pre-1923, and the author died in 1914. Even so, edition copyright is to be checked per edition when an edition is prepared, as with all reviewer items. Different editions can carry different positions, and a general statement does not replace that check.

The supporting detail in the record is specific: English-language writing about Rochas summarises him at second hand, citing the French volumes as French, for example a hypnosis survey citing the 1907 6th edition. Citation of that kind keeps the original at arm's length. It names the French book as authority, while giving the English reader a summary instead of the book.

## What completing it would take

The right beginning is *Sensibilité* alone. It is one self-contained experimental and historical volume, about 278 pages, written in clear expository French, with diagrams of the "layers" of sensibility. Its size and its self-contained shape make it completable on its own.

*Motricité* is a second, larger, Palladino-heavy volume, and it is a separate decision. Its 630 pages, in its fourth edition, and its committee subject matter raise questions of scope that do not need to be settled in order to begin with *Sensibilité*. Keeping the two decisions separate protects both books: the first can be completed whole, and the second can be judged on its own record if and when it is taken up.
