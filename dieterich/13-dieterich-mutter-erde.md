# Albrecht Dieterich — Mutter Erde (1905)

## The story

Albrecht Dieterich (1866–1908) was a student of Hermann Usener, and he later became Usener's son-in-law. At thirty-seven he was a full professor at Heidelberg. At forty-two he was dead of cerebral apoplexy — a sudden bleeding or blockage in the brain, in the language of the record.

His early death cut off a much larger work. He had intended a magnum opus, a critical history of the decline of ancient religion. That history was left unfinished, and the unfinished work is part of his story. It is not, however, a work that this collection presents as completable. No surviving-manuscript evidence was found for it, and none is claimed.

What Dieterich did finish, and what survives complete, is a shorter book with its own lasting reach. *Mutter Erde: Ein Versuch über Volksreligion*, published in 1905, can be rendered as “Mother Earth: An Essay on Folk Religion.” It studies the earth-mother in Greek and Roman folk practice — the ground associated with Demeter, the Greek goddess of grain and harvest. Folk religion here means the religious practice of ordinary people, as distinct from official doctrine or temple theology alone.

His influence ran beyond his short life, through Carl Jung, Raffaele Pettazzoni, and Mircea Eliade. The book that carries that influence in finished form is a single essay-volume: short, complete, and still without an English translation found after research.

## The work and what survives

The 1905 monograph survives complete. It is short — a single essay-volume — and German reprints of the pre-1923 book circulate. The author died in 1908, the imprint is dated 1905, and the work is in the public domain.

A scan identifier is to be pinned when a translation is prepared. That detail remains open in the record.

One caution must travel with this dossier. Dieterich's *Eine Mithrasliturgie*, published in 1903, should not be presented as untranslated. The ancient liturgy text at its centre is widely available in English — for example in Meyer's *Mithras Liturgy*, available through the Internet Archive. Only Dieterich's commentary on that text is untranslated. *Mutter Erde* is the clean candidate in this dossier, and the distinction is the reason the caution is stated so plainly.

The unfinished *Geschichte des Untergangs der antiken Religion* — the history of the decline of ancient religion — belongs in the dedication's story, as the larger work death interrupted. It is not completable on the evidence in this record: no surviving-manuscript evidence was found, and none is claimed.

## What completing it would take

Completing it would take the translation of one short monograph. The translation would need a checking stage by a classicist, a scholar of Greek and Latin, because its subject is Greek and Roman folk practice.

It pairs naturally with the Usener dossier. Presented together, teacher and student would appear as two related volumes: Usener on how the names of gods are formed, Dieterich on the earth-mother in the folk religion of Greece and Rome.
