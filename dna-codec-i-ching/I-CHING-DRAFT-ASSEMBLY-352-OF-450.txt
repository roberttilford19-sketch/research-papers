### Judgment
> 乾，元亨利貞。

**Qian. Supreme success; it is good to hold fast.**
*(Term-locks applied: 元亨利貞 per §1.1 of the term-lock file — the oracle-response shape, desk-proposed, candidate P-1/T-1.)*

### Lines
> 初九，潛龍勿用。

**First nine: A dragon hidden below. Do not act.**
*（勿用 "do not act" per term-lock §6; Legge "It is not the time for active doing," Wilhelm "Do not act," Shaughnessy "do not use.")*

> 九二，見龍在田，利見大人。

**Nine in the second: A dragon shows in the field. It is good to see the great man.**
*（利見大人 per §5.1 — all sampled controls agree on "the great man.")*

> 九三，君子終日乾乾，夕惕若，厲无咎。

**Nine in the third: The noble man works at it all day long; at dusk he is still wary, as if in danger. Danger — but no blame.**

*(Correction, 2026-11-09: the English at this line was corrected by Robert's ruling at pilot approval — 在淵 is in the deep, not over it. The superseded wording, preserved verbatim: "Perhaps a leap — but still over the deep pool. No blame.")*

**Nine in the fifth: A dragon flying in the sky. It is good to see the great man.**

> 上九，亢龍有悔。

**Nine at the top: A dragon flown too high. There will be regret.**
*（悔 "regret" per §3.3 — the desk follows Shaughnessy against Legge/Wilhelm's "repentance.")*

### Yong line

## HEXAGRAM 2 — 坤 (Kūn)

### Judgment
> 坤，元亨，利牝馬之貞。君子有攸往，先迷後得，主利，西南得朋，東北喪朋，安貞吉。

**Kun. Supreme success. It is good to hold fast like a mare. The noble man has somewhere to go: if he goes first, he loses the way; if he follows, he finds his guide — and that is the gain. In the southwest, friends are found; in the northeast, friends are lost. Quiet holding fast brings good fortune.**
*（主利 rendered "that is the gain" — candidate P-2. The friends-and-directions clause is candidate P-3. 安貞吉 per §2 table.)*

### Lines
> 初六，履霜，堅冰至。

**First six: Treading on frost — hard ice is on its way.**
*(All four sampled controls agree in sense: Legge "treading on hoarfrost. The strong ice will come (soon)"; Wilhelm "solid ice isn't far off"; Shaughnessy "the firm ice will arrive.")*

> 六二，直方大，不習无不利。

**Six in the second: It is straight, square, and great. Without practice, all goes well.**
*（无不利 "all goes well" per §6. 不習 is candidate P-7 — Wilhelm departs from the whole field here: "Without purpose." Literal, preserved per §0: "Straight, square, and great." Re-voiced 2026-11-09 (RV-1, Robert's ruling): "It is" added so the line stands without its note; no other word changed.)*

> 六三，含章可貞，或從王事，无成有終。

**Six in the third: Keep your pattern folded away inside; you may hold fast. Should you follow the king's service: nothing is finished in your name, yet the work comes to its end.**
*（可貞 "you may hold fast" per §2 table. 含章 is candidate P-8; 无成有終 is candidate P-9. Literal, preserved per §0: "Keep your pattern hidden." Re-voiced 2026-11-09 (RV-2, Robert's ruling): "folded away inside" added to locate the pattern; P-8 untouched and open.)*

> 六四，括囊，无咎无譽。

**Six in the fourth: A sack tied shut. No blame, no praise.**
*(Unanimous in sense across Legge, Wilhelm, Shaughnessy.)*

> 六五，黃裳，元吉。

**Six in the fifth: A yellow lower garment. Supreme good fortune.**
*（元吉 "supreme good fortune" per §1.2/§3.1.)*

> 上六，龍戰于野，其血玄黃。

**Six at the top: Dragons fight out in the wilds; their blood is black and yellow.**
*（玄 "black" follows Wilhelm and Shaughnessy; Legge reads "purple and yellow" — candidate P-10.)*

### Yong line
> 用六，利永貞。

**For all sixes: It is good to hold fast, always.**
*（永貞 per §2 table: Wilhelm "Lasting perseverance furthers"; Legge "perpetually correct and firm, advantage will arise"; Shaughnessy "Beneficial to determine permanently.")*

## Marked-line candidates in the pilot (counted, NOT ruled)

- **P-1. Hexagram 1, judgment — 元亨利貞.** Four attributes (Legge/Wilhelm/Lynn) vs the two-beat oracle response the pilot prints (Rutt/Shaughnessy shape). The same question as term candidate T-1 — counted once in the combined board.
- **P-2. Hexagram 2, judgment — 主利.** The pilot reads "he finds his guide — and that is the gain" （主 as the guide/lord found, 利 as its benefit; Legge "afterwards finds guidance," Wilhelm "he finds his purpose"). Shaughnessy reads 主 as a person gained: "later gains his ruler." A third family reads the phrase as a verdict on who benefits. Two genuinely different sentences stand behind one Chinese phrase.
- **P-3. Hexagram 2, judgment — the friends and directions （西南得朋，東北喪朋）.** Literal companions gained southwest, lost northeast (Legge, with his note that "friends" are those of the same principles; Shaughnessy flatly "to gain a friend / to lose a friend") vs the cosmological reading in which the directions carry the trigrams' stations and the "friends" are one's own kind. The pilot prints the literal road; the cosmological road is the standing alternative.
- **P-4. Hexagram 1.3 — 乾乾.** A reduplication of the hexagram's own name: Legge "active and vigilant," Wilhelm "creatively active all day long," Shaughnessy "so initiating." The pilot's "works at it all day long" keeps the labour and drops the vigilance/creation colouring — either could be ruled back in.
- **P-5. Hexagram 1.4 — 或.** "Perhaps" (the pilot; Shaughnessy "And now jumping") vs Wilhelm's "wavering flight" — 或 can mark the alternation itself (now leaping, now not), not just uncertainty about it.
- **P-6. Hexagram 1, yong — 无首.** "None at the head" (the pilot; the leaderless-flight reading: Legge's note reads the headless dragons as auspicious because none presumes to lead; Wilhelm "without heads. Good fortune"; Shaughnessy "without heads") vs the literal picture of dragons whose heads are not seen. The sense readers rule here decides whether the line praises leaderlessness or reports an omen-image.
- **P-7. Hexagram 2.2 — 不習.** "Without practice" (the pilot; Legge "without repeated efforts"; Shaughnessy "not repeated") vs Wilhelm's lone departure, "Without purpose, yet nothing remains unfurthered" — 習 as rehearsal/repetition against Wilhelm's purposelessness. The whole field against one control; still a genuine two-reading line, because Wilhelm's reading is argued, not a slip.
- **P-8. Hexagram 2.3 — 含章.** "Keep your pattern hidden" (the pilot; 章 as pattern/ornament — Shaughnessy "Enclosing a pattern") vs Legge's "keeping his excellence under restraint" and Wilhelm's "Hidden lines" — 章 as talent/excellence kept sheathed. What is hidden differs: a design, or a person's quality.
- **P-9. Hexagram 2.3 — 无成有終.** The pilot gives the work an end but the worker no finished thing in his name (Legge "accomplishes nothing, but ends in accordance with the undertaking"; Wilhelm "claims no success for himself, yet everything is brought to a completion"). Shaughnessy flattens the agency away entirely: "there is no [completion], there is an end." Who finishes — and who is denied the credit — is the two-reading seam.
- **P-10. Hexagram 2.6 — 玄黃.** "Black and yellow" (the pilot; Wilhelm; Shaughnessy "black and yellow") vs Legge's "purple and yellow" — 玄 sits between dark-black and dark-purple, and the colour of the blood changes the picture a reader sees.

**Pilot marked-line candidates: 10** (P-1 is the same question as term candidate T-1; the combined distinct count is given in the closing board).

## HEXAGRAM 3 — 屯 (Zhùn)

### Judgment
> 屯，元亨利貞，勿用有攸往，利建侯。

**Zhun. Supreme success; it is good to hold fast. Do not set out. It is good to set up lords.**
*(The 元亨利貞 formula per term-lock §1.1 — the oracle-response shape, standing under term candidate T-1 and pilot candidate P-1, both open. 勿用有攸往 "Do not set out" per §6. 利建侯 per §6: Rutt "Favourable for appointing lordships" (REPORTED), Shaughnessy "beneficial to establish a lord" (VERIFIED); Wilhelm departs — "It furthers one to appoint helpers" — and the desk lock keeps the Bronze Age court word: lords are set up, not helpers appointed.)*

### Lines
> 初九，磐桓，利居貞，利建侯。

**First nine: Lingering, pacing in place. It is good to hold fast where you are. It is good to set up lords.**
*(Correction, 2026-11-09, by Robert's ruling at batch-1 approval: this line previously read "A boulder, hovering in place. It is good to hold fast where you are. It is good to set up lords." — the superseded wording is preserved verbatim in this note. Ground: 磐桓/盤桓 is a compound — to linger, pace, go to and fro; the old rendering glossed the two graphs separately （磐 the great stone, 桓 the pillar-post), and the tradition reads held-back, circling motion (Legge "difficulty in advancing"; Wilhelm "hesitation and hindrance"). 利居貞 "hold fast where you are" per §2 table; Shaughnessy reads the same phrase as the augury act: "beneficial to determine about a dwelling" (VERIFIED) — the T-3 strain, noted, not ruled.)*

> 六二，屯如邅如，乘馬班如，匪寇婚媾，女子貞不字，十年乃字。

**Six in the second: Difficulty upon difficulty; the horses wheel and turn back. Not raiders — they come seeking marriage. The maiden holds fast and does not promise; after ten years, she promises.**
*(女子貞不字 is the line the term-lock flagged under T-3 (§2): Shaughnessy reads 字 as childbearing — "the child's determination is not to get pregnant" — where the tradition reads the maiden's betrothal constancy. The desk prints the betrothal reading; the seam is counted below as candidate W4-1.)*

> 六三，即鹿无虞，惟入于林中。君子幾不如舍。往吝。

**Six in the third: Chasing a deer with no forester, you only push on into the forest. The noble man sees how it will turn out: better to let it go. Going on: humiliation.**
*(往吝 "Going on: humiliation" per §3.4 — Wilhelm at this very line: "To go on brings humiliation" (VERIFIED in the term-lock's sample); Shaughnessy reads 吝 as "distress.")*

> 六四，乘馬班如，求婚媾，往，吉无不利。

**Six in the fourth: The horses wheel and turn back. To seek the marriage match, go: good fortune, all goes well.**
*（无不利 "all goes well" per §6 — the desk lock compresses the shared double negative into plain speech.)*

> 九五，屯其膏，小貞吉，大貞凶。

**Nine in the fifth: The bounty is pent up. On a small scale, hold fast: good fortune. On a great scale, hold fast: misfortune.**
*（貞吉 / 貞凶 frames per §2: "Hold fast: good fortune" / "Hold fast: misfortune." 膏 is the fat, the rich store — the tradition reads the ruler's blessings withheld in the difficulty.)*

> 上六，乘馬班如，泣血漣如。

**Six at the top: The horses wheel and turn back. Tears of blood, streaming down.**

## HEXAGRAM 4 — 蒙 (Méng)

### Judgment
> 蒙，亨。匪我求童蒙，童蒙求我。初筮告，再三瀆，瀆則不告。利貞。

**Meng. Success. It is not I who seek the young fool; the young fool seeks me. The first casting informs. A second and third casting are an insult, and what insults is not answered. It is good to hold fast.**
*(亨 alone in verdict position: "success" per §1.3, standing under term candidate T-2. 童蒙 "the young fool" — the tradition's youthful ignorance/folly (Wilhelm, whose Hexagrams 1–4 were read at the desk for the term-lock: "It is not I who seek the young fool; the young fool seeks me"). **This judgment is where register candidate M-1 lands:** the Mawangdui text lacks 亨 and reads 初筮吉 — "the first casting is *auspicious*" — for the received 初筮告， "the first casting *informs*" (register L-1, REPORTED at excerpt level; re-verification against Shaughnessy or the Jicheng edition is owed before M-1 is ever served). The desk follows the received spine, per Option B.)*

### Lines
> 初六，發蒙。利用刑人，用說桎梏，以往吝。

**First six: Dispelling the ignorance. It is good to use punishments — and with them, to strike off fetters and shackles. To go on beyond that: humiliation.**
*(The tradition reads the punishments as the means of freeing the fettered, not as an end; 以往吝 carries the same "going on: humiliation" lock as at 3.3.)*

> 九二，包蒙，吉。納婦吉，子克家。

**Nine in the second: Bearing with the ignorant: good fortune. Taking a wife: good fortune. The son is able to keep the house.**

> 六三，勿用取女，見金夫，不有躬，无攸利。

**Six in the third: Do not take this woman in marriage. She sees a rich man and no longer holds herself. No journey is good.**
*（无攸利 per §6, used as "No journey is good." 金夫： the wealth reading of the tradition — a rich man, a man of substance — stands behind the same two graphs; the text prints that sense provisionally on the W4-6 model. Literal, preserved per §0: "a man of gold." Re-voiced 2026-11-09 (RV-3, Robert's ruling). Counted below as candidate W4-2, still open.)*

> 六四，困蒙，吝。

**Six in the fourth: Hemmed in by ignorance: humiliation.**
*（吝 in verdict position: "humiliation" per §3.4.)*

> 六五，童蒙，吉。

**Six in the fifth: The young fool: good fortune.**

> 上九，擊蒙，不利為寇，利禦寇。

**Nine at the top: Striking at the ignorance. It is not good to be the raider; it is good to ward off raiders.**

## HEXAGRAM 7 — 師 (Shī)

### Judgment
> 師貞，丈人吉，无咎。

**Shi. Hold fast. For the elder: good fortune. No blame.**
*(師 is the host, the army in the field. 丈人 "the elder" — the seasoned man of experience, on whose steadiness the judgment hangs; the tradition is unanimous in sense. Logged note, 2026-11-09 (Robert, at batch-1 approval): "For the elder" is thin for 丈人 — "venerable man" carries more of the word's weight; not wrong enough to block. No change made; the note is recorded here and in the term-lock file for the next term-lock pass.)*

### Lines

## HEXAGRAM 35 — 晉 (Jìn)

### Judgment
> 晉，康侯用錫馬蕃庶，晝日三接。

**Jin. The Marquis of Kang is given horses in great number, and in a single day he is received three times.**
*(康侯： the lord of Kang, the tradition's rising favourite at court — the hexagram of advancement opens on his rewards, not on a verdict word. 錫馬蕃庶： horses given, and breeding in abundance; 晝日三接： three audiences in one daylight — favour measured in access. The judgment carries no 貞 frame and no 吉/凶： advancement is shown, not sentenced.)*

### Lines
> 初六，晉如摧如，貞吉。罔孚裕，无咎。

**First six: Advancing, then beaten back. Hold fast: good fortune. Where there is no trust, bear it broadly: no blame.**
*(摧如： the advance broken off at its first step. 罔孚： 罔 here is the negation — there is no trust — not the net of Hexagram 34.3 (candidate W4-29); the trust is absent, and the line's counsel is 裕， breadth of bearing under the absence. 孚 under the provisional lock (T-4, open) — a tripwire standing, listed in the closing board. 貞吉 frame per §2.)*

> 六二，晉如愁如，貞吉。受茲介福，于其王母。

**Six in the second: Advancing, sorrowing as he goes. Hold fast: good fortune. He receives this great blessing from the king's mother.**
*(愁如： the advance made in sorrow — held back from the audience the judgment's favourite takes three times a day. 王母： the king's mother, the royal ancestress of the tradition, from whose hand the delayed blessing finally comes. 貞吉 frame per §2.)*

> 六三，眾允，悔亡。

**Six in the third: All are in accord. Regret passes.**
*(眾允： the many agree — the advance is no longer his alone, and the regret of the two lines below dissolves in the consent. 悔亡 "regret passes" per §3.3.)*

> 九四，晉如鼫鼠，貞厲。

**Nine in the fourth: Advancing like a flying squirrel. Holding fast: danger.**
*(鼫鼠 is counted below as a candidate. 貞厲 frame per §2.)*

> 六五，悔亡，失得勿恤。往吉无不利。

**Six in the fifth: Regret passes. Loss and gain — do not grieve over them. Going on: good fortune; all goes well.**
*(恤 — NO SETTLED RENDERING. The term-lock carries no lock for this word (§5.4: an annotation, not a lock). The desk prints "grieve" provisionally, the drafted choice standing in this lane since Hexagram 11.3, and marks the line plainly: the graph's plain sense is to worry, be anxious, feel concerned care, and a later term-lock pass will weigh "grieve" against "worry." Counted below as candidate W4-31, jointly with Hexagram 37.5. 悔亡 per §3.3; 无不利 "all goes well" per §6.)*

> 上九，晉其角，維用伐邑。厲吉无咎，貞吝。

**Nine at the top: Advancing with the horns. Use them only against his own city. Danger; good fortune; no blame. Holding fast: humiliation.**
*(晉其角： the advance at its horned extreme — force at the top of the rise. 維用伐邑： the horns are licensed inward only, against his own city's disorder; turned outward they are not licensed at all. 厲 "danger" per §3.6 standing directly beside 吉， as at Hexagram 27.6 in the fourth batch. 貞吝： the frame built from §3.4's 吝 lock, as at Hexagram 11.6 in the second batch.)*

## HEXAGRAM 36 — 明夷 (Míngyí)

### Judgment
> 明夷，利艱貞。

**Mingyi. It is good to hold fast in the hardship.**
*(明夷： the light wounded, darkened — brightness gone under injury, the mirror of Hexagram 35's rise: there the favourite was received three times in a day; here the light survives by hiding. 利艱貞： the 貞 lock worked under hardship, in the frame's words at Hexagrams 11.3, 21.4, and 26.3 — the T-3 strain, noted, not ruled.)*

### Lines
> 初九，明夷于飛，垂其翼，君子于行，三日不食。有攸往，主人有言。

**First nine: The light wounded, on the wing, its wings drooping. The noble man, on his way, goes three days without food. He has somewhere to go — and his host has words against him.**
*(垂其翼： the bird of light flying low with lowered wings, so as not to be seen. 三日不食： the flight's price counted in days without food. 主人有言 is counted below as a candidate. 有攸往 per §6.)*

> 六二，明夷，夷于左股，用拯馬壯吉。

**Six in the second: The light wounded — wounded in the left thigh. A strong horse to the rescue: good fortune.**
*(左股： the left thigh, the wound that slows but does not stop — 股 is the thigh of Hexagram 31.3, where the influence stirred. 拯馬壯： a horse strong enough to lift him out of the dark — rescue, not escape by his own strength.)*

> 九三，明夷于南狩，得其大首，不可疾貞。

**Nine in the third: The light wounded, hunting in the south, takes the great head. He may not hold fast in haste.**
*(南狩： the southern hunt — the tradition reads the campaign against the dark power in its own country. 大首： the great head, the chief of the darkness, as heads were broken at Hexagram 30.6. 不可疾貞： the 可貞 frame (§2 table) negated and qualified — the holding fast itself is right; the haste in it is what is forbidden.)*

> 六四，入于左腹，獲明夷之心，于出門庭。

**Six in the fourth: He goes into the left belly, and gains the heart of the darkening light. And so he leaves the gate and the court.**
*(獲明夷之心 is counted below as a candidate. 于出門庭： having learned the darkness from inside it, he withdraws — the leaving is the line's act, and it is made with open eyes.)*

> 六五，箕子之明夷，利貞。

**Six in the fifth: The darkened light of Jizi. It is good to hold fast.**
*(箕子： Jizi, the prince of the tradition who kept his light alive under the tyrant by letting it be thought extinguished — the hexagram's exemplar, named once and carrying the whole teaching. 利貞 frame per §2.)*

> 上六，不明晦，初登于天，後入于地。

**Six at the top: Not light, but darkness. First it climbs to heaven; then it goes down into the earth.**
*(The dark power's own arc closes the hexagram: what rose highest without light in it ends in the ground. Set it beside Hexagram 35.6's horns: the two hexagrams are a pair, and their top lines fall in opposite directions.)*

## HEXAGRAM 38 — 睽 (Kuí)

### Judgment
> 睽，小事吉。

**Kui. In small matters: good fortune.**
*(睽： estrangement — eyes turned from one another, the household of Hexagram 37 come apart into opposition. The judgment's modesty is its teaching: in the time of estrangement only small things can be brought off. Compare Hexagram 22's judgment, where the benefit was "a little good" — the desk keeps the two smallnesses in their own words, as the Chinese keeps them.)*

### Lines
> 初九，悔亡，喪馬勿逐，自復。見惡人，无咎。

**First nine: Regret passes. The horse is lost — do not chase it; it comes back on its own. See the bad man: no blame.**
*(喪馬勿逐，自復： in estrangement, what is chased is lost for good; what is left alone returns — the hexagram's method in its first line. 見惡人： even meeting the ill-willed man is blameless, if the meeting is not a joining. 悔亡 per §3.3.)*

> 九二，遇主于巷，无咎。

**Nine in the second: He meets his lord in the lane. No blame.**
*(巷： the lane, the narrow way — the meeting happens off the great road, by chance in a small place, as the judgment's small-matters licence allows.)*

> 六三，見輿曳，其牛掣，其人天且劓，无初有終。

**Six in the third: He sees a cart dragged backward, its oxen pulling against the load. The man in it is branded on the forehead and his nose is cut off. No beginning — but there is an end.**
*(天且劓： the two punishments — the forehead marked, the nose cut — the estranged man's company read off his face: he is held by the mutilated and the dragging. 无初有終： the line's whole hope is in its last three graphs: the thing cannot begin well and can still end. > 九四，睽孤，遇元夫。交孚，厲无咎。

**Nine in the fourth: Estranged and alone, he meets the first man. Trust is exchanged between them. Danger; no blame.**
*(元夫： the first man — first as 元 is first in the formula (§1.2): the true counterpart, met inside the isolation. 交孚： trust meeting trust in exchange, the 交 of Hexagram 14.5's 交如 — under the provisional 孚 lock (T-4, open); a tripwire standing, listed in the closing board. 厲 "danger" per §3.6 standing beside 无咎 without the 貞 frame, as at Hexagram 27.6.)*

> 六五，悔亡。厥宗噬膚，往何咎。

**Six in the fifth: Regret passes. His ancestor bites through the covering. Going on — what blame could there be?**
*(厥宗噬膚 is counted below as a candidate. 噬膚 "biting into soft flesh" in Hexagram 21.2's own words in the third batch — there the bite was the judge's; here it is the kin's, through whatever covers and estranges. 悔亡 per §3.3; 何咎 as at Hexagram 9.1 in the first batch.)*

> 上九，睽孤，見豕負塗，載鬼一車，先張之弧，後說之弧，匪寇婚媾。往，遇雨則吉。

**Nine at the top: Estranged and alone. He sees a pig covered in mud — a cart loaded with ghosts. First he draws his bow; then he lays the bow aside. Not raiders — they come seeking marriage. Going on, he meets the rain: good fortune.**
*(The hexagram's longest line, and its turn is the whole book of estrangement in one scene: what isolation shows a man is filth and ghosts; what approaches him is a wedding. 說之弧： 說 read as 脫 — lays aside — the same reading the earlier batches entered at Hexagrams 9.3, 26.2, and 33.2. 匪寇婚媾 in Hexagram 3.2's own words — "Not raiders — they come seeking marriage" — the frame holding steady across the whole drafting, as at Hexagram 22.4. 遇雨： the rain that ends the drought of opposition, in the tradition.)*

## HEXAGRAM 48 — 井 (Jǐng)

### Judgment
> 井，改邑不改井，无喪无得，往來井，井汔至，亦未繘井，羸其瓶，凶。

**Jing. The town can be moved; the well cannot be moved. Nothing is lost, nothing is gained. Coming and going to the well — the well stays the well. The water almost drawn, the rope not yet clear — and his jug is broken: misfortune.**
*(井： the well — after Hexagram 47's exhaustion, the thing that does not exhaust: the town moves, the well stays, and its water is for whoever comes. 无喪无得： no loss, no gain — the well is not diminished by the drawing or increased by the leaving. 井汔至，亦未繘井： 汔 "almost" — the draw nearly finished, the rope （繘） not yet pulled clear of the well's mouth. 羸其瓶 is counted below as a candidate: the jug broken at the last moment of the draw is the judgment's one misfortune, and it falls on the drawing, not on the well.)*

## HEXAGRAM 50 — 鼎 (Dǐng)

### Judgment
> 鼎，元吉亨。

**Ding. Supreme good fortune. Success.**
*(鼎： the cauldron — the vessel that cooks and offers, Hexagram 49's change given its instrument: what the change is *for* is the nourishing of what comes next, and the book's first half closes on the vessel. 元吉亨： the verdict words in their own order — 元吉 (§1.2/§3.1, "supreme good fortune") and then the bare verdict 亨 under the desk lock "success" (T-2, open — the offering family at its closest here, where the vessel is itself the offering's instrument; noted, not ruled). ### Lines
> 初六，鼎顛趾，利出否，得妾以其子，无咎。

**First six: The cauldron's legs tipped up. It is good to empty out what is spoiled. He takes a concubine for the sake of her son. No blame.**
*(顛趾： the legs turned up — the vessel inverted to be cleaned, as the well of Hexagram 48 was cleaned from its mud. 出否： emptying out the spoiled — 否 here the stagnation-word, the hexagram-name of Hexagram 12 in its "what has gone bad" sense; the literal stands here: "to let out the bad." 得妾以其子： the concubine taken for her son's sake — the low door by which the line rises, cleared of blame as the inverted vessel is: the hexagram sentences purposes, not stations.)*

*(仇 is counted below as a candidate. 實： the filling — food in the vessel, real and present, against Hexagram 48.2's leaking jar and 48.3's undrunk well. 疾： a fault as readily as a sickness, as at Hexagram 41.4 in the fifth batch, where the fault decreased was the man's own; here it is the counterpart's, and it is what keeps him from reaching the full vessel. 不我能即： he cannot come at me — 即 "approach," the graph of Hexagram 43's 不利即戎 turned to a person.)*

**Nine in the third: The cauldron's ears are changed; its carrying is blocked. The pheasant fat is not eaten. When the rain comes, regret lessens. In the end: good fortune.**
*(鼎耳： the ears by which the cauldron is lifted and carried — 革 here in its "altered" dress: the ears changed, the carrying （行） blocked with them, and the vessel's good food stands uncarried. 雉膏： the pheasant's fat, the delicacy of the vessel, not eaten while the carrying fails. 方雨虧悔： when the rain comes — rain as the easing, as at Hexagram 38.6 in the fifth batch, where meeting the rain was the fortune — regret lessens （虧）, it does not vanish by decree. 終吉 "In the end: good fortune" per §3.1's 終吉 family.)*

> 九四，鼎折足，覆公餗，其形渥，凶。

> 六五，鼎黃耳金鉉，利貞。

**Six in the fifth: The cauldron has yellow ears and a metal carrying-bar. It is good to hold fast.**
*(黃耳金鉉： yellow ears, a bar of metal — 鉉 is the carrying-bar passed through the ears, the fitting by which the vessel is borne to its place; the fifth line's vessel is sound in every fitting the third line's lacked. 黃 the centre-colour (§1.2's family), as at 49.1's yellow oxhide. 利貞 frame per §2.)*

> 上九，鼎玉鉉，大吉，无不利。

**Nine at the top: The cauldron's carrying-bar is jade. Great good fortune. All goes well.**
*(玉鉉： the bar of jade — the tradition reads the jade bar as strength that does not cut: hard enough to bear the vessel, not hard as the fifth line's metal. The desk prints the stone and names the reading as the tradition's. 大吉 per §3.1; 无不利 "all goes well" per §6 — the first half of the book closes on the vessel borne well, and the desk lets the line close the batch without addition.)*
