## Note on this companion

These drafts were first prepared as part of an unsigned packet, designed on the principle that a good work needs no name attached to it. On 25 October 2026 the author set that design aside and reissued the work openly, under his own name, so that whatever credit there is for carrying it forward belongs to whoever does the carrying. The author is a resident of Centennial Towers, and says so openly; nothing in this volume depends on that fact, and every statement in it is drawn from the public record.

The volume was compiled entirely from public records — federal registrations and housing datasets, the Kansas Housing Resources Corporation's published lists and allocation plans, county-record-derived data, the published press archive, and the statutes and regulations cited. Where the public record disagrees with itself, the disagreement is printed. Where it runs out, the drafts say so and name the document — usually one in the owner's own files — that carries the answer.

Every section that can be written from public information is written, in application language. What only the owner can supply appears as a bracketed blank, and each blank is labelled with the exact document, from the owner's own records, that fills it. An application for any of this funding can only be submitted by the owner: no one else can sign it, certify it, or file it. These drafts cover everything on the public side of that line.

### 3.2 Needs narrative *(written)*

Centennial Towers is a ninety-home, fully assisted senior property in its forty-ninth year, rehabilitated once, in 1998–99, and now presenting the capital profile of its generation: building systems installed in 1977 or renewed in 1998 arriving at the end of their published service lives together, in a structure whose frame has generations of life remaining. The applicant seeks a rehabilitation allocation to renew those systems in a single planned transaction, rather than piecemeal from operating funds as they fail.

The needs case rests on the Property Needs Assessment commissioned for this application, whose scope framework is set out at § 3.4 and whose findings govern the final scope and budget. Its principal lines, stated here as the assessment's framework, are these. Domestic-water distribution of 1977 origin stands at year forty-nine against a published service-life range of forty to fifty years for galvanized supply piping; systemic corrosion at that age is the expected condition, and the assessment will verify material and extent — including what the 1998 rehabilitation renewed — rather than presume them. Heating, cooling, and ventilation plant; elevators serving a fifty-five-and-over population; windows and envelope, including repointing survey across the building's brick skin; sewer laterals; fire-alarm, detection, and emergency-call systems; and unit interiors all stand for assessment on the same vintage logic. Life-safety scope is carried as its own line: verification of sprinkler coverage home by home, with in-unit retrofit to current residential standards where coverage proves partial, as the building's 1977 code era makes likely (the residential sprinkler standard dates to 1989); smoke-control work across shafts and ducts, the documented primary hazard pathway in buildings of this form — this property's one documented fire-service event, in January 2013, was an elevator motor's smoke in the corridors, with no fire spread; and coordination with a separately sponsored rated safe-room project, described in the Roadmap, which is not a cost of this application.

The rehabilitation is to be executed in an occupied building, phased by stacks and floors — the method proven in this city by the 2019–21 renovation of the town's other senior tower — so that no household is displaced from the property by the work that preserves its home.

### 3.3 Property history *(written)*

The property was constructed in 1977 as senior housing and has served that purpose continuously since. In 1998 the applicant acquired the property and rehabilitated it under federal housing tax credits allocated by KHRC (KHRC Rental Housing Development List: 1998, acquisition/rehabilitation, senior, completed; credits of $93,000 recorded against the project), placing the rehabilitated property in service in 1999 under a recorded extended-use agreement whose thirty-year term runs to approximately 2029. The property's present assistance structure — project-based rental assistance over a tax-credit property, rents at thirty percent of income, utilities included — has operated without interruption since the rehabilitated property was placed in service in 1999. The applicant has owned and operated the property for twenty-eight years.

One reconciliation is noted for the file, in the open: KHRC's development list counts the 1998 project at seventy-eight total homes (fifty-six tax-credit, twenty-two market), while the federal tax-credit database and all current sources count ninety homes, fifty-six of them low-income. The fifty-six figure is consistent in every source. The applicant will reconcile the totals with KHRC from the 1998 project file as a first administrative step, since unit count drives credit sizing.

### 3.4 Scope framework *(written; amounts await the Assessment)*

The rehabilitation scope will be established by the Property Needs Assessment and is expected to comprise, in framework: (a) domestic-water repiping to the extent the assessment verifies surviving 1977 distribution; (b) heating, cooling, and ventilation plant renewal, with per-unit control evaluated against the in-town precedent; (c) elevator modernization; (d) window replacement and envelope repair, including brick repointing as surveyed; (e) sewer lateral renewal as scoped; (f) life-safety scope per § 3.2 (sprinkler verification and in-unit retrofit as required, smoke control, detection); (g) unit interior renewal; (h) accessibility upgrades against current standards for an aging population; (i) energy measures to KHRC's design standards (2026 QAP Appendix C), with utility-funded measures only where a program is verified at design stage; (j) roof-cap inspection and any work the inspection requires, the cap's age and construction being undocumented in any public record.

# CLOSING SHEET — WHERE THE FORMS LIVE

*The applications these drafts point to are not paper forms that circulate in advance. Each is issued by its administering agency with its funding round and is submitted by the applicant entity through the agency's own system. This sheet names the doors, as published by the agencies on 25 October 2026, so the owner's team reaches the current materials without hunting.*

**Road 1 — the KHRC 9 percent round, with its bundled HOME, National Housing Trust Fund, and state credit.** The program door is the Kansas Housing Resources Corporation's Housing Development page for LIHTC & HUD Resources at kshousingcorp.org, where the round's Application, Checklist, and Forms are posted together with the adopted 2027 Qualified Allocation Plan. Submission is electronic, through KHRC's Procorem Workcenter: an applicant must first request a Procorem login by email to housingdevelopment@kshousingcorp.org. Preliminary-application items are submitted through Procorem by noon Central on the due date; the preliminary application fee is the exception, sent to KHRC directly, and the full-application fee is likewise mailed to the KHRC office by its deadline. The published 2027 calendar: **preliminary applications due 15 January 2027; invitations to submit full applications 5 March 2027; full applications due 7 May 2027; award announcements 23 July 2027.** Rehabilitation proposals are desk-reviewed at the preliminary stage; the 33-point preliminary threshold applies to new construction only.

**Road 2 — 4 percent credits with tax-exempt bonds.** The same KHRC program door carries the 4 percent Application, Checklist, and Forms; 4 percent applications are accepted on a rolling basis. The FHA debt (Section 221(d)(4), or 223(f) for a light scope) is originated through a HUD-approved MAP lender of the owner's choosing; there is no public form to attach for it.

**Road 3 — FHLBank Topeka Affordable Housing Program.** The door is the Federal Home Loan Bank of Topeka's Affordable Housing Program (fhlbanktopeka.com). The application is made by the project sponsor jointly with a member bank, through the Bank's own system, when its round opens; the 2026 round closed 31 July 2026, and the next follows the Bank's annual cycle.

**Before any door opens,** the documents a January filing requires are assembled in the autumn: the Property Needs Assessment and the independent market study (Part Three), the recorded 1998 covenants read from the owner's own files, and documented site control. Nothing on this sheet can be submitted by anyone but the applicant. It exists so that the applicant's remaining work is errands, not research.
